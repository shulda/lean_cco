Pure C19 uses only a finite elementary abelian group, a NONEMPTY A and every
finite pairwise-intersecting translate family. Pure abstract C24–C30 use
exactly finite F2 algebra, connected connectors, or a given character with a
free groupoid point functor, as appropriate. In particular C27–C30 require no
distinguished involution, F2 isotropy or geometry once the character is given.

The actual source consumers use `AtomicSource S`, original
`HomogeneousSource S G` and the accepted exact calculus; the standing CCO
consumer constructs that calculus with
`exactCalculus_of_homogeneous_CCO S H cco`. Its displayed original
countability convention is retained. No homogeneity/CCO assertion about a
quotient, SAP, source automorphism section, phase preference, AP of a marked
age, dominance, or finite-owner kernel projection is added.

For C20–21 D is full exact, nonempty, endpoint-gate bisaturated and supported
on the union of actual rigid arrows. There is no uninstantiated affine or
Helly premise in the final source instance. C22–23 assume only nonempty exact
factors with product CONTAINED in the displayed bridge; the output enlarges
them, and does not assert equality of a factor with its enlargement. The
intermediate gate is constructed, never silently identified with an unrelated
named gate. `trivial_coset` and `identity_trivial_coset` include the singleton
subgroup and actual gate identity boundary, even with many real neighbours.

For phase seeds, equality is actual equality of tagged quotient points.
Repeated real representatives must induce one consistent prescribed function;
conflicting bits on the same gate class are explicitly ruled out. Nonrigid
flexibility changes whole orbit seeds across suitable GLOBAL choices. No claim
that one fixed marking realizes both differences on every pair is made.
The output is a unary BIT FUNCTION, not a global source automorphism or owner
motion. Empty finite input, singleton input, trivial isotropy (where there is
no nonidentity detecting input), one-object groupoids and unreached sorts are
covered by the corresponding general constructions.
