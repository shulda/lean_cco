## N01: Quotient diagrams and comparison tests — D01/D02, C01

In the source let I be the finite nonempty index set. For every i put
Q_i=X/C_i and O_i=X/H_i, with C_i<=H_i. Put Hstar=intersection_i H_i and
Omega=X/Hstar. There are canonical maps Q_i->O_i and Omega->O_i. For i,j
there are maps from both owner sorts into X/(H_i join H_j); the composites
from Omega agree because both send an original class to its class modulo
H_i join H_j. Every corresponding finite restriction commutes as well.

Let B be the given finite induced subset of Omega and let B_i be its
ACTUAL image in O_i. Thus beta_i:B->B_i is onto by definition. If two
points of Omega have equal images in every O_i, representatives are related
by every H_i and hence by Hstar. They are the same Omega point. The tuple
of beta_i maps is therefore jointly injective. This elementary argument
uses an intersection of equivalences, not a representation theorem.

For the abstract proof A02 supplies these maps, image equalities, joint
injectivity and commuting comparisons. D02 compatibility says precisely
that two owners have equal images in their specified common comparison
carrier. Every tuple beta(b) passes these tests (C01). That assertion does
not yet say that EVERY passing tuple comes from B. The converse is the
additional finite fullness premise A03. Keeping the two directions separate
will make the coordinate-surjectivity proof use less than owner-image
identification.

No individual rho_i:Aut(B)->Aut(B_i) is required to be onto in the joint
lemma. Source condition (ii) asks that these maps be defined. In the actual
canonical language that follows from the quotient-colour argument below;
in a generic finite interface it is appropriate explicit recognizability
data. It does not assert a globally liftable owner automorphism.

## N02: Full carrier, empty case and both surjections — D03/D04, C02–C05

Define W as ALL tuples x_i in D_i whose owners o_i(x_i) are pairwise
compatible in D02. Each o_i is onto B_i. This definition imposes no extra
choice of a sparse compatible subset and no colour conclusion yet.

First handle B empty (C02). All actual images B_i are empty. The very
existence of a function D_i->B_i forces each D_i empty. Since I is nonempty,
the indexed product and W are empty. The automorphism of an empty finite
structure is its unique empty permutation, so every group in JWA is
trivial, its group maps are onto, and its point/fibre assertions hold
vacuously. An input A0 subset W is empty and has the empty extension.
Do not use the reverse implication of I04 on an empty source: for arbitrary
empty actions its point condition would be vacuous even for a nononto group
homomorphism. Here triviality is proved from the actual finite carriers.

For w in W its owner tuple passes every comparison. A03 and joint
injectivity give a unique b in B with beta_i(b)=o_i(w_i). Define q(w)=b.
Conversely, for each fixed b every choice

    x_i in D_i(beta_i(b)) = o_i^{-1}({beta_i(b)})

is in W by C01 and has q-value b. Thus C03 is the exact disjoint-union
identity

    W = disjoint_union_(b in B) product_i D_i(beta_i(b)).

This is a set statement; it does not say that automorphisms of different
fibres can be chosen independently. The local factors retain all their
colours, including relations between different owner fibres.

To prove C04, fix b in B. Each displayed fibre is nonempty because o_i is
onto. Choose one element of each of the finitely many fibres; the resulting
tuple gives a point of W above b. Hence q is onto. If B is nonempty then
W is nonempty, before any use of the nonempty point-action criterion.
If W is nonempty its q-value is a B point, proving the converse as well.
All sets are finite because there are finitely many finite factors.

For coordinate surjectivity C05, fix i and x_i in D_i. Its owner is some
b_i in B_i. The actual-image map beta_i is onto, so choose b in B with
beta_i(b)=b_i. Keep the selected x_i and choose every other x_j over
beta_j(b). Their owner tuple beta(b) passes all comparisons by C01, so the
resulting tuple is in W and has the prescribed i-coordinate.

Notably this argument does NOT need A03: every actual b provides a
compatible tuple regardless of whether B omits other compatible owner
tuples. Dropping owner fullness instead breaks the assertion that the
image of the formula-defined W is exactly B; it can introduce extra owner
tuples. The separate failure of an onto D_j->B_j can prevent the coordinate
completion itself. The finite controls distinguish these mechanisms.

## N03: Actual realization, recognizers and fine colours — C06–C09

C06 uses the named imports I01 and I02. The actual full RAW exact invariant
equivalences form a commuting distributive sublattice, so their class CRT
identifies X/meet_i C_i with exactly the tuples of Q_i points agreeing in
X/(C_i join C_j) for every pair. Repeated equivalences are included in that
finite-family theorem. JOG identifies each comparison kernel C_i join C_j
with H_i join H_j, exactly the kernels used in JOW. Because meet_i C_i=Id,
the canonical map from X, not a coarser quotient, is a bijection onto these
coordinate tuples. Restricting each coordinate to its given finite D_i
therefore realizes W as an actual finite subset of X. Give it the induced
original language. This alone does not prove that a coordinatewise map
preserves that induced language.

C07 is the direct equivalence argument in N01: Hstar is the intersection,
so Omega injects into the complete owner coordinate tuple. Consequently the
B point obtained from owner fullness is the actual Hstar owner class of
its realized original W point. This verifies that q is the canonical
projection, rather than an auxiliary tuple label.

For C08 let G=Aut(X). Every canonical quotient map under discussion is
G-equivariant between the appropriate actual quotient G-sets. Its equality
kernel is G-invariant, and the inverse image of each named target pair
orbital is G-invariant. It is therefore a union of the named source pair
orbitals. There are finitely many source orbitals in the present language.
The same argument on Q_i or Omega uses the induced G-action and its complete
canonical orbital language. For nested equivalences C_i<=H_i or Hstar<=H_i,
the canonical map is well defined and the same pullback argument applies.
Restrict to the actual finite induced images D_i, B, W to obtain A05.
A quotient-colour union may contain the source diagonal or relate different
points with the same image; both are retained.

This proof does not assert that every full automorphism of an infinite
quotient source globally lifts to X. The old G-image already acts on each
named orbital and makes every pullback a union. Finite permutations
preserving those named colours preserve the union, which is precisely the
needed conclusion. The construction is the same recognizable-quotient
support used in owner_action; it is recalled operationally in C10, not
exported here as an additional human theorem. The receiving implementation
should reuse its accepted quotient/action representations and shared
conversion helpers rather than invent a second representation of them.

For C09 take two actual W points x,y and their original atomic pair orbital
a, allowing logical equality. In each Q_i the pair of their projections has
pullback C_i;a;C_i. If a second actual pair has the same complete list of
coordinate orbitals, it lies in every one of those pullbacks. I03 gives

    intersection_i (C_i;a;C_i) = (meet_i C_i);a;(meet_i C_i) = a.

It consequently has the SAME original fine colour. The argument handles
pairs whose coordinate projections coincide in some or all positions. It
proves A06 independently of JOG or set CRT: the formula concerns already
actual pairs. In its application to the formal W we first use C06 to know
that those tuples are actual. Do not infer readout from distributivity, or

## N04: Full and partial quotient descent — D05, C10/C11

The following operational argument is shared elementary support, not an
extra source export. Let t:S->T be an onto map between finite induced
binary structures. Suppose ker(t) and every pulled-back named target pair
colour are unions of named source colours, as in A05.

For a source automorphism alpha define beta(t(x))=t(alpha(x)). If t(x)=t(y),
recognized kernel preservation gives t(alpha(x))=t(alpha(y)), so beta is
well defined. Apply the same argument to alpha inverse: its descended map
is beta inverse. Thus beta is a permutation of ALL T. For a named target
relation R, its pullback t^{-1}(R) is a source colour union, preserved in
both directions by alpha. Choosing preimages of any target pair proves
that beta preserves and reflects R. Hence beta belongs to FULL Aut(T).
Preservation of the kernel alone would only prove it is a permutation.

Identity, composition and inverse commute with this formula, so descent is
a group homomorphism. In particular C11 defines own_i on all Aut(D_i) and
rho_i on all Gamma=Aut(B), not merely on motions selected in a construction.
The canonical maps make the usual equivariance equations literal.

For a partial automorphism p:S0->S1 of a finite induced A subset S, use the
same formula on t(S0). Kernel preservation makes it well defined; reflection
makes it injective; its range is exactly t(S1). For any two image-domain
points, choose representatives in S0. Pullback-colour recognition proves
preservation and reflection of every target pair relation. Thus the result
is a genuine induced partial isomorphism between subsets of t(A).
Several distinct original-domain points may have the same projected image;
well-definedness is therefore not a cosmetic step. The empty partial map
has empty image domain and is covered as well.

Use this statement for every D_i->B_i, B->B_i, W->D_i and W->B map once its
onto carrier map is established. There is no need for an ambient extension
of alpha or p, countability, ordinary EPPA, or a chosen section.

## N05: The ENTIRE group identity — D06, C12–C16

Define the unambiguous matching subgroup first as pairs:

    P = {(gamma,(alpha_i)) in Gamma times product_i Aut(D_i):
                     own_i(alpha_i)=rho_i(gamma) for every i}.

It is closed under coordinatewise multiplication and inverse because
own_i and rho_i are homomorphisms. To establish JWA one must prove both
inclusions, not merely construct some W automorphisms.

For C12 take ANY alpha in FULL Aut(W). The onto maps C04/C05 and N04 descend
it to alpha_i in Aut(D_i) and gamma in Gamma. For b_i in B_i choose x_i
above it and a W point above x_i. Compute both ways around the commuting
square: own_i(alpha_i)(b_i)=rho_i(gamma)(b_i). This holds for every b_i,
including owners not met by the original input. Since W points ARE their
complete coordinate tuples, their alpha_i images determine alpha uniquely.
This proof accounts for all finite induced automorphisms, not just those
known to extend globally or generated by previously chosen requests.

For C13, if two gamma values induce the same actions on all B_i, then for
every b their two images have identical beta_i coordinates. Joint
injectivity gives equality. Thus forgetting gamma is injective on P. In
particular the existentially described coordinate-tuple group in source
JWA is well defined, with a unique common owner component.

For C14 take an arbitrary element of P and a W point w above b. The i-th
new owner is own_i(alpha_i)(beta_i(b))=beta_i(gamma(b)). These are the
coordinates of an ACTUAL point gamma(b) of B. Every resulting tuple is in
FULL W. The inverse coordinate tuple matches gamma inverse and gives its
inverse map. Thus it is a carrier bijection even before using A06.

For C15 every alpha_i preserves its own named pair colours. For any W pair
the complete coordinate pair-colour tuple is consequently unchanged; A06
makes its fine W colour unchanged. Therefore the carrier bijection is an
automorphism of the actual declared finite W structure. Extra unrecovered
fine colours would invalidate exactly this step, even if all set-level
compatibility and quotient maps were correct.

The C12 descent and C14/C15 assembly maps are inverse. For two matching
elements, their assembled composite at coordinate i is alpha_i after
alpha_i_prime, so multiplication is respected. Identities and inverse are
also coordinatewise. This proves the canonical group isomorphism C16.
Projection to Gamma supplies the actual full owner homomorphism on Aut(W).
It is NOT a homomorphic choice of a lift of each gamma. No group section,
origins on fibres or independent fibre-permutation interpretation is used.
The nonsplit finite action control makes this distinction concrete.

## N06: Prescribed points and kernel transitivity — D07, C17/C18

Let x,y in W and gamma in FULL Gamma satisfy gamma(q(x))=q(y). Commuting
squares give

    rho_i(gamma)(o_i(x_i)) = o_i(y_i)

for every i. A07 supplies alpha_i taking x_i to y_i and inducing precisely
rho_i(gamma) on the ENTIRE B_i. All these choices are made for the SAME
fixed gamma. C16 assembles them into alpha in Aut(W), with alpha(x)=y.
The unique joint-owner component is gamma, not an unrelated owner motion
agreeing only at q(x). This proves C17.

If B is nonempty, C04 makes q:W->B onto with nonempty domain. Its group map
is equivariant by construction. Apply the already shared B001 criterion
I04: C17 is exactly every prescribed point request, so the group map is onto
and its kernel is transitive on each q-fibre. Equivalently, lift a gamma
using a chosen x and y over gamma(q(x)); setting gamma=identity gives the
kernel point motions. This is just a reminder of the shared proof, not a
second assigned formalization task. For empty B use C02 directly.

Individual rho_i maps need not be onto here. A07 quantifies all factor owner
actions, but the assembly only requests the particular rho_i(gamma). Do not
add master M3 as an unnecessary hypothesis of this consumer. Prescribed
point lifts also do not prove the sparse-input EPPA clause below: a partial
map with several points is a different extension request.

The title's phrase MI tuple does not add an unlisted meet-irreducibility

- If Hstar=Id or some C_i=Id, the theorem still applies. It does not prove
