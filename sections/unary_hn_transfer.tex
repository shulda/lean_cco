\section{Unary completion and ordered Ramsey transfer}
\label{sec:unary-hn-transfer}

The results in this section concern finite many-sorted structures with
unary functions and relations. They do not use CCO, quotient geometry or
factorisation forests. Completed structures have total unary functions on
their prescribed source sorts. Partial sources use the HN convention:
functions may initially have proper domains, and induced substructures
reflect those domains and are closed under all already defined values.

\begin{lemma}[Unary-or-binary pair coverage implies irreducibility]
\label{lem:typed-unary-binary-irreducibility}
Let $A$ be a finite induced unary-closed structure, with total unary
functions and relations of arbitrary finite arity. Suppose that for every
pair of distinct vertices $x,y\in A$, at least one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $x\in\cl_A\{y\}$;
\item $y\in\cl_A\{x\}$;
\item a positive named binary relation contains $(x,y)$ or $(y,x)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $A$ is irreducible with respect to free amalgamation of induced
unary-closed substructures. In particular, a complete binary orbital
signature makes every completed member irreducible. The same holds if
some unary graph orbitals are represented only by their named functions,
provided every remaining pair satisfies (iii).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $A$ were a free amalgam of two proper induced closed factors
$A_1,A_2$ over $A_0=A_1\cap A_2$. Choose
$x\in A_1\setminus A_0$ and $y\in A_2\setminus A_0$.
Closedness gives $\cl_A\{x\}\subseteq A_1$ and
$\cl_A\{y\}\subseteq A_2$, excluding (i) and (ii).
A positive binary tuple joining $x$ and $y$ belongs to neither factor,
excluding (iii) by freeness. This contradicts the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Universal irreflexive edge restores local irreducible reflection]
\label{lem:typed-universal-edge-hn-reflection}
Let $L$ be a finite many-sorted language with unary functions and relations
of arbitrary finite arity. Let $\mathcal K$ be a hereditary class of
completed induced unary-closed $L$-structures such that every
$L$-homomorphism-embedding between two members of $\mathcal K$ is an
embedding. This condition holds, in particular, if every completed member
is irreducible, or if every homomorphism between completed members is an
embedding. Positive binary coverage of every distinct pair is a sufficient
condition by \cref{lem:typed-unary-binary-irreducibility}.

Expand each member by a universal irreflexive binary relation $E_*$, and
write $\mathcal K^*$ for the resulting class. Let $C$ be a finite partial
$(L\cup\{E_*\})$-structure whose every induced irreducible substructure
belongs to $\mathcal K^*$. If its $L$-reduct $C^-$ has an
$L$-homomorphism-embedding $h:C^-\to H$, where $H\in\mathcal K$, then
\emph{the same vertex map} is a homomorphism-embedding $C\to H^*$.
Global injectivity of $h$ is not asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an induced irreducible $D\subseteq C$, the hypothesis gives
$D\in\mathcal K^*$, hence $D^-\in\mathcal K$.
The restriction $h|_{D^-}$ is a homomorphism-embedding: every induced
irreducible substructure of $D^-$ is also such a substructure of $C^-$.
The assumed rigidity of completed maps makes this restriction an
embedding. Since $E_*$ is universal and irreflexive on both $D$ and $H^*$,
$h|_D$ is an embedding in the expanded language as well.

For any displayed $E_*^C(x,y)$, its induced unary-closed support
$D=\cl_C\{x,y\}$ is irreducible: in a free decomposition, one factor
must contain the relation tuple and hence all unary values of its
endpoints. Thus $D\in\mathcal K^*$, which also excludes $x=y$.
The preceding paragraph makes $h$ injective on $D$, so the displayed
$E_*$-edge is preserved. All $L$-relations and defined unary values are
already preserved by $h$. Together with reflection on every irreducible
source piece, this proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The completed-map rigidity hypothesis cannot be omitted]
\label{rem:typed-universal-edge-old-completeness}
Take the empty relational/function language and let $\mathcal K$ be all
finite sets. It is hereditary and has SAP, but the constant map from a
two-point set to a singleton is a homomorphism-embedding, since its old
irreducible pieces are singletons. Let $C$ be a two-point member of
$\mathcal K^*$ with $E_*$ on its distinct pair. Every irreducible source
piece is completed, but the constant map from $C^-$ to a singleton does
not preserve $E_*$: it sends its edge to a forbidden diagonal.
A different injective completion of $C$ exists; the failure concerns only
transport of the \emph{same} arbitrary old map. Thus some completed-map
rigidity is necessary for this general argument. Binary completeness is
one sufficient hypothesis, not a necessary one: unary-generated
irreducibility can provide the same rigidity.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Unary HN transfer from irreducible-source obstructions]
\label{thm:unary-hn-irreducible-source-transfer}
Let $L$ be a finite many-sorted language with relations of arbitrary finite
arity and only unary functions. Let $\mathcal K$ be a hereditary class of
finite completed induced unary-closed \emph{irreducible} $L$-structures
with SAP. Suppose there is an integer $N$ such that every finite partial
$L$-structure $C$ satisfying
\[
  \text{every induced irreducible substructure of $C$ belongs to
  $\mathcal K$}
\]
and having no $\mathcal K$-homomorphism-embedding completion contains an
induced unary-closed noncompletable substructure of size at most $N$.
Then the class $\mathcal K^<$ of all members with arbitrary strict linear
orders is locally finite in the class of all finite ordered $L$-models,
in the sense of \cite[Definition~2.8]{HN-Ramsey}, with the uniform bound
$n=\max\{1,N\}$. Consequently $\mathcal K^<$ is Ramsey.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal R$ be the class of all finite $(L,<)$-models with $<$ a
strict linear order. It is Ramsey by \cite[Theorem~2.19]{HN-Ramsey}, and
its members are irreducible because of the order. The subclass
$\mathcal K^<$ is hereditary and has SAP: first strongly amalgamate in
$\mathcal K$, then shuffle the two orders in each interval over the
common induced suborder.

Fix any $C_0\in\mathcal R$ and $n=\max\{1,N\}$. Let $C$ satisfy the
three HN local-finiteness hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $C$ has a homomorphism-embedding to $C_0$;
\item every induced irreducible substructure of $C$ belongs to
      $\mathcal K^<$;
\item every induced substructure of $C$ on at most $n$ vertices has a
      $\mathcal K^<$-completion.
\end{enumerate}
Write $C^-$ for the reduct forgetting only the order. If $D^-$ is an
induced irreducible substructure of $C^-$, adding its induced order
preserves irreducibility. By (ii), $D^-\in\mathcal K$.
Thus $C^-$ satisfies the restricted obstruction hypothesis. If it had no
completion, a noncompletable induced unary-closed $D^-$ of size at most
$N$ would contradict (iii), by restoring the displayed order and then
forgetting it from a completion. Hence $C^-$ has a
$\mathcal K$-homomorphism-embedding completion.

Apply \cite[Proposition~2.6]{HN-Ramsey} to $\mathcal K$. Its members are
irreducible, it is hereditary with SAP, and all functions are unary.
We obtain an injective homomorphism-embedding $f:C^-\hookrightarrow B$,
where $B\in\mathcal K$. By (i) the displayed order edges of $C$ are
acyclic, since a closed strict-order walk cannot map to $C_0$. Transport
these edges through $f$ and extend them to a strict linear order on $B$.
For any induced irreducible $D\subseteq C$, (ii) says that $D$ is already
a completed ordered member. In particular, its reduct $D^-$ is an
irreducible member of $\mathcal K$, so $f|_{D^-}$ is an embedding.
Its source order is total, and extending all its comparisons therefore
also reflects that order. Thus $f|_D$ is an ordered embedding. The map
$f$ is the required strong $\mathcal K^<$-completion of $C$.

This proves local finiteness with the stated uniform bound, independently
of $C_0$. The locally finite Ramsey subclass theorem
\cite[Theorem~2.11]{HN-Ramsey} gives the Ramsey conclusion. No unary
closure-size bound or condition on arbitrary unary-malformed sources was
used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Direct unary HN transfer for irreducible SAP ages]
\label{thm:typed-direct-irreducible-unary-HN}
Let $L$ be a finite many-sorted language with relations of arbitrary finite
arity and only total unary functions on their prescribed source sorts.
Let $\mathcal K$ be a hereditary class of finite induced unary-closed
irreducible $L$-structures with SAP. Suppose there is one integer $N$
such that every noncompletable finite partial $L$-structure whose every
one-generated induced closure is already a completed member of
$\mathcal K$ contains an induced unary-closed noncompletable substructure
of size at most $N$. Completions here are homomorphism-embeddings.
Then $\mathcal K^<$, with arbitrary strict linear orders, is locally
finite with bound $\max\{1,N\}$ and is Ramsey.

The obstruction premise is required only on this unary-admissible domain;
no bound for arbitrary malformed unary cycles is assumed. No positive
binary pair cover, auxiliary universal relation, CCO or exact geometry
is part of this abstract theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If every induced irreducible substructure of a partial source belongs to
$\mathcal K$, then \cref{lem:hn-irreducible-forces-unary-admissible}
makes each one-generated closure a completed member and forces the
intended unary function domains to be total. Hence the assumed
obstruction bound applies on the domain required by
\cref{thm:unary-hn-irreducible-source-transfer}. Apply that theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A unary homogeneous finite-obstruction Ramsey transfer]
\label{thm:typed-abstract-unary-HN-ramsey}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:typed-direct-irreducible-unary-HN},
replace the irreducibility assumption by the following sufficient
condition: the relational symbols have arity at most two, and in every
completed member every ordered pair of distinct vertices is covered by a
positive named binary relation. Then the same Ramsey conclusion holds.
In particular, the bounded-obstruction hypothesis remains restricted to
sources with completed one-generated closures; it is not supplied by
binary coverage or by this theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:typed-unary-binary-irreducibility}, positive binary coverage
makes every completed member irreducible. Apply
\cref{thm:typed-direct-irreducible-unary-HN}.
\end{proof}
