\section{Represented exact relations and exact quotient sorts}
We work throughout this section with an actual countable transitive
homogeneous $M$ with triangle-defined $\Age(M)$ and semantic
homomorphic CCO; no SAP is assumed. Let $\Id$ and $\Top$ denote,
respectively, equality and the universal binary relation on $M$.
A \emph{raw support} is a union of actual binary atomic relations,
possibly including equality. The product $RS$ is the represented
relational product $\{(x,z):\exists y\;xRy\wedge ySz\}$, not a freely
declared Boolean-table operation. Every invariant binary relation is a union of orbital relations,
and every such orbital is symmetric (by homogeneity and symmetry of
all named colours).  Products remain invariant, so $RS$ is
symmetric.  Taking converses gives $(RS)^{-1}=SR$, hence $RS=SR$. For atomic supports $a,b,c$, the
\emph{Peirce rotations} are the elementary equivalences
\[
 c\subseteq ab\ \Longleftrightarrow\ a\subseteq cb
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ b\subseteq ac,
\]
obtained by permuting the three vertices of a witnessed triangle.

A \emph{word profile} is the represented product of a finite word of
atomic relations, with the empty word interpreted as $\Id$.
A \emph{nonempty exact relation} is a nonempty finite intersection of
such profiles; the intersection of an empty family is $\Top$.
For exact equivalence relations we use the actual quotient structure
of $M$, not a formal quotient of an abstract colour algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Full typed exact calculus and heterogeneous ball Helly]
\label{thm:full-typed-exact-balls}
The following common statement also applies when the original source
has several \emph{named}, individually transitive sorts. Let $N$ be a
countable homogeneous structure in a finite complete atomic binary
language whose same-sort
colours are symmetric and whose cross-sort colours come in typed
converse pairs. Assume semantic homomorphic CCO; SAP is \emph{not}
required. In this theorem products are the \emph{actual represented}
relations, and the palette for a loop sort includes its logical
identity atom.

For each ordered original sort pair $(i,j)$, let
$\mathcal W_{ij}$ consist of the nonempty represented atomic word
profiles, with intermediate sorts arbitrary, and let
$\mathcal H^{\rm full}_{ij}$ consist of their nonempty finite
intersections, including the full typed top as the empty intersection.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item The family $\mathcal W_{ij}$ is $2$-Helly. So is
$\mathcal H^{\rm full}_{ij}$; the latter is closed under
nonempty meets and typed converse.
\item For nonempty $D=\bigcap_\alpha P_\alpha$ and
$F=\bigcap_\beta Q_\beta$ of matching types,
\[
  D;F=\bigcap_{\alpha,\beta}(P_\alpha;Q_\beta),
\]
with a typed top treated as an empty list of restrictions. The
product is nonempty and full exact, and products distribute over
nonempty finite exact meets in either argument.
\item Fix a target original sort $u$. For actual centres
$b_1,\ldots,b_n$ in \emph{arbitrary} named sorts $t_i$ and
nonempty $D_i\in\mathcal H^{\rm full}_{u,t_i}$, put
\[
  B_{D_i}(b_i)=\{z\in N_u:(z,b_i)\in D_i\}.
\]
If these balls intersect pairwise, then all of them intersect;
repeated centres and identity-bearing menus are permitted. In
particular, their pairwise-intersection test is
\[
 B_{D_i}(b_i)\cap B_{D_j}(b_j)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (b_i,b_j)\in\bar D_i;D_j.
\]
\end{enumerate}
These are full (identity-aware) statements, \emph{not} assertions
about the strong diversity-only guarded product on distinct points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Word Helly.} For each of finitely many typed words between
sorts $i,j$, delete genuine equality factors, and attach a private
path spelling the remaining diversity colours between two formal
terminal vertices. If one word is empty, its profile is the
ACTUAL old logical identity of the common loop sort.
Pairwise intersection forces that identity atom to
belong to every other represented word profile, so
the total intersection contains the identity and
the assertion follows, without attaching any
zero-length path to two distinct formal endpoints.
Otherwise any repeated length-one paths between
the two common terminals impose the same atomic
colour: singleton atomic profiles with nonempty
intersection must be identical. Merge these
duplicate specified edges, avoiding artificial
parallel edges and 2-cycles.
Every specified simple cycle in the resulting theta
consists of two different private paths with the same
two marked terminals. By pairwise intersection their
full endpoint profiles share an old atomic output
\(c\), possibly logical identity. For each path,
\(c\) belongs to its ACTUAL represented relational
product, so a word walk exists with endpoints
forming a \(c\)-pair. If \(c\ne\Id\), old
pair homogeneity moves both walks to the SAME
chosen actual ordered \(c\)-pair; if \(c=\Id\),
both walks may be based at one chosen actual
point of the appropriate loop sort. Their
private interiors may be mapped independently,
so the simple cycle has an old homomorphism.
Semantic homomorphic CCO now completes the
entire theta, possibly identifying its two
formal terminals. Its actual endpoint atom,
including the identity case, belongs to
EVERY original word profile. Neither SAP nor
injectivity of the internal word witnesses
is required. Flattening
finite meet presentations gives $2$-Helly for full exact menus.
The converse and meet assertions follow by reversing words and
combining their defining lists.

\emph{Exact convolution.} Peircean rotation for actual typed
relations, with the required converses, says that an atom
$c:i\to k$ belongs to $A;B$ iff
$A\cap(\{c\};\bar B)\ne\varnothing$. Suppose
$c\in P_\alpha;Q_\beta$ for every pair of defining words.
The word profiles $P_\alpha$ meet pairwise because their
intersection $D$ is nonempty. For every $\beta$ the profile
$\{c\};\bar Q_\beta$ is a word product; these meet pairwise,
since for a fixed $b\in F$ the nonempty product
$\{c\};\{\bar b\}$ belongs to all of them. Every cross
intersection is nonempty by typed Peirce. Word Helly therefore
gives
\[
 a\in D\cap\bigcap_\beta(\{c\};\bar Q_\beta).
\]
Now every $Q_\beta$ meets the single word profile
$\{\bar a\};\{c\}$. Apply word Helly again, using the
nonempty common intersection $F$ of the $Q_\beta$, to get
$b\in F$ with $c\in a;b$. Hence $c\in D;F$. The opposite
inclusion is monotonicity. All the products of defining words
are represented word profiles.

If one defining family is empty, the corresponding typed top
is absorbing under composition with any nonempty invariant
relation of compatible sorts: fix an arbitrary output atomic
pair and use transitivity of the intermediate original sort to
place a witness of the nonempty other factor over its appropriate
endpoint. This also covers a logical-identity output. Thus the
same formula, interpreted with the top convention, applies. The
finite-meet distributivity identities follow by flattening the
word presentations. All the products are nonempty by transitivity
of the participating original sorts.

\emph{Exact balls.} If a $D_i$ is the singleton identity relation
of sort $u$, pairwise ball intersection says its centre itself
belongs to every other ball, proving the claim. A general
nonempty menu containing an empty-word restriction is just this
case. Discard all unrestricted top menus. For each remaining
word in each presentation of $D_i$, attach a private typed
path from a new formal vertex $z$ of sort $u$ to the actual
centre $b_i$. Keep the complete atomic diagram on the distinct
centres; repeated centres are represented by the same vertex.
A nonempty invariant typed relation \(D_i:u\to t_i\)
is serial at EVERY actual centre \(b_i\in N_{t_i}\),
by transitivity of its target original named sort.
Thus each individual ball is nonempty.
Consider two length-one constraints from \(z\)
to the SAME old centre point \(b_i=b_j\).
When they come from the same exact menu,
a witness in its nonempty ball satisfies
both constituent word profiles. When they
come from different menus, a witness in the
assumed pairwise intersection of those two
balls satisfies both. Since each length-one
profile is a SINGLE old atomic relation,
the two atoms must be equal. Merge these
duplicate edges rather than retaining
an artificial parallel-edge cycle.
Repeated centre values are represented by
ONE formal old centre vertex, not two
different vertices.

A specified simple cycle avoiding \(z\) is a cycle
in the ACTUAL complete centre diagram. Every simple
cycle containing \(z\) traverses two different
private paths and a route in the COMPLETE
actual old-centre diagram between their
attached centres. This route may be EMPTY
if the two paths attach to the SAME old
centre vertex; otherwise it is a positive
path of old recorded centre--centre atoms.
The private path interiors are disjoint,
so the cycle needs no further artificial
compatibility assumption. Suppose the two private paths belong to the
menus \(D_i,D_j\) at actual centres \(b_i,b_j\),
which MAY be the same old point. Choose a
real witness \(w\) in
\(B_{D_i}(b_i)\cap B_{D_j}(b_j)\).
For \(i\ne j\) this is nonempty by the
hypothesis of PAIRWISE ball intersection,
even when \(b_i=b_j\). For \(i=j\) it
is the one nonempty ball, by seriality.
In particular, if two DIFFERENT menus share
one centre, it would be incorrect to choose
an arbitrary witness of only one of them.
Since \(D_i\) and \(D_j\) lie in EVERY word
of their respective exact presentations,
there exist actual word walks from \(w\)
to the selected centre endpoint for EACH
chosen private path. Map the private
interiors independently, fixing \(w\) and
the ACTUAL old centre points; all old
centre-route edges retain their prescribed
atomic colours. This proves a homomorphism
of the chosen simple cycle, including
repeated-centre and identity-output cases.
Semantic CCO completes the entire diagram.

The centre images form a genuine induced
embedding: each pair of DISTINCT old centres
has its ACTUAL specified irreflexive atomic
edge, so they cannot be identified.
Homogeneity of the original finite typed
centre diagram moves these images back
to the prescribed actual centres. The image
of \(z\) then belongs to every required
full exact ball. It may equal one centre
when the exact relation permits the actual
identity; no artificial equality-labelled
diversity edge was introduced. The displayed pairwise criterion follows directly
from typed relational composition and converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Full typed exact saturation commutes with nonempty meets]
\label{lem:full-typed-exact-saturation}
Let $a,b$ be named transitive original sorts.
Let $E_a$ and $E_b$ be full exact invariant
equivalences on them, and let
$D_1,\ldots,D_m\subseteq L_{ab}$ be
nonempty full exact typed relations with
\[
                    D=\bigcap_{i=1}^mD_i\ne\varnothing.
\]
All products are \emph{actual represented},
including equality on same-sort endpoint types.
Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 E_a;D;E_b=\bigcap_{i=1}^m(E_a;D_i;E_b).
 \quad}\tag{ES}\label{eq:foundational-exact-saturation}
\]
In particular the right-hand side is a
nonempty full exact relation. For an
empty index family take $D=L_{ab}$;
both sides are the full typed top.

The nonempty-intersection assumption is
essential: no preservation of arbitrary
empty exact intersections is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a nonempty family, apply twice the
nonempty exact finite-meet distributivity
from \cref{thm:full-typed-exact-balls}(ii).
First, with fixed exact nonempty $E_a$,
\[
 E_a;\Bigl(\bigcap_iD_i\Bigr)
     =\bigcap_i(E_a;D_i).
\]
Each displayed factor and the intersection
are nonempty full exact by that same theorem.
Now compose on the right with $E_b$ and
apply the distributivity once again:
\[
 \Bigl(\bigcap_iE_a;D_i\Bigr);E_b
     =\bigcap_i(E_a;D_i;E_b).
\]
This argument respects the ordered
sorts and converse symbols and does not
use commutativity of mixed arrows.
For the empty family both sides are
the top relation since composition of
typed top with any nonempty invariant
relation of compatible transitive
sorts is top.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Word-profile Helly and exact product calculus]
\label{lem:exact-product}
For nonempty word profiles $P_1,\dots,P_m$ whose pairwise
intersections are nonempty, the total intersection is nonempty.
Consequently the nonempty exact supports enjoy the same $2$-Helly
property (flatten all their word presentations: pairwise
intersection of the exact supports implies pairwise intersection
of every selected pair of words) and are closed under product; more precisely, if
$E=\bigcap_iP_i\ne\varnothing$ and
$F=\bigcap_jQ_j\ne\varnothing$, then
\[
 E\,a=\bigcap_i(P_i a),\qquad
 EF=\bigcap_{i,j}(P_iQ_j),
\]
where $a$ is an atomic relation and the empty defining families
receive their evident $\Top$ convention.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:full-typed-exact-balls}(i),(ii) to its
single-original-sort case. Here same-sort converse is trivial,
so the full typed Peirce law becomes the symmetric relation
identities displayed above. Equality outputs are retained in
these raw products.
\end{proof}

For an exact relation $R$ and a real point $x$, set
$R[x]=\{y:yRx\}$.
\begin{lemma}[Exact-ball Helly with different centres]\label{lem:exact-balls}
Let $x_1,\dots,x_m\in M$ and let $R_1,\dots,R_m$ be exact relations.
If $R_i[x_i]\cap R_j[x_j]\ne\varnothing$ for every $i,j$, then
$\bigcap_iR_i[x_i]\ne\varnothing$. Moreover
\[
 R_i[x_i]\cap R_j[x_j]\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (x_i,x_j)\in R_iR_j.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{thm:full-typed-exact-balls}(iii) with one
original named sort. Symmetry makes $\bar R_i=R_i$, so the
typed pairwise criterion becomes $(x_i,x_j)\in R_iR_j$.
\end{proof}


\begin{example}[Why exactness is essential for ball Helly]
\label{ex:exact-ball-sharp}
Let $V=\mathbb F_2^2$, and for every nonzero $\delta\in V$ name the
symmetric diversity colour $a_\delta(x,y)$ given by $y-x=\delta$.
The resulting four-point structure is transitive and homogeneous:
all finite partial colour isomorphisms are restrictions of
translations.  Its age is triangle-defined (the three differences
around each legal triangle sum to zero), has AP, and has semantic
homomorphic CCO: a labelled partial graph maps to $V$ precisely
when the edge differences sum to zero on every specified cycle.
Choose independent $e_1,e_2\in V$ and put
\[
 A_1=\Id\cup a_{e_1},\qquad A_2=\Id\cup a_{e_2},
 \qquad A_3=a_{e_1}\cup a_{e_2}.
\]
The three balls with common centre $0$ are respectively
$\{0,e_1\}$, $\{0,e_2\}$, and $\{e_1,e_2\}$.
They intersect pairwise but have empty total intersection.
These supports are invariant, but are \emph{not} exact: every
word profile is one difference atom (including $\Id$), so every
nonempty exact support is either one atom or $\Top$.
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-balls} cannot be applied to arbitrary
invariant supports in place of exact ones.
\end{example}
