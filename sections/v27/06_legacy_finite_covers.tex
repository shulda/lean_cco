\section{Finite covers and exact-complement elimination}
\label{sec:finite-covers}

We now remove the terminal hypothesis \ref{A:cover} for the Ramsey theorem.
The finite-cover reduction is the most delicate part of the general argument,
and we give the complete pair-shadow interface rather than referring to a
``phase split'' as a black box.  At intermediate stages the realised unary
$1$-types are treated as finitely many \emph{sorts}; all path profiles and
exact domains below are typed by their endpoint sorts.

\subsection{Orbital parity and index two}

The general SAP collision-unfolding lemma preserving frozen complete
higher-support factors is now foundational,
\cref{lem:collision-unfolding}. Orbital parity below instead uses the
SAP-free old parity theorem plus typed STRONG injectivisation.


\begin{theorem}[Orbital parity]\label{thm:orbital-parity}
Let $E<F$ be $0$-definable equivalences on one current
transitive unary sort and suppose $[F:E]=n<\infty$.
If $t\subseteq F\setminus E$ is a canonical diversity
pair orbit, then there are actual invariant equivalences
\[
 Y_t<E_t\leq F,\qquad [E_t:Y_t]=2,
\]
where $Y_t$ is the FULL RAW exact even-walk equivalence
(including real equality) and
$O_t=E_t\setminus Y_t$ is the odd-walk relation.
In the present SAP source, $Y_t>\Id$; moreover its
\emph{diversity part} $Y_t^\circ=Y_t\setminus\Id$
and $O_t$ are nonempty STRONG reachable profiles.

The distinction between the RAW equivalence $Y_t$
and its identity-free STRONG profile $Y_t^\circ$
is essential: no logical equality is inserted as
an artificial diversity edge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the SAP-free ORIGINAL orbital-parity theorem
\cref{lem:ep-ap-orbital-parity} to the named
homogeneous current sort and the actual invariant
interval $E<F$. It supplies bipartiteness, the
even/odd represented RAW word relations and the
index-two equivalence interval, with $Y_t$
FULL RAW exact. In particular the eventual even
and odd powers of the ORIGINAL atomic relation $t$
stabilise to $Y_t$ and $O_t$, respectively.

The current source also has visible SAP, so
\cref{lem:typed-injectivisation} identifies the
DIVERSITY slices of these positive represented
words with their corresponding STRONG
simple-path profiles. The odd slice $O_t$ is
nonempty because it contains $t$ itself.

Finally take an actual old $t$-edge $xy$.
Strongly amalgamate two copies of this
two-point structure over the common real
vertex $y$. Their two copies $x_1\ne x_2$
have a common $t$-neighbour $y$, hence
$(x_1,x_2)\in Y_t\setminus\Id$.
Thus $Y_t^\circ\ne\varnothing$, and the
stabilised positive even word represents
the claimed nonempty STRONG profile.
No independent collision-unfolding argument
or strengthening of the original AP-only
parity theorem was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite covers have index two]
\label{thm:finite-cover-two}
Let $E_X\prec E_Y$ be a cover in the lattice $\Lambda$ of
\cref{prop:lattice-interface}. If $[Y:X]$ is finite, then
$[Y:X]=2$. Hence any finite relative index between such
invariant equivalences is a power of two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is the full invariant-lattice SAP-free result
\cref{thm:full-relative-Hasse}, applied to this one-sort
SAP+CCO source. The proof is independent of SAP and appears
in Foundations so that it also applies to AP-only exact quotients.
\end{proof}

The index-two conclusion does not mean that an arbitrary such cover may be
oriented directly.  The following example is deliberately coarse: its bad
cover is not a reachable path profile.

\begin{proposition}[A preselected two-cover need not be splittable]
\label{prop:bad-diagonal-split}
There is a nine-colour symmetric triangle-constrained SAP+CCO class with a
definable cover $K<X$, $[X:K]=2$, such that every unary orientation of the two
$K$-classes inside each $X$-class destroys AP.  The same structure has two
finer orbital coordinate covers which can be split successively.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $U,V,N$ be countably infinite pure sets and put
\[
 M=U\times V\times\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2\times N.
\]
Write $x=(u_x,v_x,p_x,q_x,n_x)$.  For distinct $x,y$, the first coordinate
colour is $e_i$ if $u_x=u_y$ and $p_x+p_y=i$, and is $n$ if $u_x\ne u_y$.
Define the second coordinate colour analogously from $(v,q)$.  Thus
\[
 L=\{e_0,e_1,n\}^2.
\]
The two coordinates are independent.  A finite colour-preserving partial map
extends by extending the used permutations of $U,V$, the local shifts
$p\mapsto p+a(u)$ and $q\mapsto q+b(v)$, and finally the clone permutations.
Hence the structure is homogeneous.  Infinite clone fibres also give SAP.

For one coordinate the product is
\[
 e_i\starprod e_j=\{e_{i+j}\},\qquad
 e_i\starprod n=n\starprod e_i=\{n\},\qquad
 n\starprod n=\{e_0,e_1,n\}.
\]
Its reachable profiles are exactly
$\{e_0\},\{e_1\},\{n\}$ and the full three-element palette, hence are
$2$-Helly.  The nine-colour algebra is their direct product, so its reachable
profiles are boxes and are again $2$-Helly.  This proves semantic CCO; the
coordinate description shows that the resulting triangle table is exactly
the age just described.

Let $X$ mean equality of the $U$- and $V$-coordinates.  Its palette is
\[
 X=\{e_0,e_1\}\times\{e_0,e_1\}.
\]
Inside an $X$-class let $K$ additionally remember $p+q$; its palette is the
diagonal
\[
 K=\{(e_0,e_0),(e_1,e_1)\}.
\]
Then $[X:K]=2$.  Every unary predicate $P$ orienting these two $K$-classes has
the form
\[
 P(u,v,p,q,n)=p+q+h(u,v)
\tag{3}\label{eq:bad-diagonal-orientation}
\]
for some function $h:U\times V\to\mathbb F_2$.

Choose distinct $u_0,u_1$ and $v_0,v_1$, put
$h_{ij}=h(u_i,v_j)$, and for $\varepsilon\in\mathbb F_2$ choose fresh clones
\[
 b_\varepsilon=(u_0,v_0,\varepsilon,\varepsilon+h_{00}),
 \qquad d=(u_1,v_1,h_{11},0),
 \qquad z_\varepsilon=(u_0,v_1,\varepsilon,0).
\]
Then $P(b_\varepsilon)=P(d)=0$ while
$P(z_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon+h_{01}$.  Independently of $\varepsilon$,
\[
 \operatorname{col}(b_\varepsilon,d)=(n,n),\qquad
 \operatorname{col}(b_\varepsilon,z_\varepsilon)=(e_0,n),\qquad
 \operatorname{col}(z_\varepsilon,d)=(n,e_0).
\]
Thus the two expanded triangles
$\{b_0,d,z_0\}$ and $\{b_1,d,z_1\}$ have isomorphic bases
$\{b_0,d\}\cong\{b_1,d\}$ but prescribe opposite unary values on their new
vertices.  In any old-language amalgam the two paths through the first base
point force
\[
 \operatorname{col}(z_0,z_1)
 \in\{(e_0,e_0),(e_0,e_1),(e_0,n)\},
\]
while the paths through the second force
\[
 \operatorname{col}(z_0,z_1)
 \in\{(e_0,e_0),(e_1,e_0),(n,e_0)\}.
\]
The intersection is the singleton $\{(e_0,e_0)\}\subseteq K$.  The
orientation axiom therefore requires equal $P$-values, contradicting the two
extensions.  Hence every orientation fails AP.

On the other hand the two finer coordinate covers
\[
 \{(e_0,e_0)\}
 <\{(e_0,e_0),(e_1,e_0)\},
 \qquad
 \{(e_0,e_0)\}
 <\{(e_0,e_0),(e_0,e_1)\}
\]
are generated by individual coordinate orbitals and can be split
successively.  The obstruction therefore concerns a coarse preselected
cover, not the bottom-up orbital construction used below.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact-complement pivot in a typed pair shadow}

For current unary sorts $a,b$, let $\mathcal T^{ab}$ denote the finite set of
canonical raw pair types from $a$ to $b$.  A typed exact domain is a nonempty
finite intersection of typed reachable profiles.  If $K_a\subseteq
\mathcal T^{aa}$ and $K_b\subseteq\mathcal T^{bb}$ are exact equivalences,
write
\[
 \operatorname{Sat}_{K_a,K_b}(D)
   =K_a\starprod D\starprod K_b.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Typed strong exact saturation commutes with nonempty meets]
\label{lem:exact-saturation-intersection}
Let $K_a\in\mathcal H_{aa}$ and $K_b\in\mathcal H_{bb}$
be nonempty strong exact equivalence palettes in the
current SAP+homomorphic-CCO pair-shadow calculus.
If the nonempty strong exact menus
$D_i\subseteq\mathcal T^{ab}$ have a
\emph{nonempty} common exact intersection
$D=\bigcap_iD_i$, then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \bigcap_i(K_a\starprod D_i\starprod K_b)
     =K_a\starprod D\starprod K_b .
 \quad}
\]
Every product is the supported STRONG diversity product
of distinct-vertex legal triangles. This is a separate
application of the strong exact calculus, not the
equality-inclusive raw identity
\cref{lem:full-typed-exact-saturation}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The strong typed distributivity
\cref{lem:typed-exact-products,eq:typed-distrib}
gives
\[
 K_a\starprod\Bigl(\bigcap_iD_i\Bigr)
       =\bigcap_i(K_a\starprod D_i).
\]
All factors and their common intersection
are nonempty strong exact menus under the
hypotheses of that theorem. Compose on the
right by the nonempty exact $K_b$ and use the
same distributivity once more. This yields
the displayed equation. No actual represented
logical identity was introduced into the
off-diagonal palette; in particular the result
makes no assertion for AP-only networks where
formal vertices can be identified.
\end{proof}

For an exact domain $A$ containing the identity cell, let
$\langle A\rangle$ denote the stable exact equivalence obtained from the
increasing powers of $A$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact closure separation]
\label{thm:closure-separation}
Let $A,B$ be nonempty full exact same-sort domains in the
\emph{represented typed pair shadow}, both containing its identity
cell $e$. If $A\cap B=\{e\}$, then
\[
 \langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle=\{e\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the relative-$K$ theorem
\cref{lem:ep-relative-K-closure} on the actual homogeneous
AP+CCO sort represented by this shadow, using its own equality
orbital as $K$. When the shadow is a quotient of an earlier real
sort, its identity cell pulls back to the \emph{whole quotient
kernel}, not necessarily to original real equality. Equivalently,
one may apply the same relative-$K$ theorem to those full
$K$-bisaturated old exact relations. In either reading, products
are actual represented products, not an artificial unit adjoined
to diversity relations.
\end{proof}

Fix a source sort $a_*$ and an orbital parity cover
\[
 Y<X,
 \qquad O=X\setminus Y,
 \qquad [X:Y]=2.
\]
Choose an inclusion-maximal exact equivalence $K_*$ satisfying
\[
 Y\leq K_*,
 \qquad K_*\cap X=Y.
\tag{4}\label{eq:max-exact}
\]
Such a $K_*$ exists because $Y$ is full exact; the
even and odd orbit relations $Y,O$ are represented
stabilized word profiles. Put
$F_*=K_*\vee X$ and, in the
$K_*$-semantic quotient, write
\[
 e_*=K_*,\qquad
 t_*=K_*\starprod O\starprod K_*.
\]
By the common SAP-free maximal parity-gate theorem
\cref{thm:foundational-maximal-parity-gate},
the relation $t_*$ is a nonidentity full exact
involution, with $t_*^2=e_*$ and
$[F_*:K_*]=2$.



The common source theorem
\cref{thm:foundational-rigid-groupoid} constructs all exact
functional bridges for arbitrary named transitive original
sorts in the \emph{represented full exact} AP+CCO algebra.
Here we apply it to the current homogeneous typed pair shadow
at the maximal gate $K_*$ from \eqref{eq:max-exact}.
In particular the exact-bridge equations are
\eqref{eq:exact-bridge} from Foundations;
no new formal identity cell is adjoined.

\begin{proposition}[Rigid bridge groupoid]
\label{prop:rigid-bridge-groupoid}
Starting from $(a_*,K_*)$ and closing under full exact
bridges gives one finite connected component of current sorts.
For each reached sort $b$ its exact kernel $K_b$ is
path-independent. The quotient-functional $K_a,K_b$-bisaturated
full exact correspondences form the hom-set
$\mathcal H(a,b)$ of a connected groupoid with finite
elementary abelian $2$-group isotropy; every nonempty hom-set
is a torsor. In addition, for any bridge $R:a_*\to b$,
\[
             t_b=R^\smile t_*R
\]
is a path-independent nonidentity involution in the
$K_b$-semantic quotient, and every nonidentity exact
equivalence on that quotient contains $t_b$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The finite groupoid, kernels, torsors and isotropy follow from
\cref{thm:foundational-rigid-groupoid}. The source involution
$t_*$ is an original full exact nonidentity isotropy arrow;
\cref{thm:foundational-maximal-parity-gate} states that it is
contained in every original full exact equivalence strictly
above $K_*$. Apply the common dominant-involution transport
lemma \cref{lem:foundational-rigid-gate-transport} to obtain
path-independence, nontriviality and the required inclusion
in every nonidentity full exact quotient equivalence at every
reached object. The quotient conclusion uses only the full exact
inverse-image direction of quotient-word pullback; it does not
assert unrestricted exact descent of mixed old relations.
\end{proof}





Choose base bridges $B_b:a_*\to b$, with
$B_{a_*}=K_*$. Since the finite isotropy group
$\mathcal H_*$ is elementary abelian and
$t_*\ne K_*$, choose a linear character
$\chi_*:\mathcal H_*\to\mathbb F_2$ with
$\chi_*(t_*)=1$. Every arrow $R:b\to c$ has a
unique representation $R=B_b^\smile hB_c$ with
$h\in\mathcal H_*$. Define
\[
 \chi(R)=\chi_*(h).
 \tag{6}\label{eq:groupoid-character}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Groupoid phase labelling]
\label{prop:groupoid-phase}
The character $\chi$ is a functor to $\mathbb F_2$,
with $\chi(t_b)=1$ for every reached sort $b$.
The quotient points admit global binary labels
satisfying
\[
 \lambda(Rq)=\lambda(q)+\chi(R).
 \tag{7}\label{eq:quotient-phase}
\]
A raw orbital whose $K_a,K_b$-semantic cylinder is
rigid has exactly its forced character phase; a
nonrigid cylinder admits both phase differences.
All finite old structures admit such phase
markings, hence the unary phase expansion is
reduct-surjective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The functoriality, freeness, global seed assignment,
rigid/nonrigid dichotomy and reduct-surjectivity
are \cref{thm:foundational-rigid-character},
applied with distinguished $t_*$.
The selected source parity cover is actually
oriented because $t_*$ has character one.
These conclusions alone do not prove SAP or CCO
of the marked age: they are verified separately
by phase totality/primality below.
\end{proof}

For component sorts $a,b$ and
$\delta\in\mathbb F_2$, let $B_{ab}^{\delta}$
be the union of rigid arrows of character
$\delta$. Nonrigid cylinders belong to neither
deterministic sheet. The allowed raw pair
types at endpoint phase difference $\delta$ are
\[
 A_{ab}^{\delta}
  = B_{ab}^{\delta}\cup
    \{\text{raw types in nonrigid cylinders}\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Rigid-product factorisation]
\label{lem:rigid-product-factorisation}
Let $A\subseteq\mathcal T^{ab}$ and
$B\subseteq\mathcal T^{bc}$ be nonempty
reachable \emph{represented} profiles.
If $AB\subseteq R$ for one rigid arrow
$R\in\mathcal H(a,c)$, then $b$ is in the
same rigid component and there are arrows
$R_1\in\mathcal H(a,b)$,
$R_2\in\mathcal H(b,c)$ with
\[
 A\subseteq R_1,\qquad B\subseteq R_2,
                  \qquad R_1R_2=R.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{lem:foundational-rigid-factor}
in the current full exact typed pair shadow.
Its proof uses only seriality, exact convolution
and actual represented composition; no source SAP.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Totality of the lifted phase product]
\label{lem:phase-totality}
Every compatible pair of allowed lifted atomic pair types has an output with
the required xor of endpoint phase differences.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only the case in which both output endpoint sorts lie in the rigid component
is nontrivial.  Suppose the two input phase differences are $\alpha,\beta$,
but every raw output has phase different from $\alpha+\beta$.  Any output in
a nonrigid cylinder would be phase-flexible, so after bisaturating the
nonempty raw output profile by the endpoint exact equivalences we obtain a
nonempty exact semantic domain $D$ contained in the rigid hom-torsor
$\mathcal H(a,c)$, all of whose arrows have the same wrong character.

By \cref{lem:foundational-rigid-affine}, $D=dS$ for an exact subgroup
$S\le\mathcal H_a$.  Constancy of $\chi$ on $D$ gives
$S\le\ker\chi$.  If $S$ is nontrivial, it is a nonidentity exact equivalence
in the $K_a$-semantic quotient and cannot contain $t_a$, since
$\chi(t_a)=1$.  This contradicts the transported exact gate in
\cref{prop:rigid-bridge-groupoid}.  Hence $S$ is trivial and the output
profile lies in one rigid bridge.  By
\cref{lem:rigid-product-factorisation} both input profiles lie in rigid
bridges, whose characters add to the character of the output bridge.  This is
exactly the required phase $\alpha+\beta$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Typed exact phase primality]
\label{thm:typed-epp}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_m\subseteq\mathcal T^{ab}$ be reachable profiles between
component sorts.  If
\[
 \varnothing\ne\bigcap_iP_i\subseteq B_{ab}^{\delta},
\tag{8}\label{eq:epp-hyp}
\]
then $P_j\subseteq B_{ab}^{\delta}$ for some $j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bisaturate $A_i=K_aP_iK_b$.  By
\cref{lem:exact-saturation-intersection}, their intersection is the
saturation of the old nonempty intersection, so it is nonempty and contained
in $B_{ab}^{\delta}$.  Suppose no $A_i$ is contained in that deterministic
sheet and choose a deletion-minimal counterexample.  Put $D=\bigcap_iA_i$.
It is an exact domain contained in the rigid hom-torsor.  Hence
$D=dS$ by \cref{lem:foundational-rigid-affine}, with
$S\le\ker\chi$.  The transported exact gate forces $S$ to be trivial.
Therefore $D=\{d\}$ at the semantic level.

Right-translate every $A_i$ by $d^{\smile}$.  This preserves exactness and all
intersections, turns the cross-sort domains into same-sort domains at $a$, and
sends the deterministic sheet to phase $0$.  We may therefore assume
\[
 \bigcap_iA_i=\{K_a\}.
\]
Put $A=A_1$ and $B=\bigcap_{i>1}A_i$.  Both are nonempty exact same-sort
domains containing $K_a$, and $A\cap B=\{K_a\}$.  Deletion-minimality says
that neither $A$ nor $B$ is contained in phase $0$; consequently both stable
exact-equivalence closures are nonidentity.  Exact closure separation,
\cref{thm:closure-separation}, gives
\[
 \langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle=\{K_a\},
\]
whereas the transported exact gate forces both nonidentity closures to contain
$t_a$.  Contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Phase-primality sufficiency]
\label{lem:phase-primality-sufficiency}
Assume a finite typed unary phase sector is reduct-surjective, has total lifted
atomic products, and satisfies \cref{thm:typed-epp}.  If a lifted path has old
endpoint profile $P$ and total endpoint phase difference $\varepsilon$, then
its endpoint profile is exactly
\[
 P\cap A_{ab}^{\varepsilon}.
\tag{9}\label{eq:lifted-profile-normal-form}
\]
Consequently the lifted product is associative, its reachable profiles are
typed $2$-Helly, and the unary expansion has SAP and semantic CCO.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induct on path length. The one-edge case is the definition.
To keep all typed converses explicit, write the intermediate
sorts as $i,j,k$: the prefix profile is
$S:i\to j$, the final old atom $b:j\to k$,
and the desired output atom $c:i\to k$.
Let their phases be $\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon$,
respectively, with $\varepsilon=\alpha+\beta$.
The forward inclusion in
\eqref{eq:lifted-profile-normal-form} is formal.
For the converse choose
\[
 c\in(S\starprod\{b\})\cap A_{ik}^{\varepsilon},
 \qquad T=\{c\}\starprod\{\bar b\}
                 \subseteq L_{ij}.
\]
The original expression $c\starprod b$ would
be \emph{ill-typed} for different intermediate
sorts. The typed Peirce equivalence gives
$S\cap T\ne\varnothing$ directly from
$c\in S\starprod\{b\}$.
By induction $S\cap A_{ij}^{\alpha}\ne\varnothing$.
Totality of the lifted fork with colours
$(c,\varepsilon)$ and $(\bar b,\beta)$
gives $T\cap A_{ij}^{\alpha}\ne\varnothing$;
reversal preserves a binary phase difference
because $\mathbb F_2$ has exponent two.
If
$S\cap T\cap A_{ij}^{\alpha}$ were empty, then
the nonempty intersection $S\cap T$ would lie
in the opposite deterministic sheet
$B_{ij}^{1-\alpha}$.  The two-profile case of
\cref{thm:typed-epp} would put $S$ or $T$ wholly in that wrong sheet,
contradicting the two phase-compatible witnesses.  Hence a correct-phase
intermediate colour exists and the induction closes.

The normal form depends only on the old word and xor of the edge phases, so
the two parenthesisations of a product agree; the lifted product is
associative.  Finally let $P_i\cap A^{\varepsilon}$ be pairwise-intersecting
lifted reachable profiles.  Old $2$-Helly gives
$D=\bigcap_iP_i\ne\varnothing$.  If
$D\cap A^{\varepsilon}=\varnothing$, then
$D\subseteq B^{1-\varepsilon}$, and full exact phase primality puts one
$P_j$ wholly in the wrong sheet, contradicting nonemptiness of its lifted
profile. Thus the lifted profiles are $2$-Helly. The fixed-vertex
completion criterion \cref{lem:helly-to-cco}, applied to the full lifted
triangle class and its total realisable forks, gives SAP and semantic CCO.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Safe exact-complement split]
\label{thm:safe-exact-split}
Suppose a current unary sort of a finite-sorted symmetric SAP+CCO pair-shadow
has a proper finite relative index.  Then there is a finite precompact unary
expansion which is reduct-surjective, preserves SAP and CCO, and orients an
index-two orbital parity layer.  The split is chosen from a maximal exact
complement; it need not orient a preassigned finite cover.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use \cref{thm:orbital-parity} to choose a reachable parity cover $Y<X$ and
choose the maximal exact complement $K_*$ in \eqref{eq:max-exact}.  The rigid
bridge groupoid and its character are given by
\cref{prop:rigid-bridge-groupoid,prop:groupoid-phase}.  The latter gives a
finite unary phase expansion which is reduct-surjective and which orients the
source parity cover.  Totality of its pair product is
\cref{lem:phase-totality}, and exact phase primality is
\cref{thm:typed-epp}.  Apply
\cref{lem:phase-primality-sufficiency}.  The resulting pair-shadow therefore
has SAP and semantic CCO.  All participating sorts are synchronised by the
single groupoid character; sorts outside the rigid component receive the
constant new bit $0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite termination and the typed terminal interface}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-cover elimination]
\label{thm:finite-cover-elimination}
Starting from a class satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:general}, finitely many
applications of \cref{thm:safe-exact-split} produce a finite precompact unary
expansion $\K^{\mathrm{term}}$ which has SAP and CCO and in which every proper
same-sort cover of definable equivalences has infinite relative index.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At each stage regard the realised unary valuations as
current named sorts. The current age retains SAP and
semantic CCO by the preceding safe split theorem.
If a sort still has a proper finite relative
equivalence index,
\cref{thm:orbital-parity,thm:safe-exact-split}
provide a genuine maximal character split.
Every participating sort has a nontrivial
phase-one same-sort involution, whose old
atomic support is absent inside each child.
Consequently the common rank-drop lemma
\cref{lem:exact-character-rank-drop}
applies to the positive integer
\[
                      \Phi=\sum_a3^{k_a},
\]
where $k_a$ counts the current nonidentity
same-sort pair orbitals. Each nontrivial step
strictly decreases $\Phi$, and thus only
finitely many steps are possible.
At termination no proper finite same-sort
relative index remains, since the same
parity/safe-split argument would then allow
another step.

This has the same numerical descent as the
SAP-free foundational result
\cref{thm:ap-finite-character-elimination},
but the current V27 safe splits have the
\emph{additional SAP preservation} proved
by \cref{thm:safe-exact-split}. Finiteness,
precompactness and reduct-surjectivity persist
under finite composition of unary expansions.
\end{proof}

The preceding argument was formulated at the canonical pair-shadow level.  We
record separately the full-language interface needed both for higher arity and
for the terminal Ramsey/EPPA theorems.

\begin{lemma}[Root-free phase-safe detours]
\label{lem:root-free-phase-safe}
Fix one safe split and let $c\in\mathcal T^{ab}$ be an old canonical pair
type together with an allowed endpoint phase difference $\varepsilon$.
There is a finite path gadget $W(c,\varepsilon)$ between two distinct roots
$x,y$ of sorts $a,b$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the root pair $xy$ itself is left unspecified and every internal vertex
      is private;
\item every consecutive edge carries a realised complete canonical
      two-point type with phase-compatible endpoint bits, and the gadget
      carries no relation tuple of support at least three;
\item if $P(c)$ is the old endpoint profile of the path, then
      $c\in P(c)$ and every member of $P(c)$ is compatible with the prescribed
      endpoint phases.
\end{enumerate}
If both endpoint sorts lie in the active rigid component, one may choose a
three-edge detour and, with
$C(c)=K_a\starprod\{c\}\starprod K_b$, arrange
\[
        c\in P(c)\subseteq C(c).
\]
No assertion that $C(c)$ itself is reachable or exact is made.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose that $a,b$ lie in the active rigid component.  Choose a global
phase labelling supplied by \cref{prop:groupoid-phase} which realises the
allowed lifted pair $(c,\varepsilon)$ on actual roots $x,y$.  Let
$B_a:a_*\to a$ and $B_b:a_*\to b$ be the fixed base bridges used in
\eqref{eq:groupoid-character}.  Choose distinct source representatives
$u,v$ with $uB_ax$ and $vB_by$; infinitude of the relevant source
$K_*$-classes, obtained from SAP, permits the representatives to be chosen
fresh even when the two quotient classes coincide.  Retain only the path
\[
        x-u-v-y.
\]
Write
$r_a=\operatorname{tp}_2(x,u)$,
$d=\operatorname{tp}_2(u,v)$ and
$r_b=\operatorname{tp}_2(v,y)$.  The actual quadruple gives
$c\in r_a\starprod d\starprod r_b$.  Moreover
\[
 \{r_a\}\subseteq B_a^{\smile},\qquad
 \{d\}\subseteq B_a\starprod C(c)\starprod B_b^{\smile},\qquad
 \{r_b\}\subseteq B_b,
\]
so associativity and the bridge identities give
\[
 P(c)=\{r_a\}\starprod\{d\}\starprod\{r_b\}
 \subseteq
 B_a^{\smile}B_a\starprod C(c)\starprod
 B_b^{\smile}B_b
 =K_a\starprod C(c)\starprod K_b=C(c).
\]
If $C(c)$ is rigid, every type in it has the unique phase difference
$\varepsilon$; if it is nonrigid, \cref{prop:groupoid-phase} says that both
phase differences are allowed.  Thus every possible root type forced by this
path is phase-compatible.

If at least one endpoint sort is outside the active component, realise
$(c,\varepsilon)$ on an actual pair $x,y$, choose a fresh point $z$, and
retain only the two complete two-point structures on $xz$ and $zy$.  Their
endpoint profile contains $c$.  The phase sector imposes no old pair-type
restriction through a nonactive endpoint (and nonactive sorts carry the
constant bit $0$ when both endpoints are nonactive), so every possible root
type of this two-edge path is again phase-compatible.  In both cases the
internal vertices are made private and no higher-support tuple is retained.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full-language finite-cover elimination]
\label{thm:full-language-elimination}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a homogeneous class in a finite relational language.
Pass to the finite quantifier-free definitional expansion naming all realised
unary and canonical pair types.  Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item all canonical pair types are symmetric;
\item the canonical pair shadow has SAP and semantic CCO;
\item complete old substructures are irreducible in the working expansion,
      and higher-support relations are \emph{support-normal} in the following
      explicit sense.  Whenever all specified support-$\ge3$ tuples of a
      finite partial canonical structure lie in finitely many distinguished
      complete old substructures and its canonical pair shadow admits a
      cycle-constrained completion fixing every pair inside those factors,
      the full partial structure admits a completion in $\mathcal C$ fixing
      each distinguished factor pointwise as an induced structure.  For the
      coherent-EPPA conclusion we moreover assume the automorphism-preserving
      form of this interface: an isomorphism-natural pair-shadow completion
      can be lifted while preserving every automorphism of the partial full
      structure which preserves the distinguished factors.
\end{enumerate}
Then the iterated exact-complement construction lifts to the full finite
language.  It yields a finite precompact unary expansion
$\mathcal C^{\mathrm{term}}$ whose reduct is $\mathcal C$, whose age has SAP,
and whose pair shadow is semantic CCO with all proper same-sort covers of
infinite relative index.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One split is lifted as follows.  Canonical pair predicates make every complete
old factor irreducible, so a homomorphism-embedding is automatically an
embedding on each such factor.  Start with a phase-compatible complete
completion of the canonical pair shadow.  For every pair of old vertices
whose old canonical two-point type was unspecified, let $(c,\varepsilon)$ be
its completed lifted pair type and attach a private copy of
$W(c,\varepsilon)$ from \cref{lem:root-free-phase-safe}, leaving the root pair
itself unspecified.  The gadgets carry no support of size at least three.  A
finite family of old complete factors is kept distinguished.

Consider a simple sparse cycle in the resulting full-language partial
structure.  If it avoids private vertices, it was already an old specified
cycle.  Otherwise degree two forces it to traverse each encountered private
gadget from one old root to the other.  Contract every such path to its chosen
completed pair type $c$.  The contracted pair cycle lies in the
phase-compatible complete pair shadow.  Realise that contracted cycle in the
old homogeneous structure and, for every contracted gadget edge, strongly
amalgamate a realisation of $W(c,\varepsilon)$ over the same endpoint
canonical two-point structure.  The expanded sparse cycle therefore
homomorphism-embeds into the old structure.  Private internal vertices occur
in no support-$\ge3$ tuple and have Gaifman degree two along their own path,
so no new irreducible higher-support obstruction can contain one.

By hypothesis (iii) of the theorem, applied
to the pair-shadow cycle completion just verified and to the frozen complete
higher-support factors, the whole sparse partial structure has a
homomorphism-embedding into the old homogeneous structure.  Any
collisions are unfolded by \cref{lem:collision-unfolding} while simultaneously
preserving all distinguished complete factors.  Restrict to the images of the
old vertices.  Originally specified canonical two-point types and every
higher-support tuple on a distinguished old factor are unchanged; on a pair
which received a gadget, the surviving path forces its induced root type to
belong to $P(c)$ and hence to be phase-compatible by
\cref{lem:root-free-phase-safe}.  Thus the restriction is a complete
phase-compatible full-language lift.

Applied to a free partial amalgam of two expanded factors, with both factors
distinguished, this proves SAP of the split class.  Applied without
distinguished factors, it proves the required full-language
cycle/irreducible-completion interface.  Notice that nowhere is a semantic
cell asserted to be a reachable profile or an exact domain.

After one split the language is still finite, pair types remain symmetric,
and the same hypotheses are preserved.  The pair-shadow potential $\Phi$ from
\cref{thm:finite-cover-elimination} therefore proves finite termination in the
full language as well.  Reduct-surjectivity at each stage is inherited from
\cref{prop:groupoid-phase}.
\end{proof}
