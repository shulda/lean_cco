\section{Typed terminal Ramsey conclusion and finite-cover transfer}
\label{sec:internal-ramsey-terminal}
\begin{lemma}[Exact real-root closure and point-generated cofinality]
\label{lem:typed-real-root-closure-cofinal}
Let $\widehat{\M}$ be the actual terminal completed
transition-enriched hidden structure, with its original real sorts
and named proper MI quotient sorts. Assume the literal unary
signature of Part~III: nonidentity real-source maps project to
proper quotients, and the remaining nonidentity maps are upward
maps or quotient transitions, with no real-valued map from a
quotient sort. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every finite set $D$ of original real points,
\[
       \cl_{\widehat{\M}}(D)\cap M_{\mathrm{real}}=D.
\tag{RCF}\label{eq:typed-real-root-closure}
\]
\item Every finite induced unary-closed completed hidden
substructure $B\subseteq\widehat{\M}$ is contained inducedly
in a finite point-generated substructure
$\cl_{\widehat{\M}}(D)$. One may take $D$ to contain the real
points of $B$ and one actual real representative of each proper
quotient vertex of $B$.
\item Every linear order on $B$ extends to a linear order on
this finite $\cl_{\widehat{\M}}(D)$.
\end{enumerate}
No automorphism section or SAP over an orphan-only base is
required. The no-real-output hypothesis is substantive: adding
a unary selector from a proper quotient back to a real point
could invalidate (i).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:typed-coordinate-normal-form}, a term starting at
an original real point is either its identity term or takes
values in a proper quotient sort. Subsequent maps stay in
quotient sorts. This proves (i); \cref{lem:no-real-algebraic-transition}
justifies this actual signature upstream.

Apply \cref{lem:foundational-root-cover-cofinal} with ambient
structure $P=\widehat{\M}$ and visible roots the length-one
tuples of original real points. The term bound is
\cref{lem:typed-unary-closure-bound}. A real hidden vertex is
its own identity-term value, and every proper quotient vertex
$b$ has an actual representative $x_b$ with
$p_{J_b}(x_b)=b$. Thus the required ambient root cover holds.
Using the given inclusion $B\subseteq P$ in that lemma's
construction gives exactly $\cl_P(D)$ containing $B$, with
$D$ as in (ii). This is induced containment because both are
induced substructures of the same actual $P$; it is not merely
a homomorphic completion. Finally any order on a finite subset
extends to its finite superset, proving (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite full-code hidden order readout on original real sorts]
\label{lem:typed-finite-ordered-real-readout}
For each original named real sort $a$, choose a finite list
$\mathcal T_a$ representing all applicable unary term functions
in the terminal typed hidden signature, including the identity
real-root term, as supplied by
\cref{lem:typed-unary-closure-bound,lem:typed-coordinate-normal-form}.
For every ordered pair of real sorts $a,b$ and terms
$\tau\in\mathcal T_a$, $\sigma\in\mathcal T_b$, add the binary
comparison predicate
\[
 C_{\tau,\sigma}(x,y)
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \tau(x)<\sigma(y).
\tag{TOR}\label{eq:typed-ordered-real-readout}
\]
The tuples $x=y$ are included: terms with different hidden
values may give a true diagonal comparison, so these added
binary predicates need not be irreflexive.

The resulting finite relational expansion satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every finite original real $A$, its point-generated
hidden closure $\widehat A$ is determined up to unique
isomorphism over $A$ by the original atomic unary, pair and
equality types. These determine all hidden term equalities,
unary-function values and complete named semantic-cylinder
relations.
\item A linear order on $\widehat A$ is determined exactly by
its comparison predicates. More precisely, put
\[
 T_A=\{(x,\tau):x\in A,\ \tau\in\mathcal T_{\operatorname{sort}(x)}\},
 \qquad Q_A=T_A/{\equiv_A},
\]
where $(x,\tau)\equiv_A(y,\sigma)$ means equality of their
hidden term values, determined by the old atomic types.
A comparison list is compatible precisely when
\[
 [(x,\tau)]<[(y,\sigma)]
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_{\tau,\sigma}(x,y)
\]
defines a strict linear order on $Q_A$, including the tuples
$x=y$. Every embedding of real readout expansions
$f:A^*\to B^*$ extends uniquely to an ordered hidden embedding
$\widehat f:\widehat A\to\widehat B$, and conversely every
such hidden embedding restricts to a real readout embedding.
\item The expansion is precompact and reasonable: there are
finitely many expansions of each finite $A$, and along every
old embedding $A\hookrightarrow B$ every order on $\widehat A$
extends to an order on $\widehat B$.
\item If the hereditary ordered hidden envelope is Ramsey,
then the finite real readout class is Ramsey.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first verify the source-specific input to the general
full-code lemma. All hidden coordinates, unary maps and
semantic-cylinder relations are defined on the actual
canonical quotient sorts and are $\Aut(\M)$-invariant.
For fixed terms $\tau,\sigma$, both
\(\tau(x)=\sigma(y)\) and the truth of any named hidden
binary relation on their values are therefore invariant
properties of the ordered real pair $(x,y)$. Homogeneity of
the complete binary old source implies that its fine atomic
pair type, or its equality and unary type when $x=y$,
determines exactly one such orbit. Thus all these codes are
determined by the old types, including equalities between
presentations coming from different roots. Hidden unary
predicates on $\tau(x)$ are determined in the same way by
the unary type of $x$.

The named unary functions are total on their prescribed
source sorts; hence definedness of every applicable term is
fixed by those sorts. Composition with a named function is
represented by another normal-form term in the same finite
list. Function graph codes consequently reduce to the term
equalities just considered. We have verified that \emph{all}
non-order codes required by
\cref{lem:foundational-full-code-embeddings} are determined
by the original unary, pair and equality types, not just
selected quotient incidences.

Apply that lemma with length-one visible root tuples consisting
of original real vertices, first without order and then with
all the comparisons in \eqref{eq:typed-ordered-real-readout}.
The old-definable non-order codes can be omitted without
changing any embedding set. Its reconstruction identifies
$Q_A$ canonically with $\widehat A$ and gives the unique extension
\[
                      \widehat f(\tau(x))=\tau(f(x)).
\]
By \cref{lem:typed-real-root-closure-cofinal}(i), the real
portion of $\widehat A$ is exactly $A$, so restricting hidden
embeddings returns precisely the intended old real domain.
This proves (i) and the embedding assertion of (ii). Since
every hidden vertex has a term presentation, every pair of
hidden vertices is represented in (TOR). The compatible lists
are therefore exactly the strict linear orders on $Q_A$,
proving the remaining assertion of (ii).

Diagonal comparisons cannot in general be discarded. In the
two-original-sort common-block source, one old real point $x$
can generate three distinct hidden values
$x,F_{E_A}(x),\pi(F_{E_A}(x))$ in three different named sorts.
Their six linear orders give the same readout without the
comparisons $C_{\tau,\sigma}(x,x)$, and these comparisons
distinguish all six. The following example shows the issue
already with one original sort and one MI coordinate.

For (iii), $|\widehat A|\le m_{\mathrm{typ}}|A|$, so only
finitely many orders are possible. The unexpanded hidden
embedding induced by an old embedding $A\hookrightarrow B$
exists by (i); extend the order on its image to the finite
$\widehat B$ and read back its comparisons.

For (iv), apply \cref{thm:ramsey-tuple-readout} to the ordered
hidden envelope, again using length-one real roots. Their
quantifier-free compatibility conditions are just the named
real sort predicates. For an arbitrary finite hidden host $H$,
$N(H)$ is its original real-sort portion equipped with the full
term code. Its generated support is an induced unary-closed
substructure of $H$, so its code belongs to our readout class
even if $H$ has orphan quotient vertices outside that support.
Conversely every finite real readout occurs in its own ordered
point-generated closure. The classes therefore agree after
omitting the old-definable non-order codes established above,
and the generic theorem gives the required Ramsey arrows.
No cofinal enlargement of $H$ and no expansion-property
hypothesis are needed for this transfer.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Diagonal term-order comparisons are already necessary with one MI]
\label{ex:typed-diagonal-term-order-necessary}
Let $M$ be the countable
homogeneous one-sort
structure with ONE
nontrivial equivalence E,
infinitely many infinite
E-classes, and the COMPLETE
symmetric binary old
diversity colours
$p$ (distinct same-class
pairs) and $q$
(different-class pairs).
This original source has
SAP, triangle-defined age
and semantic homomorphic
CCO, with FULL original
NR2.

Its actual MI-only hidden
signature has one original
REAL sort M and the
proper quotient sort
$Q=M/E$, with the
named unary projection
$F_E:M\to Q$. For the
SINGLE original real
point $A=\{x\}$,
the point-generated
hidden closure contains
the TWO DISTINCT
hidden vertices
\[
                       x,\qquad F_E(x).
\]
There are two possible
strict linear orders
on this hidden closure:
$x<F_E(x)$ and
$F_E(x)<x$. They give
the SAME old real
one-point diagram and
there are NO pairs of
DISTINCT original real
vertices on which an
off-diagonal term-order
readout could distinguish
the two orders. But
\[
   C_{\operatorname{id},F_E}(x,x)
       \Longleftrightarrow x<F_E(x)
\]
distinguishes them
immediately. Therefore
one cannot delete
diagonal comparisons
$x=y$ from
\eqref{eq:typed-ordered-real-readout},
even with ONE original
real sort, ONE proper
MI quotient and NO
mixed original-sort
transports.
\end{example}

\begin{lemma}[Actual terminal hidden orbit completeness gives irreducibility]
\label{lem:typed-terminal-hidden-irreducible}
In the ACTUAL finite
terminal MI-only hidden
signature, take ALL old
original real sorts,
proper represented
MI quotient sorts,
named unary projections,
upward maps and quotient
transitions, all named
old fine atomic
real-pair predicates,
and the FULL named
semantic-cylinder
binary orbital partition
on every ordered
sort-pair containing
at least one proper
quotient sort, as in
\cref{lem:typed-cylinder-realisation}.
Then EVERY finite induced
UNARY-CLOSED completed
hidden substructure is
IRREDUCIBLE in its
ORIGINAL hidden language,
without adjoining
a universal $E_*$ edge.

This statement concerns
ACTUAL complete hidden
orbitals and named unary
graphs, not an arbitrary
reduct retaining only
selected cylinders.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix two DISTINCT hidden
vertices $x,y$ of a
completed induced member.
If both belong to
original real sorts,
their pair has one
NAMED original fine
atomic diversity
relation, because
the original real
binary language is
COMPLETE. This includes
mixed original real sorts
with a named typed
pair orbital.

If at least one
endpoint lies in a
PROPER represented
MI quotient sort,
its old real
equivalence class
has infinitely many
representatives by
original visible SAP.
Choose old real
representatives of
the two vertices
which are DISTINCT:
the real endpoint,
if present, has its
unique representative,
while any proper
quotient endpoint
can avoid the finitely
many forbidden
real points. For
two proper endpoints
also choose distinct
representatives,
even when the hidden
sort pair is repeated.
Their original fine
atomic pair type
belongs to ONE full
old cylinder
\(\operatorname{Cyl}_{J,K}(t)\).
By the actual
\cref{lem:typed-cylinder-realisation},
this cylinder labels a
NAMED POSITIVE
binary semantic relation
on exactly that ordered
hidden pair.
The hidden vertices
are distinct, so
the possible same-sort
logical hidden diagonal
is not involved.
A pair that also
forms the graph of a
named unary projection
or transition is still
either joined by its
actual named cylinder
or, independently,
by one of the unary
term clauses (i),(ii)
of
\cref{lem:typed-unary-binary-irreducibility}.

Thus EVERY pair of
distinct hidden
vertices satisfies
the binary clause of
\cref{lem:typed-unary-binary-irreducibility}
(or additionally a
named unary clause).
Apply that lemma to
the completed finite
induced unary-closed
member. It is
IRREDUCIBLE without
a synthetic E* relation.
\end{proof}
