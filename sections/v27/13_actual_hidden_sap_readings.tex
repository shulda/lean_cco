\begin{theorem}[Typed strong hidden amalgamation]
\label{thm:typed-hidden-sap}
The transition-enriched typed hereditary hidden envelope has strong
amalgamation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Direct unary-algebraic-closure proof]
The actual terminal hidden structure $\widehat\M$, with all complete
original hidden binary orbitals named, is homogeneous by
\cref{thm:typed-joint-exact-lifting}: every finite hidden partial
isomorphism extends through one original real automorphism, preserving
all named equivariant projections and quotient transitions.
For finite $T$, its unary closure has size at most
$m_{\mathrm{typ}}|T|$ by \cref{lem:typed-unary-closure-bound}.

For every finite hidden unary-closed $A$ and every $u\notin A$,
\cref{lem:typed-hereditary-hidden-orbits} gives an infinite orbit under
the original group $\Aut(\M/A)$. Its induced action is contained in
$\Aut(\widehat\M/A)$, so the latter orbit is infinite as well.
Thus \cref{lem:unary-homogeneous-sap-criterion} applies to the
actual hidden structure and gives SAP for its entire hereditary
finite unary-closed age, including orphan-only bases.

The source-specific inputs remain the original full exact lifting,
real-base orbit theorem and typed fresh representatives, with the
last two linked by the common group-action frame criterion.
The full working manuscript retains a separate alternative construction.
\end{proof}



\begin{lemma}[Rigidity of completed typed hidden structures]
\label{lem:typed-hidden-rigidity}
Every homomorphism between completed members of the transition-enriched typed
hidden envelope is an embedding.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Unary sort predicates prevent identifications between different real or
coordinate sorts.  On two distinct real vertices the canonical pair-type
relations form a partition, so a homomorphism is injective there and preserves
and reflects their pair type.  By
\cref{lem:typed-cylinder-realisation}, on every compatible ordered pair of
represented coordinate positions with at least one proper quotient endpoint
the named semantic-cylinder relations form a partition.  This includes the
mixed real--coordinate case, with the real sort interpreted as the equality
coordinate.  A homomorphism preserves the unique cylinder relation carried by
a source pair; uniqueness of the cylinder in the target therefore reflects
that relation as well.  In particular, on one proper coordinate sort it
cannot identify two distinct vertices, since the cylinder predicates are
interpreted off the diagonal.  Sort predicates already separate vertices
belonging to different coordinate sorts.  The
finite canonical definitional completion fixed at the beginning of this
subsection makes the old tuple-type relations a partition on every compatible
real tuple up to the maximum arity, so all old higher-support relations are
reflected as well.  Finally, the domains of all projections, upward maps and
quotient transitions are fixed by their source sorts, and completed hidden
structures are induced and functionally closed.  Thus function values and all
remaining displayed relations are reflected.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An anchored fine pair type fixes every old hidden coordinate reading]
\label{lem:typed-anchored-real-clone-readout}
Let \(p\) be an actual real point of original
sort \(a\), and let \(B\) be a finite
COMPLETE old visible frame not containing
\(p\). Assume that for every old proper
hidden coordinate vertex \(q\) whose
reading must be retained, \(B\) contains
one designated actual real representative
\(a_q\) of the target quotient class.
Also include representatives of all
proper quotient coordinates \(F_J(p)\)
that must be retained from \(p\).
Suppose \(p'\ne p\) is another real point
of sort \(a\) and
\[
       \col(p',z)=\col(p,z)\qquad(z\in B).
\tag{ACR}\label{eq:typed-anchor-clone-reading}
\]
Then \(p'\) has exactly the same
represented proper \(J\)-coordinates as
\(p\) for every relevant \(J\), and
the same named unary term values after
all upward maps and quotient transitions.
Every old real--proper-coordinate
semantic cylinder with first endpoint
\(p\) and second endpoint among
the retained \(q\)'s has the same
value with first endpoint \(p'\).
All relations between proper coordinates
of \(p\) and old proper coordinates
\(q\), as well as between two
old proper coordinates, are unchanged.

The conclusion is only about hidden
RELATIONS/FUNCTIONS that are canonically
defined by original fine pair orbitals
and represented quotient classes.
Higher-arity original tuple constraints
are not covered by this binary lemma.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a relevant proper represented
\(J\), choose in the frame one
\(a_q\in[p]_J\).
By the hypothesis (ACR),
the old fine orbital of
\((p',a_q)\) equals that of
\((p,a_q)\), and hence
\(p'J a_q\). Therefore
\([p']_J=[p]_J\).
Every named upward quotient map
and transition is a fixed actual
function of the represented input
class, by
\cref{lem:typed-quotient-transport,lem:typed-coordinate-normal-form};
thus it has the same output on
the corresponding coordinates of
\(p,p'\).

Fix an old proper coordinate
\(q\in B_{b,K}\), represented in
the frame by a real point
\(a_q\in b\). The semantic
cylinder on \((p,q)\)
is determined by the old fine
atomic orbital on \((p,a_q)\)
and the quotient kernel \(K\),
with the equality-coordinate
convention for the real endpoint,
by \cref{lem:typed-cylinder-realisation}.
The same fine orbital on
\((p',a_q)\) gives the SAME
cylinder. Every proper coordinate
of \(p'\) equals the corresponding
proper coordinate of \(p\), so
all cylinders between these
coordinates and the old \(q\)'s
are identical, and cylinders
between old proper coordinates
are untouched. These
observations also cover mixed
original sorts: the relevant
fine orbital \(p a_q\) is a
named typed cross orbital,
and no same-sort commutation
is being used. This establishes
all asserted readings.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Typed representative splitting]
\label{lem:typed-real-sort-splitting}
Let \(h:C\to H\) be a homomorphism-embedding of a finite functionally closed
transition-enriched hidden structure into a full hidden expansion of some
finite visible \(A\in\mathcal C\).  After passing to a finite visible
extension \(A'\in\mathcal C\), one may choose pairwise distinct real images
for all old real vertices of \(C\) and pairwise distinct designated real
representatives for all old proper coordinate vertices.  All specified old
relations, coordinate functions, quotient transitions and semantic-cylinder
relations are preserved.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work inside the homogeneous limit.
BEFORE splitting any old real collisions,
fix one finite COMPLETE visible anchor frame.
For EVERY old proper coordinate vertex
\(q\in C\), including all unary term
values of old real roots and all old
orphan quotient vertices, choose an
actual real anchor \(a_q\) in the
target quotient class \(h(q)\).
Likewise fix an anchor in every
represented proper quotient class
of every old real image, if not
already represented by one of the
\(a_q\). Choose ALL anchors pairwise distinct
and away from the finite old real
image, even when two old proper
coordinate vertices have the same
target quotient class.
This is possible: in a visible
SAP source each nonzero invariant
equivalence class is infinite,
by strong duplication of a
nonidentity same-class pair over
one pinned point. There are
only finitely many old quotient
vertices and named coordinate
sorts, so the frame is finite.
Take its complete induced old
visible structure together with
all current old real images.

If two distinct old real source
vertices have the same image \(p\),
they cannot lie together in one
displayed irreducible old factor,
because \(h\) is an embedding on
irreducibles. Strongly amalgamate
two copies of the current COMPLETE
visible structure over its
complement of \(p\), keeping
the entire finite anchor frame
and all OTHER DISTINCT current
real image points in the base.
Several unprocessed old source
real positions may still share
the point \(p\); they are not
erroneously counted as distinct
base elements. Assign \(p'\)
to one of those positions
and retain \(p\) for the others.
The second copy \(p'\ne p\)
has exactly the same old fine
atomic relation to every fixed
frame anchor as \(p\). In
particular \(p'\) belongs to
all the SAME named proper
quotient classes as \(p\),
including its designated
\(F_J\)-values and their
transition/upward images.
By
\cref{lem:typed-anchored-real-clone-readout},
the complete frame equalities of
old fine pair colours imply
preservation of EVERY relevant
mixed real--quotient cylinder
and quotient--quotient cylinder,
as well as all upward and
transition unary-term readings.
No independent choice of
unforced new quotient values
is made during this cloning. Any originally
displayed old REAL fine atomic
pair whose endpoint is \(p\)
has its other endpoint distinct
from \(p\), hence fixed in the
complete base and preserved.
There is no specified fine old
relation between two source
real vertices that \(h\) had
collapsed to \(p\), since that
two-point source would be
irreducible and \(h\) an
embedding on it.

Repeat with all previous clones
in the fixed complete base.
This separates the real source
vertices while preserving their
literal old unary term values.
In the support-normal
finite-arity setting the
corresponding SAP amalgam must
additionally preserve the
distinguished complete old
higher-support factors;
that is its SEPARATE assumed
interface, not a consequence
of the binary anchor argument.

For every old proper coordinate vertex
\(q\) use its previously fixed
pairwise-distinct anchor \(a_q\)
as a designated real representative
of its ACTUAL target quotient class.
Alternatively,
\cref{lem:typed-fresh-representatives}
allows a fresh representative of that class
while retaining the class itself and its
named forced term values. Existing hidden
vertices remain fixed; unforced term values
of the new representative may change.
Upward maps and quotient
transitions constrain actual
quotient \emph{classes}, so these
choices preserve every displayed
functional identity.  A displayed semantic-cylinder relation is bisaturated by the corresponding
endpoint equivalences and is therefore preserved by arbitrary representatives
of the displayed classes.  Exact-domain labels are not hidden-language data;
when they occur in guarded templates their defining anchors and private paths
are retained separately and are reinstalled after this splitting step.  There
is no equality-coordinate orphan to treat: by
\cref{lem:no-real-algebraic-transition} every transition target is a proper
coordinate sort and the real sorts are reached only by the original real
vertices.  Taking a finite induced visible substructure containing all chosen
points gives the required \(A'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Irreducible source supports and complete-pattern defects]
\label{lem:typed-joined-source-support}
Let \(C\) be a finite functionally closed PARTIAL
hidden structure, and let \(D=\cl_C(B)\)
for at most \(k\) OLD hidden source vertices.
Assume \( |\cl_C\{b\}|\le m_{\mathrm{typ}}\) for each chosen generator
\(b\in B\), as holds when \(C\) is unary-admissible.
Suppose \(D\), with its actual induced
PARTIAL diagram, is irreducible in the
hidden language (including its unary functions).
If \(C\) has a hidden
homomorphism-embedding completion,
then the map on \(D\) is an EMBEDDING,
so \(D\) is isomorphic to a completed
induced hidden substructure. In particular,
if the full induced diagram of \(D\) is
missing a relation, inequality, or
functional identity that every actual
completed realisation of its displayed
joining datum has, then \(C\) has NO
homomorphism-embedding completion.
The defect is supported on at most
\(k m_{\mathrm{typ}}\) old vertices.

It is NOT enough that a displayed relation
joins some unary TERM VALUES from
\(\cl_C(b)\) and \(\cl_C(c)\):
the joint closure \(\cl_C\{b,c\}\)
can remain reducible. Such an
unjoined pair must NOT be subjected
to the complete two-root test merely
because a relation holds in a common
quotient-coordinate subdiagram.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of a homomorphism-embedding,
its restriction to every source irreducible
substructure is an embedding.
Thus its restriction to \(D\) is an
isomorphism onto a FUNCTIONALLY CLOSED
induced substructure of a completed
hidden member. The completed hidden class
is hereditary, so the induced diagram
on \(D\) must already be one of the
completed diagrams. Any missing mandatory
named datum prevents this. Since
\(\cl_C(B)\) is the union of the
one-generated closures of the at
most \(k\) old generators, its size
is at most \(k m_{\mathrm{typ}}\).
No conclusion about completion of
a reducible \(D\) follows from this
argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A reducible two-root control]
\label{rem:typed-term-join-not-irreducible}
In the one-sort age of an equivalence
with infinitely many infinite classes,
let \(x,y\) be real vertices and let
\(u=F_E(x)\), \(v=F_E(y)\) be their
proper quotient coordinates. Take
the functionally closed partial
hidden diagram induced by the
two complete one-generated diagrams
of \(x,y\), together with the
displayed OFF-DIAGONAL quotient-cylinder
relation on \(u,v\), but with NO
original fine atomic relation on
\(x,y\) (nor arbitrary cross-cylinder
relations on \(x,v\), \(y,u\)).
This diagram is completable,
choosing \(x,y\) in distinct
\(E\)-classes. It is the free
amalgam of its restrictions
to \(\{x,u,v\}\) and
\(\{y,u,v\}\) over \(\{u,v\}\).
A relation between \(F_E(x)\) and
\(F_E(y)\) thus DOES NOT make
the full two-real-root closure
irreducible. In a guarded lift
the quotient-cylinder datum is
attached to its ACTUAL old source
endpoints \(u,v\), while the
individual projection equations
are attached to \(x,u\) and \(y,v\).
It is invalid to fill in the
previously unspecified fine
\(x,y\) relation before completion.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Exactly when a hidden term-pair orbital has no diversity preimage]
\label{lem:typed-term-orbit-diagonal-dichotomy}
Let \(a,b\) be original
transitive named real sorts
of one homogeneous complete
typed binary SAP structure,
and let
\(\tau:a\to Q\),
\(\sigma:b\to R\)
be surjective equivariant
unary-term maps with
original real kernels
\(J=K_\tau\), \(K=K_\sigma\).
For every NONEMPTY
\(\Aut(\M)\)-orbit
\(\Theta\subseteq Q\times R\)
exactly one of the following
holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Its pullback contains
an ordered pair of
DISTINCT original real
vertices. Then its
DIVERSITY preimage is
the nonempty full
representative saturation
\(\operatorname{Cyl}_{J,K}(t)\)
of any one old atomic
diversity pair type
\(t\) in that pullback.
\item Its pullback contains
NO distinct original
real pair. Necessarily
\(a=b\), \(J=K=\Id_a\),
both maps are injective
equivariant identifications
of their real source with
their respective output
sorts, and \(\Theta\)
is the graph pairing
\(\tau(x)\) with
\(\sigma(x)\) for the
SAME original real point
\(x\in a\).
\end{enumerate}
In case (ii), a requirement
on two distinct, irreducibly
joined OLD source vertices
to realise \(\Theta\)
is a bounded real-identity
defect, not a nonempty
STRONG diversity constraint.
For a single OLD source
real root the corresponding
term identity is tested
in its unary closure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:foundational-orbital-pullback} to the actual
$G=\Aut(\M)$ action on the two original real sorts. The named terms
are equivariant surjections, and their actual kernels are $J,K$.
Old complete binary homogeneity identifies each diversity pair orbit
with its fine atomic colour. Hence the general saturation formula
identifies the nonempty diversity preimage with
$\operatorname{Cyl}_{J,K}(t)$ for any old diversity witness $t$.
Its empty-diversity alternative gives exactly (ii), including the
same-original-sort and true identity-kernel requirements. This part
of the argument needs no SAP and does not depend on the later
positive STRONG representation.

In case (ii), any real preimage of a pair in $\Theta$ has equal
endpoints. An induced embedding of an irreducibly joined OLD two-root
support cannot identify its distinct real roots, so such a requirement
is a bounded real-identity defect. For two terms on the same OLD root,
the comparison is instead checked in its one-generated unary closure;
no STRONG identity edge is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[CCO-free positive pullback for complete quotient-pair orbitals]
\label{lem:typed-complete-orbital-positive-NR2-SAP}
Let $M$ be any countable HOMOGENEOUS finite complete
binary structure with named individually transitive
original real sorts, symmetric same-sort old atoms,
and visible SAP. Assume that on EACH original
real sort there is NO strict finite-relative-index
interval in the FULL lattice of original
invariant equivalences.  Semantic CCO, exact-ball
Helly, and distributivity of the invariant
equivalence lattice are NOT assumed.

For ANY surjective $\Aut(M)$-equivariant maps
$\tau:M_a\to Q$, $\sigma:M_b\to R$
to transitive quotient $G$-sets,
each complete $G$-orbital $\Theta\subseteq Q\times R$
has exactly one of the following OLD diversity
pullbacks:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item ONE nonempty ORIGINAL strong-positive typed
word profile, equal to the full
$\ker\tau,\ker\sigma$-saturated old cylinder
of ANY of its fine atomic diversity witnesses;
\item the EMPTY diversity spectrum, possible
only if $a=b$, both kernels are TRUE real
equality, and $\Theta$ is the same-real-point
graph of $\tau,\sigma$.
\end{enumerate}
The nonempty spectra of all such orbitals partition
the ORIGINAL old diversity atomic palette.
In particular, the terminal named-term readout below obtains
positivity from old SAP plus full-lattice terminality,
without CCO. This does NOT imply hidden SAP, exact
quotient structure inheritance or any Ramsey arrow.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $J=\ker\tau$ and $K=\ker\sigma$. Apply
\cref{lem:foundational-orbital-pullback} to the actual
$G=\Aut(M)$ action. By old complete binary homogeneity a diversity
root-pair orbit is one fine atomic colour $t_0$. Thus every nonempty
diversity pullback is the off-diagonal part of the actual RAW relation
$J;\{t_0\};K$. The same general lemma supplies the unique possible
empty-diversity exception and the partition of original fine orbitals.
It remains to prove positive STRONG reachability of each nonempty part.

FULL original NR2 excludes all intervals of relative index two.
Therefore \cref{lem:typed-all-invariant-equivalences-raw-word}
gives one actual RAW atomic word for each nonidentity original kernel
$J$ or $K$. Such a word is positive, since the empty word represents
only true real identity. For an identity kernel use the empty word.
Concatenating these two endpoint words with the middle diversity atom
$t_0$ gives one positive typed atomic word whose actual RAW relation
is $J;\{t_0\};K$.

Visible SAP permits the pinned-endpoint injectivisation of
\cref{lem:typed-injectivisation}: for each distinct original endpoint
pair, separate the occurrences of a witnessing word walk while fixing
both endpoints. Consequently the off-diagonal part of this RAW word
is exactly its STRONG word profile. No associativity of STRONG
products is needed for this whole-word argument.
The same SAP conversion on the exterior equivalence factors is
\cref{lem:typed-raw-strong-saturation}; in the established notation it gives
\[
 (J;\{t_0\};K)^\circ
       =J^\circ\starprod\{t_0\}\starprod K^\circ,
\]
with each true-identity exterior factor omitted. This is the full
saturated cylinder in (i), and it is nonempty because $t_0$ was chosen
from a distinct-real witness. The group-action partition and exception
already proved give all remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact diversity partition by complete hidden term-pair orbitals]
