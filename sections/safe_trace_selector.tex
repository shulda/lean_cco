
\section{Safe traces and the selector interface}
\label{sec:safe-traces}

The original selector, Theorem~\ref{thm:typed-HS}, selects
an atom from an \emph{original} strong exact menu.
A nonempty trace $D\cap S$ of an exact menu by a safe palette
$S$ need not be a member of that original family.  We state
explicitly the additional finite-algebra argument needed by
rooted clique-faithful completion.  These claims are in the
\emph{pure owner-language action} setting: $\Gamma$ permutes
owner names but fixes every literal fine atomic colour.
The endpoint thin-$G$ switching of Section~\ref{sec:full-rows}
is handled separately by the rectangular moving-readings laws.

\begin{lemma}[One-edge recolouring from strong $S$-amalgamation]
\label{lem:one-edge-safe-recolour}
Let $C$ be any finite \emph{complete} typed structure in a
hereditary class with strong $S$-amalgamation of all finite
typed spans.  For any two distinct vertices $x,y\in C$,
there exists a complete typed structure $C'$ on the
\emph{same vertex set and with the same owner labels},
which agrees with $C$ on every pair other than $xy$
and has $\col_{C'}(x,y)\in S_{\operatorname{own}(x),\operatorname{own}(y)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Strongly $S$-amalgamate the two complete factors
$C\setminus\{x\}$ and $C\setminus\{y\}$ over their complete
common part $C\setminus\{x,y\}$.  Their off-base parts are
precisely the singleton vertices $y$ and $x$, respectively;
strongness keeps them distinct and makes their new cross edge
safe.  Restrict the amalgam to the two factor images and identify
these images with the original vertex set.  All other pairs and
all owner predicates were already present in one factor and so
are unchanged.  The case of a two-point $C$ has empty base
and is included.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Safe traces and arbitrary-multiplier product identity]
\label{lem:safe-trace-product}
Let $U$ be the primitive, typed, SAP and CCO transversal,
with a nonempty pure-$\Gamma$-covariant palette
$S_{st}\subseteq L_{st}$ which \emph{strongly}
$S$-amalgamates every finite typed span.
For every nonempty original strong exact menu $D\in\mathcal H_{st}$,
the trace $D\cap S_{st}$ is nonempty unless $D$ is
an atomic singleton outside $S_{st}$.  Whenever
$D\cap S_{st}\ne\varnothing$, for every nonempty
\emph{arbitrary atomic subset} $E\subseteq L_{tu}$, not
necessarily original exact, one has
\[
          (D\cap S_{st})*E=D*E,
\]
and likewise on the left with matching types.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $D$ is an atomic singleton $\{d\}$, its trace is
nonempty precisely when $d\in S$, and in this case the
product identity is tautological.  Otherwise any finite
meet presentation of $D$ uses only profiles of positive
paths of length at least two (or the empty family presenting
full top), since a length-one profile would make $D$
an atomic singleton.

Fix a realised root edge $xy=d\in D$.  For each profile in
a chosen finite meet presentation of $D$, choose an injective
path witness whose endpoints have colour $d$.  Amalgamate
these complete finite path structures over their common
\emph{complete} root edge $xy$ by AP.  Paths may overlap
outside the roots, but each individual path remains injective.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:one-edge-safe-recolour} to this
complete structure: the root edge changes to some
$d'\in S$ while every path witness remains valid.  Thus
$d'\in D\cap S$.  The empty presentation of the top
menu is handled by choosing any legal root edge first.

For the product identity, choose any $c\in D*E$, realised
by a triangle $xy=d\in D$, $yz=e\in E$, $xz=c$.
Amalgamate all chosen path witnesses for $D$ with this
triangle over its complete root edge $xy$.  Recolour only
that edge by Lemma~\ref{lem:one-edge-safe-recolour},
fixing $xz=c$, $yz=e$ and every path edge.  The new
root colour $d'\in S$ still belongs to $D$, so the
same triangle proves $c\in(D\cap S)*E$.
The reverse inclusion is monotonicity.  Crucially the
argument never presents $E$ by paths: it works for an
arbitrary nonempty atomic subset.  Converse yields the
left identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit skeleton calculus for augmented safe traces]
\label{lem:augmented-safe-skeleton-calculus}
Work in the finite original
typed strong exact family
$\mathcal H$ of
\cref{lem:safe-trace-product}.
For each actual typed
hom-palette $L_{st}$,
put $S^0_{st}=L_{st}$
(the genuine FULL
DIVERSITY palette)
and $S^1_{st}=S_{st}$.
Write any nonempty
augmented typed menu
$X\subseteq L_{st}$ in
the form
\[
        X=D_X\cap S^\epsilon_{st},
 \qquad
 \epsilon\in\{0,1\},
\]
where $D_X$ belongs to the
ORIGINAL exact family and
the displayed menu is
nonempty; for $\epsilon=0$,
this means literally $X=D_X$.
This representation need
not be unique. For any
compatible augmented
$X,Y$ with chosen original
skeletons $D_X,D_Y$,
\[
                       X*Y=D_X*D_Y
                  \in\mathcal H.
\tag{AS1}\label{eq:augmented-skeleton-product}
\]
For any NONEMPTY finite
intersection of parallel
augmented menus,
\[
  \bigcap_{i=1}^m X_i
   =\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^m D_{X_i}\right)
             \cap S^{\max_i\epsilon_i},
\tag{AS2}\label{eq:augmented-skeleton-meet}
\]
which is again one of
the displayed nonempty
augmented menus. In
particular for all
compatible augmented
$X,Y,Z$ with
$Y\cap Z\ne\varnothing$,
both right- and left-
multiplication preserve
this nonempty meet:
\[
       X*(Y\cap Z)
          =(X*Y)\cap(X*Z).
\tag{AS3}\label{eq:augmented-skeleton-distrib}
\]
No identity or FALSE
distributivity over EMPTY
exact intersections is
being introduced by
$S^0$; it is only a
NOTATIONAL absent-trace
flag. Converse transports
these formulas to reversed
sorts because $S$ is
converse-compatible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If neither factor is
traced, (AS1) is
tautological. If exactly
one is traced, the
arbitrary-nonempty-multiplier
identity of
\cref{lem:safe-trace-product}
removes that safe trace
without changing the
strong product. If
BOTH are traced, first
remove the trace in X
with multiplier Y, which
is an arbitrary NONEMPTY
atomic subset, and then
remove the trace in Y
with multiplier $D_X$.
Both applications are
type-compatible and
give $X*Y=D_X*D_Y$.

Formula (AS2) is an
ordinary SET intersection
identity: all $S$ factors
are the SAME fixed safe
palette of the parallel
typed hom-set, so either
one safe trace survives
or none occurs. The
underlying
$\bigcap_iD_{X_i}$
is a nonempty ORIGINAL
exact meet because
$\bigcap_iX_i\ne
\varnothing$; when
a trace is present,
the resulting safe
intersection is
nonempty by assumption.
This establishes closure
under nonempty meets
WITHOUT asserting the
same for empty meets.

For (AS3), choose
skeletons $D_X,D_Y,D_Z$
and let
$D_{YZ}=D_Y\cap D_Z$,
which is nonempty and
ORIGINAL exact by (AS2).
Apply (AS1) to the
nonempty augmented meet
$Y\cap Z$:
\[
 X*(Y\cap Z)
      =D_X*(D_Y\cap D_Z).
\]
Original full strong
exact meet-product
distributivity yields
\[
 D_X*(D_Y\cap D_Z)
    =(D_X*D_Y)
          \cap(D_X*D_Z).
\]
Each product on the
right equals its
corresponding augmented
product by (AS1).
Converse gives the
other multiplication
direction, with the
ACTUAL original typed
endpoint labels.
No uniqueness of the
chosen skeleton
representation was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Augmented safe-domain algebra]
\label{lem:augmented-safe-algebra}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the nonempty augmented
families
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_{st}=\mathcal H_{st}\cup
 \{D\cap S_{st}:D\in\mathcal H_{st},\ D\cap S_{st}\ne\varnothing\}
\]
are finite, closed under nonempty meets, strong products and
converse, satisfy nonempty Helly and meet-product
distributivity, and are pure-$\Gamma$-covariant.
They admit a joint hereditary singleton selector which,
when precomposed with the safe trace whenever that trace
is nonempty, chooses from $S_{st}$ for every such menu.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The precise augmented-product,
nonempty-meet and typed
meet-distributivity identities
are established independently
in
\cref{lem:augmented-safe-skeleton-calculus}.
In particular every product
of two augmented menus is
the ORIGINAL exact product
of any two chosen original
skeletons; if both factors
carry safe traces, the first
trace-removal uses an
ARBITRARY atomic-subset
multiplier, not a
nonjustified exactness
assumption on the second
trace. Empty exact
intersections remain EMPTY
and are never silently
adjoined to the family.

For Helly, take a pairwise-intersecting finite list
of augmented menus, and replace every trace by its underlying
original exact menu.  The resulting list is pairwise
intersecting, so original exact 2-Helly gives a common atom
$d$.  If no listed menu is a safe trace, this already
settles the claim.  If one of the underlying original
menus is an atomic singleton $\{e\}$, then the atom $e=d$
is common to every augmented menu; because at least one
listed menu is a safe trace, this atom already lies in $S$.
Otherwise every original menu in the list has a finite
meet presentation by paths of length at least two, with
full top represented by the empty list.  Amalgamate all
these path witnesses simultaneously over the complete
root edge $xy=d$.  A single application of the
one-edge recolouring lemma changes $d$ to some
$d'\in S$ while leaving all path edges untouched.
Therefore $d'$ belongs to every underlying exact menu
and to every listed safe trace.  This proves augmented
2-Helly, including repeated sorts and empty presentation
lists, without any equality-edge convention.

For completeness we give the additional *pure-$\Gamma$*
descent argument, rather than invoking the noncovariant selector
theorem and implicitly assuming covariance.    The common primitive-loop saturation theorem
\cref{lem:locality-positive} applies to EVERY nonempty subset
$W\subseteq P_{tt}$, even when $W$ is an augmented safe trace
rather than an original exact or word-represented menu.  Thus
$W^{*n}=P_{tt}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.  Its proof
uses only symmetry, SAP and primitivity.  Full absorption of
compatible nonempty augmented factors follows directly from
\cref{lem:locality-absorb}; neither claim needs an adjoined
identity atom.

For an augmented seed $C$, let $\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)$ be its
finite tagged family generated by pure owner $\Gamma$ actions,
converse and all compatible positive left/right multiplications
by the $\widehat{\mathcal H}$ menus.  A path in this generation
graph has a normal form $L*(\gamma C)^\varepsilon*R$, with
outside factors possibly absent but otherwise nonempty
\emph{positive} menus.  Multiplying, taking converse and applying
a language relabelling never erases a positive factor: there is
no equality operation introduced into the strong diversity
algebra.  A positive closed return through a proper menu $D$
would, after an even multiple of the finite order of $\gamma$,
induce $D=P*D*Q$ with at least one positive loop multiplier.
Iterating and using the preceding full-power saturation makes
the right side equal to the full tagged palette, a contradiction.
In particular if parallel augmented menus generate the same
$\Gamma$-closed family, there are return paths between them,
none involving multiplication.  Only relabelling and converse
remain, which preserve the underlying literal symmetric raw
colour names, hence the parallel menus are equal.

Now run the descending-filter construction with the
$\Gamma$-closed generated families $\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)$,
not the noncovariant original filters. The gate proofs
\cref{lem:typed-bilateral,lem:typed-all-PC,lem:typed-gate-image}
apply to $\widehat{\mathcal H}$ ON THEIR ACTUAL
ALGEBRAIC HYPOTHESES, rather than by silently
declaring every safe trace an original exact menu:
the augmented finite menus have all atomic singletons,
nonempty meet closure and $2$-Helly (proved above);
the skeleton calculus
\cref{lem:augmented-safe-skeleton-calculus}
supplies strong products, converse and
NONEMPTY meet-product distributivity.
The typed Peirce rotations and the strong
SAP duplication inclusions are unchanged
because these concern actual fine-colour
triangles, not membership in a skeleton.
Thus the bilateral gate and all three
PC gate coordinates are legitimate in
the AUGMENTED family.

Choose a seed $C$ having a proper
nonempty trace on some current menu and
minimising $|\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)|$.
If relative visibility failed, there
would be PARALLEL augmented
$A,B\in\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)$ active
on the SAME current menu but with
$A\cap B=\varnothing$.
Each of $A$ and $B$ is then itself
a viable seed with a proper nonempty
current trace, while
$\mathfrak F_\Gamma(A)$ and
$\mathfrak F_\Gamma(B)$ are
SUBFAMILIES of
$\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)$.
If both had the same cardinality,
their generated families would be
equal to $\mathfrak F_\Gamma(C)$;
parallel no-positive-return separation
would give $A=B$, a contradiction.
Hence one seed has a STRICTLY
smaller generated family, contradicting
minimality. This proves relative
visibility with ALL quantifiers in
the augmented setting, including
repeated sort labels and nonfree
$\Gamma$-stabilisers.

Its gate is nonempty by augmented Helly,
is PC-preserving in all three positions
by the transferred bilateral/Peirce
proof, and respects converse and
survivor heredity. It is
\emph{$\Gamma$-covariant}, because the
ENTIRE active filter family was closed
under $\Gamma$ before the gate was taken;
one never chooses separately inside
different owner orbits.
Each proper gate strictly decreases the finite family of tagged
menus while keeping all atomic singleton menus.  Iteration
terminates at a joint pure-$\Gamma$-covariant hereditary
singleton selector on $\widehat{\mathcal H}$.  This is the
specific augmented instance of the no-positive-return and
gate mechanism in Theorem~\ref{thm:covariant-HS}; it does
not follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:typed-HS} alone.
Finally define the conservative safe
pre-gate ON THE ENTIRE AUGMENTED FAMILY,
not only on its original exact skeleton:
\[
 T(D)=\begin{cases}
    D\cap S_{st},&D\cap S_{st}\ne\varnothing,\\
    D,&D\cap S_{st}=\varnothing
      \end{cases}
      \qquad(D\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{st}).
\]
It is well-defined in the augmented family,
and is idempotent. For ANY compatible
nonempty AUGMENTED menus $D,E$ one has
\[
                         T(D)*T(E)=D*E.
\]
Indeed a factor which changes under $T$
must be an ORIGINAL exact menu: an
already traced augmented menu lies wholly
in $S$. Remove its safe trace using
\cref{lem:safe-trace-product}, whose
other multiplier is permitted to be
ANY nonempty atomic subset, including an
augmented trace. If BOTH factors change,
remove the two traces successively,
using the opposite-sided version for
the second one. If neither changes the
identity is tautological. Thus the
displayed product identity holds on
ALL of $\widehat{\mathcal H}$, not only
on $\mathcal H$.

Consequently for EVERY augmented
typed PC triple, each of the three
input menus shrinks under $T$ while
the products on the right of EVERY
rotated PC inclusion remain literally
unchanged. Hence $T$ preserves all
three inclusions. It commutes with
typed converse because $S$ is
converse-compatible, and with the
pure owner $\Gamma$-action because
$S$ is $\Gamma$-covariant.
Finally, if nonempty augmented
$E\subseteq D$ meets $T(D)$,
then $T(E)=E\cap T(D)$:
if $D\cap S\ne\varnothing$, the
hypothesis implies $E\cap S\ne
\varnothing$, so both traces are
nonempty and the equality is direct;
if $D\cap S=\varnothing$, also
$E\cap S=\varnothing$ and both are
unchanged. This proves survivor
heredity on the whole augmented
domain, including repeated sorts,
nonfree owner stabilisers and
identical source menu variables.

Let $h_0$ be the singleton selector obtained by the descending gates.
The promised safe selector is explicitly
\[
                         h_S(D)=h_0(T(D)).
\]
Both factors preserve converse, pure-$\Gamma$ covariance and all three
PC inclusions. Survivor heredity also composes: a surviving output
belongs to $T(D)$, so first apply heredity of $T$ and then of $h_0$.
Finally $h_S(D)\in T(D)\subseteq D$, and whenever $D$ meets $S$,
its value lies in $D\cap S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite orbit bound for the augmented pure-owner gate iteration]
\label{cor:augmented-gamma-orbit-gate-bound}
Under the hypotheses of
\cref{lem:augmented-safe-algebra},
put
\[
 \mathscr M_{\ge2}
  =\{(s,t,D):D\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{st},
                         \ |D|\ge2\},
 \qquad
 N_\Gamma=|\Gamma\backslash\mathscr M_{\ge2}|.
\]
After the conservative pre-gate $T$,
at most $N_\Gamma$ PROPER
$\Gamma$-closed descending filter
gates are needed to obtain one joint
pure-$\Gamma$-covariant
hereditary singleton selector.
In particular
\[
 N_\Gamma\le
 \sum_{s,t}\bigl(
       |\widehat{\mathcal H}_{st}|
                   -|L_{st}|\bigr)
 \le\sum_{s,t}
              (2|\mathcal H_{st}|-|L_{st}|).
\]
No free action on the owner sorts
or on the tagged menus is required.
In fact an owner-tag stabiliser acts
trivially on each literal original
symmetric fine atom name.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At each stage the current family
$\mathcal J$ of NONEMPTY augmented
menus is $\Gamma$-invariant, is
downward closed relative to the
original augmented family, and
contains every tagged atomic
singleton. The generated filter
is first CLOSED under the ENTIRE
$\Gamma$ action. Its gate
$\mathcal G$ is consequently
$\Gamma$-covariant. The ORIGINAL
gate-image proof
\cref{lem:typed-gate-image}
applies because, as explicitly
checked in the preceding augmented
lemma, all its nonempty-meet,
Helly, distributivity, Peirce
and bilateral-support premises
hold in $\widehat{\mathcal H}$.
In particular survivor heredity
gives
$\mathcal G(\mathcal G(D))=\mathcal G(D)$:
apply it with $E=\mathcal G(D)$,
an AUGMENTED menu contained in D.
Thus the image family
$\mathcal G(\mathcal J)$ is EXACTLY
the family of fixed menus, is again
downward and $\Gamma$-invariant,
and contains all atomic singletons.

If $\mathcal J$ still contains a
nonsingleton $D$, one atomic
singleton $C\subsetneq D$ has a
proper nonempty current trace,
so the minimal generated-filter
procedure of
\cref{lem:typed-pure-action-gate-engine}
produces a gate with
$\mathcal G(D')\subsetneq D'$
for SOME nonsingleton $D'$
(possibly different from $D$).
Since $\mathcal G$ is
IDEMPOTENT, $D'$ is NOT in its
image. For every $\gamma\in\Gamma$,
\[
 \mathcal G(\gamma D')
       =\gamma\mathcal G(D')
                         \subsetneq\gamma D',
\]
so the ENTIRE $\Gamma$-orbit of
$D'$ is absent from the image
family. In particular the number
of represented $\Gamma$-orbits
of nonsingleton menus decreases
strictly after each proper gate.
At most $N_\Gamma$ gates therefore
reach a family consisting only of
atomic singleton menus. All gates
preserve converse, the THREE PC
inclusions and survivor heredity,
so their composite after $T$
is the desired selector.

The cardinality bound counts
each original exact menu at most
once and each nonempty safe trace
at most once more; every tagged
atomic singleton belongs to
$\mathcal H_{st}$. The action
need not be free: a menu fixed
by a nontrivial stabiliser is
simply an orbit of size one.
In the ACTUAL symmetric-original
source both $\Gamma$ and typed
converse fix the underlying
original literal fine atom names,
so stabilisers introduce no
atom-level fixed-point obstruction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why pure owner covariance cannot be replaced by atom permutations]
\label{rem:augmented-safe-pure-gamma-essential}
The $\Gamma$-action used in
\cref{lem:augmented-safe-algebra}
permutes only the ACTUAL
owner SORT TAGS and fixes
every literal original
fine atomic colour name.
This is essential for the
no-positive-return gate
argument and cannot be
weakened to an arbitrary
$\Gamma$-permutation of
fine atomic colour names.

Indeed consider only the
ABSTRACT finite selection
problem on one
two-element atom set
$L=\{a,b\}$, with
$\Gamma=C_2$ interchanging
a and b and with the
$\Gamma$-invariant
menu $D=L$.
A conservative
$\Gamma$-covariant
singleton selector
would satisfy
\[
                  \gamma(h(D))
                    =h(\gamma D)=h(D)
\]
for the nontrivial
$\gamma$. No atom of D
is fixed by $\gamma$,
so such a selector is
IMPOSSIBLE. This is
an obstruction to the
proposed STRONGER
abstract selector scope,
not an assertion that
these toy data form
an actual homogeneous
CCO structure.

In the actual
full-owner transversal,
the simultaneous owner
permutation is realised
by an ambient original
automorphism and fixes
the literal old fine
atom names. The
unilateral thin-$G$
switch instead moves
both endpoint tags and
colour names and is
handled by the SEPARATE
moving-reading
\cref{prop:moving-readings}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Safe completion from an injective certificate]
\label{lem:safe-global-completion}
In the primitive owner-typed SAP source with strong $S$-amalgamation,
let $P$ be a finite partial diagram with an injective completion
and at most one recorded literal atomic colour per pair.
There exists an injective \emph{same-vertex} completion in which
every originally unrecorded pair receives a colour in its
corresponding typed $S$ palette.
Moreover, if $P$ admits a structural pure-$\Gamma$ action,
the greatest nonempty strong-PC fixed family followed by the
augmented hereditary selector produces one legal complete
colouring preserving all these automorphisms and filling
all originally unrecorded pairs in $S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Start with any legal complete injective colouring on
the given finite vertex set.  Apply the one-edge recolouring
lemma successively to every originally unrecorded pair.
Each application changes only the selected pair and
retains all other pairs, including previous safe choices
and all original literal records.  It produces a complete
legal structure with the same owner labels, with every
original hole filled in $S$.

The latter complete colouring witnesses nonemptiness of
every domain in the greatest oriented strong-PC process:
its chosen atom survives every update because its triangles
remain legal.  It also witnesses that the final domain of
\emph{each originally unrecorded pair} meets $S$.
The augmented hereditary selector of
Lemma~\ref{lem:augmented-safe-algebra} therefore chooses
an $S$ atom at each such final domain, while preserving
original singleton records.  Its full-three-PC property
makes every resulting coloured triangle legal.
The family of final domains is canonical as the greatest
common fixed point, hence the pure-$\Gamma$ action
preserves it.  The selector is pure-$\Gamma$-covariant,
so the resulting fine colouring preserves every requested
structural pure-$\Gamma$ automorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The actual nontrivial packet interface]
\label{rem:safe-gauge-firewall}
For nontrivial thin $G$, the safety condition must be checked
for \emph{entire interpacket matrices}, under every unilateral
thin translation.  This orbit-safe full-matrix palette is
not automatically invariant under a new action on the
\emph{literal} raw colour names.  The selected reading
$h^u_{st}$ is obtained from the pure owner selector using the
original rectangular switching construction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:moving-readings}.  Its compatibility
with the $G$ endpoint switch is a \emph{separate}
requirement; it cannot be deduced merely by replacing the
pure $\Gamma$ action by an action permuting raw colours.
For the actual residual full-owner source, this packet-safe instance
is supplied by \cref{lem:orbit-safe-strong,lem:orbit-safe-all-row-interface}.
The first uses absolute real closure and full-owner closed SAP; the second
uses the injective full-row completion certificate from
\cref{lem:R07-W-completable}, with no equivariance requirement on that
certificate. Its rooted use is explicit in
\cref{lem:acf-relative-mi-normal}. These named provider hypotheses remain
part of the source-relative verification of the global theorem.
\end{remark}
