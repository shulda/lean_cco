\section{A reset-midpoint criterion for relative ordering EP}
\label{sec:reset-midpoint-ep}

We record an order-theoretic statement independent of CCO, exact
relations, or the particular hidden-coordinate construction.
Its \emph{relative} formulation matters: the Ramsey provider may
retain \emph{all} final order tasks, even when the source and
target of the EP step remember only the earlier tasks.

\begin{lemma}[Ramsey inheritance under a unary-closed sort restriction]
\label{lem:foundational-ramsey-sort-restriction}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a hereditary Ramsey class
of finite structures in a finite language with
named disjoint sorts, arbitrary relations, and
unary partial functions whose domains are
determined by source sorts. Let $I$ be a fixed
collection of sort symbols such that no unary
function with source in $I$ has its target
outside $I$. Define $\mathcal R|_I$ to be
the class of structures obtained by restricting
objects of $\mathcal R$ to their vertices in
sorts $I$, retaining all induced symbols on
those sorts. Then $\mathcal R|_I$ is hereditary
and Ramsey. If $\mathcal R$ has JEP or SAP,
then so does $\mathcal R|_I$.
No extra strong-amalgamation or real-representative
choices are needed for the Ramsey inheritance.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any $C\in\mathcal R$ its $I$-part
$C|_I$ is a functionally closed induced
substructure, hence itself belongs to
$\mathcal R$ by heredity. Every
$A\in\mathcal R|_I$ is therefore a
member of $\mathcal R$ whose universe
uses only sorts in $I$ (after choosing
its inducing isomorphism).

Fix $A,B\in\mathcal R|_I$ and $k$.
Choose $C\in\mathcal R$ with
$C\longrightarrow(B)^A_k$. The
sort predicates force EVERY embedding
of $A$ or $B$ into $C$ to have image
entirely in $C|_I$. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Emb}(A,C|_I)
         =\operatorname{Emb}(A,C)
\]
on the literal vertex maps, and every
monochromatic copy of $B$ supplied by
the full Ramsey arrow lies inside
$C|_I$. Thus $C|_I$ witnesses the
Ramsey arrow in $\mathcal R|_I$.
Heredity follows from closure under
induced functionally closed substructures.

For JEP or SAP, apply the corresponding
amalgamation/joint embedding construction
in $\mathcal R$ to the same all-$I$
inputs, and restrict the resulting output
to $I$. The existing embeddings are
unchanged on their images, and strong
intersection is preserved by restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ramsey inheritance under a functorial normalization]
\label{lem:foundational-ramsey-normalization}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a hereditary Ramsey class of finite structures,
with embeddings as morphisms. Let $N$ be an endofunctor such that
$N(A)$ has the same universe as $A$, $N(f)$ is the same vertex map
as $f$ for every embedding, and $N^2=N$. Then the full class
\[
                  \mathcal R_N=\{A\in\mathcal R:N(A)=A\}
\]
is hereditary and Ramsey for embeddings. If $\mathcal R$ has JEP,
then so does $\mathcal R_N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an induced substructure $B\subseteq A$ with $N(A)=A$, applying
$N$ to the inclusion gives an embedding $N(B)\hookrightarrow A$
with the same image and vertex map. Inducedness forces $N(B)=B$,
proving heredity. For fixed objects $A,B$ choose
$C\longrightarrow(B)^A_k$ in $\mathcal R$.
A colouring of $\operatorname{Emb}(A,N(C))$ pulls back to
$\operatorname{Emb}(A,C)$ by $e\mapsto N(e)$.
If $g:B\hookrightarrow C$ gives a monochromatic copy, then
$N(g):B\hookrightarrow N(C)$ does too: for every $f:A\hookrightarrow B$,
\[
                       N(g\circ f)=N(g)\circ f,
\]
since $N(f)=f$. The host $N(C)$ is fixed by idempotence.
No surjectivity of the map on embedding sets is needed.
Normalizing a joint embedding host similarly proves JEP.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Functorial normalization of synchronized quotient orders]
\label{lem:foundational-hierarchical-order-normalization}
Let the hidden finite-sorted language have a finite
family of proper meet-irreducible quotient sorts
\(B_J\), indexed by a finite distributive
equivalence lattice on each actual named original
sort, together with all definable upward quotient
maps and named quotient-transition bijections.
Assume closed transition holonomy is the identity.
Fix a priority on sort symbols, a priority on
the finitely many strict coarser coordinates
above each \(J\), and one representative
\(B_J\) of every transition orbit of cuts
\(J\prec J^+\).

From ANY total order on each hidden quotient sort,
extract for each representative cut the induced
order on the \(J\)-points INSIDE each fixed
\(J^+\)-class; transport that family of fibre
orders to all transition-equivalent copies.
Order all quotient sorts recursively from coarser
to finer, by first lexicographically comparing
the already ordered tuple of ALL strict coarser
proper MI coordinates and then, on equality
of that tuple, using the extracted order inside
the common \(J^+\)-fibre. Across different
hidden sort symbols use the fixed priority.

This construction is a functorial idempotent
normalization \(N\) on finite functionally
closed ordered hidden objects: it preserves
all old hidden structures and embeddings,
and its image is precisely the synchronized
parent-lex order class. Reversing the orders
of ONE selected cut task simultaneously along
its transition orbit does not change any
coarser task, and all finer derived comparisons
can be recomputed functorially. In particular
no independent sort-order or parent-lex choice
is made after a host has been selected.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a represented meet-irreducible \(J\)
with unique cover \(J^+\), every strict
coarser represented MI \(L>J\) contains
\(J^+\), and the intersection of all such
\(L\) is exactly \(J^+\). Indeed every
element of a finite distributive lattice
is the meet of the meet-irreducibles
above it, and \(L>J\) is equivalent to
\(L\ge J^+\). If \(J^+\) is the top
equivalence, the tuple of strict coarser
coordinates is empty and is assigned one
constant comparison key.

Thus two distinct \(J\)-points have the
SAME tuple of all strict coarser MI
coordinate values if and only if they
lie in the SAME \(J^+\)-class.
Induct downward on the finite lattice:
all strict coarser coordinate orders
are already defined, so their fixed-priority
tuple comparison gives a total order on
the distinct \(J^+\)-classes; within each
class the extracted local \(J\)-order
is a total tie-breaker. This yields one
total order on the \(J\)-sort, and the
definition is independent of individual
vertex labels.

A closed quotient transition is a
bijective unary term and transports
the named pair of quotients
\(J\prec J^+\) to its corresponding
cut. Path independence follows from
trivial transition holonomy. Therefore
all orders in the same transition orbit
can be synchronized from the fixed
representative task without ambiguity.

Let \(f:A\hookrightarrow B\) be an
embedding of arbitrarily ordered hidden
objects. It preserves the sort predicates,
all upward quotient and transition terms,
and the old within-sort total order.
Consequently it preserves each extracted
representative-fibre order and its transported
copies. By downward induction it preserves
each normalized coarser coordinate order,
hence every parent-key lex comparison and
every within-parent tie-breaker. Thus
the unchanged vertex map \(f\) is still
an embedding \(N(A)\hookrightarrow N(B)\).
Functoriality on compositions follows
because all underlying maps are unchanged.

On a normalized object the extracted
representative task orders are exactly
those from which its current normalized
orders were built; hence repeating the
construction changes nothing, proving
\(N^2=N\). Finally the coarser
coordinate keys are independent of
the choice of order within a single
\(J^+\)-fibre. Reversing that one
fibre-order task, coherently throughout
its transition orbit, fixes every
previous coarser task and only changes
the current task and later derived
lex comparisons. This proves the
claimed relative-order independence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-task reset-midpoint EP criterion]
\label{thm:abstract-reset-order-EP}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a hereditary Ramsey class with JEP of finite
ordered structures (possibly with unary partial functions, whose
domains are fixed by sorts and whose closures are finite).
Let $\mathcal U_1\to\mathcal U_0$ be reducts of $\mathcal R$,
reasonable, precompact and reduct-surjective, with
$\mathcal U_0$ forgetting \emph{one} task of ordering distinct
points inside designated disjoint finite fibres. Assume:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item Every $\mathcal U_1$ object has a full $\mathcal R$
expansion. The only data in $\mathcal U_1$ not present in
$\mathcal U_0$ are the linear orders on the designated fibres
and their functorially determined codes. Reversing the orders
in \emph{all} designated fibres of a $\mathcal U_1$ object,
with the derived codes changed accordingly, gives another
legal $\mathcal U_1$ object on exactly the same
$\mathcal U_0$ reduct.
\item The designated fibre equivalence and the symmetric
old binary relation $\mathfrak m$ are named or
quantifier-free definable already in the
$\mathcal U_0$ reduct. The relation $\mathfrak m$
only joins distinct designated points \emph{in one fibre},
and its complete finite \emph{unary-generated expanded}
ordered edge type is always ONE isomorphism type
$H_{\mathfrak m}$ when the first endpoint precedes
the second. In particular, the whole edge closure,
including all named fine relations and all auxiliary
order/equality codes, is determined by the oriented edge.
\item For every $A\in\mathcal R$ and each designated-fibre
comparison $u<v$ in $A$, there is an \emph{induced}
embedding $A\hookrightarrow A^+\in\mathcal R$ and
a fresh designated point $z$ in the same fibre with
\[
       u<z<v,\qquad
       u\,\mathfrak m\,z\,\mathfrak m\,v.
\]
The extension preserves the entire earlier expanded
structure. By iteration the same can be done for
\emph{every} such comparison in any one finite input.
\end{enumerate}

Then $\mathcal U_1$ has the expansion property over
$\mathcal U_0$. The witness is an unexpanded
$\mathcal U_0$ object chosen \emph{before} any competing
task-order expansion. No Ramsey hypothesis is imposed
on the intermediate classes $\mathcal U_0$ or
$\mathcal U_1$ separately.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $A^1\in\mathcal U_1$. Let $A^{1,-}$ be the
legal reversal of all its designated fibre orders,
over the same $A^0=A^1|_{\mathcal U_0}$.
Choose arbitrary full $\mathcal R$ expansions
$\widehat A^+$ and $\widehat A^-$ of these two
objects. By (iii), extend each so that every
comparison $u<v$ on a designated fibre has a
\emph{fresh} midpoint $z$ with two consecutive
$\mathfrak m$ edges $u<z<v$.
Because each midpoint extension is an induced
embedding, earlier comparisons and codes survive.
By JEP choose one $D\in\mathcal R$ containing
both extended patterns.

Use the \emph{full ambient} Ramsey property to
choose $C\in\mathcal R$ with
\[
                  C\longrightarrow
                      (D)^{H_{\mathfrak m}}_2 .
\]
Put $B^0=C|_{\mathcal U_0}$.
This is the fixed unexpanded host.

Take \emph{any} admissible competitor
$B^1\in\mathcal U_1$ whose $\mathcal U_0$
reduct is exactly $B^0$. For each embedding
of the ordered edge closure
$H_{\mathfrak m}\hookrightarrow C$, colour
it $+$ if the orientation of its two distinguished
roots in $B^1$ agrees with that in $C$, and $-$
otherwise. This is well-defined for all
embeddings because the roots lie in a single
designated fibre and their complete expanded
edge type is fixed by (ii).
The Ramsey arrow supplies an induced copy
$D'\subseteq C$ on which \emph{all} oriented
$\mathfrak m$ edges have the same comparison sign.

If the sign is $+$, use the stored
$\widehat A^+$ pattern inside $D'$.
For every one of its comparisons $u<v$
the chosen midpoint gives $u<z<v$ through
two $\mathfrak m$ edges; both retain
their orientation in $B^1$, so $u<v$
also in the competitor. The $\mathcal U_0$
structure is preserved automatically, hence
the stored roots yield an embedding
$A^1\hookrightarrow B^1$.

If the sign is $-$, instead use the stored
$\widehat A^-$ pattern. The two midpoint
edges now reverse their orientations in
$B^1$, so on \emph{every} pair of its
designated-fibre roots the competitor order
is the reverse of the stored reference order.
But $\widehat A^-$ was the reversal of
$A^1$ over the \emph{same} $\mathcal U_0$
structure. Thus the same induced roots,
viewed in $B^1$, form an expanded copy
of $A^1$. Any functorially determined task
codes agree because they are determined by
the indicated fibre orders and the unchanged
$\mathcal U_0$ data.

The host $B^0$ was selected independently
of the competing order, so this is precisely
EP for $\mathcal U_1/\mathcal U_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fresh reset midpoints from closed SAP]
\label{lem:abstract-fresh-reset-midpoint}
Let $\mathcal H$ be a hereditary class of finite
unary-functionally closed structures with SAP,
and let $\mathcal H^o$ be an ordered SAP
expansion whose orders can be freely extended
inside the designated fibres compatibly
with existing closed substructures.
Suppose for each pair of distinct same-fibre
points $u,v$ of a member $A\in\mathcal H$,
some permitted finite closed extension of
the unary closure $\langle u,v\rangle$ contains
$z$ in that fibre, outside $\langle u,v\rangle$,
such that $u\,\mathfrak m\,z\,\mathfrak m\,v$.
Then property (iii) of
\cref{thm:abstract-reset-order-EP} holds in
$\mathcal H^o$, provided the two-root generated
structures of the existing pair and of the
midpoint witness have the same \emph{complete}
unexpanded named type.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A^o$ be the existing finite ordered
object and $u<v$ the chosen pair.
Let $P=\langle u,v\rangle\le A^o$
be its actual induced unary closure.
By the complete two-root type assumption,
identify $P$ with the corresponding closure
inside a finite midpoint witness $T$.
Amalgamate $A^o$ and the ordered copy of $T$
\emph{strongly over} $P$, first placing the
new $z$ between $u,v$ in its designated
fibre; every comparison with other points
can be extended freely by the order-extension
hypothesis. Strongness keeps
$z\notin A^o$, since $z\notin P$ in the
midpoint witness. All original relations
of both finite sides, including the
two $\mathfrak m$ edges, are preserved.
Iterating over the finitely many designated
comparisons creates distinct fresh midpoints
while preserving every earlier placement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Composition of relative expansion properties]
\label{lem:foundational-relative-EP-composition}
Let $\mathcal K_2\to\mathcal K_1\to
\mathcal K_0$ be reduct maps of hereditary
finite relational/unary-functional ages, with
each expanded age reduct-surjective onto its
predecessor. If $\mathcal K_2$ has EP over
$\mathcal K_1$ and $\mathcal K_1$ has EP over
$\mathcal K_0$, then $\mathcal K_2$ has EP
over $\mathcal K_0$. The same holds for
any finite chain. No Ramsey assumption on
the intermediate classes is necessary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $A^2\in\mathcal K_2$. Use EP of
$\mathcal K_2/\mathcal K_1$ to choose
$B^1\in\mathcal K_1$ such that
\emph{every} $\mathcal K_2$ expansion
of $B^1$ contains an embedding of
$A^2$. Next use EP of
$\mathcal K_1/\mathcal K_0$ to choose
$C^0\in\mathcal K_0$ such that
\emph{every} $\mathcal K_1$ expansion
of $C^0$ contains $B^1$.

Given any $\mathcal K_2$ expansion
$C^2$ of the \emph{already fixed}
$C^0$, its intermediate reduct
$C^1$ contains some embedding
$f:B^1\hookrightarrow C^1$.
Restrict the full $\mathcal K_2$
structure of $C^2$ to the induced
image $f(B^1)$ and transport it
back along $f$. By heredity this
is a legal $\mathcal K_2$ expansion
$B^2$ of precisely $B^1$.
The defining property of $B^1$
gives $A^2\hookrightarrow B^2$,
and composing with $f$ yields
$A^2\hookrightarrow C^2$.
Thus $C^0$ witnesses the composite
EP, with the existential host
chosen before the arbitrary final
expansion. Iterate along a finite
chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source and quantifier firewall]
\label{rem:abstract-reset-EP-scope}
Condition (ii) is about the \emph{entire}
unary-generated expanded fine type, not just
the two-point equivalence reduct.
A relation $\mathfrak m$ satisfying
$\mathfrak m;\mathfrak m=J^+$ supplies
one midpoint for each pair in the same
$J^+$ fibre, but \emph{by itself} does
not supply a fresh midpoint in every
finite closed configuration: the latter
also needs the closure type and SAP in
\cref{lem:abstract-fresh-reset-midpoint}.
The ordering reversal in (i) concerns
only the \emph{one current order task};
earlier named order tasks remain fixed.
For multiple tasks, relative EP must
be composed separately along the tower
of reasonable precompact reducts.
The proof never treats an intermediate
partially ordered class as Ramsey.
\end{remark}
