\section{A completable structural HKN reservoir}\label{sec:hkn-reservoir}

This is the only use of the published HKN theorems in the structural
stage.  We use the versions for finite languages with unary
functions and an action $\Gamma$ permuting language symbols.  HKN~\cite{HKN}
Theorem~1.1 supplies an irreducible-structure-faithful EPPA witness;
HKN Theorem~1.2, for an irreducible finite source $A$ and an
integer $n$, supplies a further such witness $B_0$ in which each
substructure on at most $n$ vertices has a homomorphism-embedding
into a free tree amalgam of $\Gamma$-relabelled copies of $A$.
For clarity, the maps in the HKN conclusions are permitted to have
one global language component, as in the published $\Gamma_L$
formalism.  No completeness, owner exclusivity, or bijective
function axioms are assumed of the unrestricted HKN output;
they will be recovered below from irreducible faithfulness.

Remain in the full-owner residual setting
$N=\pi_E^{-1}(B)$, $R_E=F$, with a chosen subgroup
$\Gamma\le\Aut(B)$ acting only on owner names, and with
$\operatorname{acl}_N(X)=F[X]$ and the full-owner transversal
$U_B$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:full-owner-transversal}.
Fix a finite nonempty \emph{complete full-packet} substructure
$A\subseteq N$; all its $G$-orbits are included.  Every
requested partial automorphism of the original source, together
with its compatible owner action $\gamma\in\Gamma$, extends to
a partial automorphism of $A$ with the same language component:
saturate its real domain and range by $G$ and use the thin
transport law.  For an empty real-domain request there is no
saturation to make; the language component remains $\gamma$.
The finite list of all such compatible requests is independent of
the subsequent completion choices.

Let $b=\max\{3,\ell+1\}$ be the bad-cycle bound from
Theorem~\ref{thm:locality-bound}, calculated \emph{after} the
finite owner set $B$ has been fixed.  It depends only on the
finite typed palette, and no uniform bound across different
quotient witnesses is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Completable HKN packet reservoir]
\label{thm:completable-reservoir}
There exists a finite $\Gamma$-structure $B_0\supseteq A$, in the
language of named owner predicates, original fine binary colours
and unary functions $t_g$ ($g\in G$), such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every point belongs to a unique literal regular full
      $G$-packet, with exactly one owner label at each point;
\item each requested partial map of $A$ with its compatible owner
      language component $\gamma\in\Gamma$ extends to a
      $\Gamma$-automorphism of $B_0$;
\item each pair of distinct packets has either a complete recorded
      fine matrix of colours outside $F$, or no recorded interpacket
      edges;
\item the entire partial structure $B_0$ has an actual
      \emph{injective} completion $C_0$ in $N$ fixing $A$;
\item every irreducible substructure of $B_0$ is carried into
      $A$ by one $\Gamma$-automorphism of the partial structure
      $B_0$ (irreducible-structure faithfulness).
\end{enumerate}
No compatibility of the choice $C_0$ with the $\Gamma$-action is
required.  In particular, the missing interpacket edges of $B_0$
are \emph{not} filled in permanently before the later valuation
construction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Exact HKN scope]
HKN Theorem~1.2 controls the $\Gamma$-structure on at most
$n$ \emph{vertices}, so the locality parameter is
$b|G|$, not just $b$.  Irreducible structure faithfulness
controls functional closure and the all-or-nothing recorded
matrices.  Neither of these alone implies completion in the
CCO source; completion additionally uses closed SAP, the
origin-transversal reduction, and finite bad-cycle locality.
The result is ordinary structural $\Gamma$-EPPA at this stage;
coherence is not used and no second valued HKN application is
assumed.
\end{remark}

The full-row construction in \cref{sec:full-rows} uses this reservoir
contract. The remaining subsections prove it: first the packet-completion
tools in the named-owner source, then the local HKN maps, and finally
the assembly of $B_0$.

\subsection{Packet completion in the named-owner source}

\begin{lemma}[Collision separation for recorded packet diagrams]
\label{lem:packet-collision}
Let $P$ be a finite partial diagram consisting of literal disjoint
regular full $G$-packets, with exactly one actual owner label at
each point, total functions $t_g$ within each packet, and only
complete fine interpacket matrices in colours outside $F$ as
records.  Suppose there is an owner-preserving, packetwise injective
homomorphism $q:P\to N$ respecting all the records, and let $A_\ast$
be a specified complete full-packet base on which $q$ is already
an induced embedding.  It can be modified to an \emph{injective
completion} in $N$ of the displayed partial diagram, with all
distinct packets separated, while fixing $q|_{A_\ast}$.
The base may be empty.  Here ``completion'' adds colours to
unrecorded pairs; this is not an HKN embedding reflecting their
absence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the images of two distinct packets collide in the same
$F$-packet, move one packet $\Pi$ which is not in the protected
complete base.  Let $S$ be the finite set of \emph{entire images of
the recorded neighbouring packets}, and fix all named actual
owners.  Since each edge from $\Pi$ to those neighbours has colour
outside $F$, its origin image $q(o_\Pi)$ lies outside
$C=F[S]=\operatorname{acl}_N(S)$.  In this residual case the
full-$F$-packet closure $F[\cdot]$ is the actual named groupoid
closure.  Therefore $C\cup F[q(o_\Pi)]$ is a finite closed
extension of the finite closed set $C$, with disjoint new packet.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-finite-avoidance} in $N$, taking
the finite forbidden set $Z$ to consist of \emph{all} other
currently used packet images outside $C$ (and, if desired, the
old image of $\Pi$ itself).  The lemma supplies an owner-preserving
automorphism fixing $C$ pointwise which sends the whole image
packet of $\Pi$ away from $Z$.  Replace only the vertex images
in $\Pi$ by their images under this automorphism.  All $t_g$
commute with owner-preserving automorphisms, so every packet
function equation and recorded fine matrix to the fixed neighbouring
packets survives.  No unrecorded pair imposes a condition.
At least one packet in every collision lies outside $A_\ast$,
because $q|_{A_\ast}$ is already embedded.  The replacement separates
$\Pi$ from \emph{every} other used packet: images in $C$ cannot
collide with it because its new image is disjoint from $C$, and
images outside $C$ are in the forbidden set $Z$.  Thus the number
of unordered colliding packet pairs strictly decreases, and no new
collision is introduced.  The finite process terminates with an
injective record-preserving completion.  In particular, we
never fix an unrecorded collision target during a move.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Origin-transversal reduction]
\label{lem:packet-origins}
Choose an origin $o_i$ for each full packet $\Pi_i$ in such a
diagram and put $\beta_i=\operatorname{own}(o_i)\in B$.  For each
recorded pair let $c_{ij}=\col(o_i,o_j)\notin F$; the whole
matrix is required to be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-matrix}
 \col(t_g o_i,t_h o_j)=(g+h)c_{ij}\qquad(g,h\in G).
\end{equation}
Form a partial typed diagram on the indices $i$, with sort
$\beta_i$ and edge $c_{ij}$ exactly for recorded packet pairs.
An injective typed completion of this \emph{origin transversal}
in $U_B$ lifts to an injective completion of the entire recorded
packet diagram in $N$, preserving a complete embedded source base
up to its unique named-owner isomorphism.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a completion $e(i)$ of the origins in distinct full
$F$-packets, set
$\widehat e(t_g o_i)=t_g e(i)$.  This is well-defined and
injective, has the prescribed owner labels
$g\beta_i$, and preserves every record by
\eqref{eq:packet-matrix} and the thin transport law.
There are no other recorded interpacket relations.  The complete
source substructure is recovered as an induced complete
substructure; its image is moved to the prescribed copy by joint
homogeneity of the actual named owner sorts
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-sorts-source}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Realisation of tree amalgams over complete packet bases]
\label{lem:packet-tree-realisation}
Let $D$ be a finite free tree amalgam of copies of $A$ with
independently permitted $\Gamma$-relabelled owner predicates and
unchanged function symbols $t_g$.  There is an injective map
$\iota:D\to N$ preserving every displayed relation, owner predicate
and unary function, and restricting to an induced embedding on each
complete source copy.  If a designated source copy has already been
realised in $N$, its image may be kept fixed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $\Gamma$-relabelled copy of $A$ belongs to the actual named-owner
age of $N$: realise its language permutation by the ambient owner
lift, which fixes fine-colour names and commutes with all $t_g$.
By the definition of a substructure for the HKN unary-function
language, an overlap in a free amalgam is closed under every $t_g$.
Since the copies are regular full packets and the $t_g$ are total,
that overlap is a union of whole $G$-packets.  Hence it is closed
under the actual relative algebraic closure
$\operatorname{acl}_N(S)=F[S]$ from~\eqref{eq:full-owner-acl}.

Build the finite tree one free amalgamation at a time.  Realise its
first source copy in $N$, respecting a prescribed copy if given.
Inductively, keep a \emph{complete} induced realisation of the finite
full-packet structure already constructed; only the relations
specified by the tree need be retained.  To attach a further source
copy over the displayed common substructure $S$, use named-owner
homogeneity to realise that copy with its overlap on the already
chosen image of $S$.  The two complete finite packet structures
have a common closed full-packet base $S$.  Closed SAP
(Corollary~\ref{cor:relative-closed-sap}) gives a strong amalgam in
the actual named-owner age, with no new identifications.  Embed this
finite amalgam back into $N$ over the existing complete realisation,
using its homogeneous extension property.  Forget the incident
fine colours that were not specified in the free tree amalgam.
Iterating yields an injective homomorphism of the entire partial tree
and an induced embedding on each complete source copy.  Different
source copies may carry different $\Gamma$-relabelled owner names;
the common overlap has the same \emph{actual} names on both sides,
and no equivariant choice of origins or completions is involved.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local HKN maps and injective packet completions}

\begin{lemma}[What an HKN homomorphism-embedding preserves]
\label{lem:hkn-irreducible-packet-control}
Let $P$ be a finite unary-function-closed partial diagram made of
literal disjoint regular full $G$-packets, with recorded interpacket
edges only in complete fine matrices.  Let $f:P\to D$ be a
$\Gamma$-homomorphism-embedding in the HKN sense, allowing its one
global language component.  Then $f$ restricts to an embedding on
\emph{each} full packet and on the union of \emph{each recorded pair}
of distinct packets.  In particular, it preserves the entire
specified function-value sets and all recorded fine edges on those
substructures.  It need not be injective on the union of two
\emph{unrecorded} packets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A single full packet is generated by any of its points under the
unary functions $t_g$.  It is irreducible: any substructure
containing a generator contains its whole packet, so it cannot
split as a free amalgam of two proper substructures.  If two
packets have a recorded edge between them, their union is also
irreducible.  Indeed the unary closure forces each packet to be
contained in one side of a purported free splitting, and the
recorded cross-edge forces both packets into the same side.

By the \emph{definition} of HKN homomorphism-embedding, its
restriction to every irreducible substructure is an embedding;
see HKN, Section~2.3.  Apply this to the packet and recorded-pair
unions.  For unary set-valued functions, the embedding identity
uses equality of the complete function-value sets, not merely
inclusion.  The single global $\Gamma$-language component is the
same on all these restrictions.  After composing with any
injective record-preserving realisation into $N$, two recorded
adjacent packets must also occupy different $F$-packets: a
recorded cross-colour lies outside $F$, whereas every pair in
one $F$-packet has colour in $F$.  No such conclusion about
unrecorded pairs follows: their union can be a free amalgam over
the empty substructure and need not be irreducible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Global language normalisation for local HKN tree maps]
\label{lem:hkn-global-language-normalisation}
Let $P$ be any finite $\Gamma$-structure
in the owner-language/packet signature
and let $f=(\gamma,f_V):P\to D$ be a
$\Gamma$-homomorphism-embedding, where
$D$ is a free tree amalgamation of
permitted $\Gamma$-relabelled copies
of one complete full-packet source $A$.
Here $\Gamma$ permutes ONLY owner
predicate names, fixing all literal
fine binary atoms and all named
unary translations $t_g$.

Then, after replacing the ENTIRE
target $D$ by its ONE GLOBAL language
relabel $D'=\gamma^{-1}D$, the same
vertex map defines a
homomorphism-embedding
\[
              f'=(\mathrm{id}_L,f_V):P\to D'.
\tag{HLN}\label{eq:hkn-global-language-normalisation}
\]
The structure $D'$ remains a free
tree amalgamation of permitted
$\Gamma$-relabelled copies of $A$.
Every source irreducible substructure
on which $f$ was an embedding
remains embedded by $f'$.
In particular, a protected complete
source copy has its ACTUAL original
owner predicates, fine atom names
and unary translation equations
preserved literally BEFORE any
tree realisation inside $N_B$.

No choice of a separate language
component on individual tree
copies, nor a group-theoretic
section $\Gamma\to\Aut(N_B)$, is
involved in this normalisation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The relabelling morphism
$(\gamma^{-1},\mathrm{id}_D):
D\to\gamma^{-1}D$
is an isomorphism of
$\Gamma$-structures.
Its composition with
$f=(\gamma,f_V)$ is
$(\mathrm{id}_L,f_V)$,
which remains a
homomorphism-embedding:
an isomorphism preserves
and reflects all relations,
full unary function-value
sets and irreducibility.
Apply the SAME relabelling
$\gamma^{-1}$ to every
node/copy and every overlap
of the original free tree
construction. Free
amalgamation commutes with
global language relabelling,
and every relabelled copy
of A remains an allowed
$\Gamma$-relabelled copy
because $\Gamma$ is a group.
Thus $D'$ is a tree
amalgamation of the same
permitted source copies,
with unchanged underlying
vertex incidence graph.
On each embedded irreducible
piece, the composite now has
IDENTITY language component,
as asserted. In particular,
if a complete source subcopy
must be protected, its actual
named owner labels are not
modified when matching its
image to the prescribed
copy in the owner-named
ambient source.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Injectivisation of a local HKN tree packet map]
\label{lem:hkn-local-tree-packet-injectivisation}
Let \(P\) be a finite partial
\(\Gamma\)-diagram on \(k\) distinct
ACTUAL regular full \(G\)-packets,
with exactly one owner label on
each point, all unary
\(t_g\)-functions total, and
recorded interpacket relations
consisting only of entire
nonthin fine-colour matrices.
Assume there exists a
\(\Gamma\)-homomorphism-embedding
\[
        f:P\longrightarrow D,
\]
where \(D\) is a finite free
tree amalgam of permitted
\(\Gamma\)-relabelled copies of
the same complete full-packet
source \(A\). Then \(P\)
admits an INJECTIVE
record-preserving completion
in \(N_B\) on its original
finite vertex set, with
all \(k\) packets occupying
distinct actual \(F\)-classes.

If \(P\) contains a designated
complete full-packet copy
\(A_\ast\) on which \(f\)
restricts to an embedding,
its image can be identified
with the prescribed copy
of \(A_\ast\subseteq N_B\)
and kept pointwise fixed
throughout injectivisation.
No global injectivity of
\(f\) is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:hkn-global-language-normalisation}, replace the target
by one global $\gamma^{-1}$-relabel so that $f$ has identity language
component. This preserves the permitted tree structure and makes all
owner predicates literal before any protected-base alignment.

If a complete $A_\ast$ is protected, its image under the normalised
map is an irreducible substructure of the tree. It lies in one
source copy by the irreducible-piece property of tree amalgamations
\cite[Observation~9.3]{HKN}. Realise that source copy in $N_B$ and
use named-owner homogeneity to align its induced full-packet
substructure $f(A_\ast)$ with the prescribed $A_\ast$. Now
\cref{lem:packet-tree-realisation}, with this whole source copy
fixed, gives an injective realisation $j:D\to N_B$ of the tree.
If no base is protected, apply that lemma directly.

The composite $q=j\circ f$ preserves literal owners, all unary
packet equations and every recorded matrix. By
\cref{lem:hkn-irreducible-packet-control} it is injective on each
packet and on each recorded two-packet union. Its restriction to
$A_\ast$ is the prescribed induced embedding. Since all recorded
cross colours lie outside $F$, these are the hypotheses of
\cref{lem:packet-collision}. Applying that lemma gives an injective
record-preserving $q':P\to N_B$ fixing the protected base; only
unrecorded packet collisions are separated. Pull back the complete
induced structure on $q'(P)$ to obtain the asserted same-vertex
completion. No owner relabelling is performed after base alignment.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Chord-complete HKN locality on short origin cycles]
\label{lem:hkn-chord-complete-short-cycle}
Let \(P\) be a finite
partially recorded actual
regular full-\(G\)-packet
diagram satisfying the
three source conditions
of
\cref{lem:hkn-local-tree-packet-injectivisation}.
Fix a simple SPECIFIED
origin cycle with \(k\)
DISTINCT actual packet
indices
\(p_1,\ldots,p_k\),
and assume \(k|G|\le n\).
Suppose the HKN locality
condition of Theorem~1.2
holds for EVERY
functionally closed
substructure of \(P\)
with at most \(n\)
actual vertices.
Then the COMPLETE
INDUCED partial packet
diagram on the packets
\(p_1,\ldots,p_k\)
admits an injective
same-vertex completion
in \(N_B\), preserving
ALL its recorded
nonthin fine matrices,
including EVERY recorded
chord between
nonconsecutive indices
of the chosen cycle.
In particular the
cycle has an injective
typed origin completion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the substructure
\(P_C\) generated by the
\(k\) listed origin
vertices under all
total \(t_g\)-functions.
Since the actual
packets are disjoint,
\(P_C\) has precisely
\(k|G|\le n\)
vertices, and it is
the FULL induced
PARTIAL subdiagram on
these packets. Every
recorded chord belongs
to \(P_C\), along with
the entire
\(G\times G\) fine
matrix that it forces;
none is discarded
while singling out
the chosen cycle.

HKN locality therefore
gives ONE
\(\Gamma\)-homomorphism-
embedding
\(f:P_C\to D\) into
one finite tree
amalgamation of
permitted owner-
relabelled source
copies. Apply
\cref{lem:hkn-local-tree-packet-injectivisation}
to the ENTIRE \(P_C\).
Its output is
a same-vertex
injective completion
of ALL the old
recorded packet data
on those \(k\)
indices in \(N_B\).
Restricting this
completion to the
\(k\) origin points
realises the selected
typed cycle.
The proof never assumes
that an arbitrary map
of JUST its \(k\)
unclosed origins is a
valid HKN source
substructure, and
never drops an induced
recorded chord.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Unrecorded packets can collapse under an HKN map]
\label{ex:hkn-unrecorded-collapse}
Take $G\cong C_2$ and three regular packets
$\Pi_0,\Pi_1,\Pi_2$.  Record one complete nonthin matrix
between $\Pi_0,\Pi_1$ and the same matrix between
$\Pi_1,\Pi_2$, but no matrix between $\Pi_0,\Pi_2$.
The map to a complete two-packet source which identifies
$\Pi_0$ with $\Pi_2$ and fixes $\Pi_1$ is a
homomorphism-embedding: all irreducible substructures lie in
one packet or one recorded adjacent pair, on which the map is
an embedding.  It is not globally injective.  In a source with
infinitely many regular packets and the same nonthin matrix
between distinct packets, the diagram nevertheless has an
injective completion using three distinct packets.  This example
separates the HKN definition from the later, genuinely injective
completion step.
\end{example}

\subsection{Assembly of the reservoir}

We now prove \cref{thm:completable-reservoir}.

\begin{proof}
The finite source $A$ is irreducible because it is a complete
fine-colour diagram on its vertices. Its $\Gamma$-relabellings
are realisable in $N$, by actual quotient-owner homogeneity
and its fixed language action. The HKN language group acts
ONLY on owner-predicate symbols, leaving the named fine
binary atom symbols and unary $t_g$ symbols fixed.
There are finitely many compatible requests, including
EVERY empty-real-domain request with its specified
$\gamma\in\Gamma$. The latter are genuine partial
$\Gamma$-automorphisms of the empty substructure:
the language component does not disappear when
the real domain is empty.

Invoke the unrestricted irreducible-faithful HKN EPPA
construction and the HKN sparsening/local tree theorem,
with $n\ge b|G|$. The resulting finite $\Gamma$-structure
$B_0$ extends those language-component requests, is
irreducible-structure faithful, and admits for EVERY
substructure on at most $n$ actual vertices a
$\Gamma$-homomorphism-embedding into a finite free
tree amalgam of permitted relabelled source copies.
This is a HKN homomorphism-embedding, NOT an
injective completion of the whole $B_0$.
The latter is the additional CCO task proved below.

For $v\in B_0$ take its closure under the unary symbols
$t_g$.  It is one-generated and therefore irreducible: a free
splitting into two proper closed substructures would have to
place its generator on one side and all its closure on that side.
Irreducible faithfulness sends this closure by
an automorphism of $B_0$ (with ONE global permitted
language component) into the literal source copy
$A\subseteq B_0$; hence its inverse transport
is isomorphic to an induced functionally closed
substructure of $A$. Here HKN EMBEDDINGS in a
unary-functional language must preserve and REFLECT
the entire function-value set. If $t_g$ were
undefined or failed the full regular group equation
on even one point of this generated closure, its
image could not be an induced closed substructure
of the original total-regular $G$-source $A$.
Every nonempty substructure of $A$ closed under
all $t_g$ is a union of WHOLE regular $G$-orbits,
so the ONE-generated closure embeds as exactly
one regular packet. Consequently each
$t_g$ is total there and all group equations
hold. The generated regular packets partition
the universe of $B_0$: two one-generated
closures are either identical or disjoint,
because their group equations make every
orbit of $G$ a partition class.
The source has exactly one owner predicate
per vertex, and the singleton irreducible
faithfulness forces the same for every
point of $B_0$.

If one recorded fine edge joins two distinct generated packets,
the union of the two function closures is irreducible: no free
amalgam decomposition can separate the endpoints of that edge,
and each full packet is itself irreducible.  By faithfulness it
lies, after an automorphism of $B_0$, in a $\Gamma$-relabelling
of $A$.  Hence every one of the $|G|^2$ fine edges of the
interpacket matrix is present and agrees with
\eqref{eq:packet-matrix}; these colours are all outside $F$.
This proves the first three assertions, including the exact
\emph{all-or-nothing} nature of recorded pairs.

For every simple specified origin cycle on $k\le b$ distinct
packet indices, apply \cref{lem:hkn-chord-complete-short-cycle}
with $n\ge b|G|$. Its hypothesis concerns the full induced,
functionally closed $k|G|$-vertex packet diagram, including every
recorded chord, and is supplied by the HKN local tree property.
The lemma gives an injective packet-separated completion of that
short origin cycle in $U_B$; its language normalisation, closed
SAP realisation and collision separation need not be repeated here.
A simple origin cycle with more than $b$
vertices is automatically completable by the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:locality-bound}.  Therefore \emph{every}
specified simple cycle of the complete origin partial diagram
has a typed homomorphic completion.  Apply semantic homomorphic
CCO to the origin partial diagram in $U_B$ and its injective
completion assertion from Lemma~\ref{lem:full-owner-transversal}.
This embeds its origins into distinct $G$-packets.
Lemma~\ref{lem:packet-origins} now restores the complete thin
packets and recorded matrices, yielding an injective completion
$C_0$ of the whole $B_0$ inside $N$.  Its source copy $A$ is
complete and, by actual named-owner homogeneity, can be aligned
with the original designated $A$.

Throughout, $B_0$ retains its \emph{original} partially recorded
interpacket relations, its $\Gamma$-automorphisms and the
irreducible-structure faithfulness supplied by HKN. This proves
assertion (5) as well.  The
embedding $C_0$ is only an existence certificate, so the
construction never requires the arbitrarily supplied completion
colours to be invariant under those automorphisms.
\end{proof}
