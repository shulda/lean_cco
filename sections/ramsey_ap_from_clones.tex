\section{Ramsey expansions by compatible meet-irreducible imaginaries}\label{sec:ramsey-clones}

\noindent\emph{Status and source boundary.}
The following result is an internal, source-relative deduction from
(A108) pure-clone SAPification and the safe splitting, hidden SAP,
bounded-obstruction and typed terminal Ramsey theorems of the
internal Ramsey proof appendix, Theorem~\ref{thm:internal-v27-general-ramsey}.  In particular it is
\emph{not} a consequence of ordinary EPPA, the HKN valuation
construction, or of generic $\Lambda$-ultrametric Ramsey theory
alone.  The novel reduction to compatible MI-tuples was developed in
R17--R18 and checked internally, but the long V27 source theorems
have not thereby received an independent certification.


Let $Q$ be countably infinite, transitive and homogeneous in a finite complete
symmetric binary language. Assume AP and semantic \emph{homomorphic} CCO.
Triangle fullness follows from the AP fan argument C02 (or can be retained in
the source contract). Write $F=\Sigma_Q(\Id)$ for the largest invariant
finite-class equivalence. We do \emph{not} assume that $F$ is MI, exact or thin.

\begin{theorem}[AP Ramsey expansion, relative to A108 and V27]\label{thm:ramsey-general-ap}
The class $\Age(Q)$ has a reasonable, reduct-surjective, precompact Ramsey
expansion by finitely many unary and binary relations.
\end{theorem}

The inputs are the internal pure-clone SAPification Theorem~\ref{thm:internal-clone-sap} and the actual safe-phase and
terminal hidden construction: reasonable finite unary terminalisation;
represented terminal same-sort equivalence lattices and class Helly; typed
profile Helly and Peircean composition; mixed semantic cylinders on all proper
coordinate pairs; uniformly finitely many unary term normal forms; and the
hereditary ordered hidden Ramsey class. The last assertion contains the
substantive hidden SAP and bounded-obstruction/HN argument. It is not inferred
merely from naming imaginaries. Their complete non-published proofs are included in the internal Ramsey proof appendix, together with source-provenance pointers. The R17--R18 audits record the new transfer.

We use the clone-preservation argument of R17 and only the \emph{empty-base}
part of its quotient infinitude argument. Neither B32's real-hull theorem nor
EPPA, finite Ramsey degrees, a general finite-exact Ramsey lift, or the
Birkhoff owner-witness construction is an input here. For finite $Q$, uniquely
labelling all its points gives the conclusion separately.

\subsection{The terminal clone source and its coordinates}

By A108 replace $Q$ by $M=Q\times\NN$. Distinct clones of one old point
receive a new colour $c$; all other colours come from $Q$. Then $M$ is
homogeneous SAP+CCO, and its clone congruence is
\[
                       K=\Id\cup c=c^2.
\]
The square here denotes actual represented composition, including equality.

\begin{lemma}[Clone-stable terminalisation]\label{lem:ramsey-clone-stability}
The safe unary splits of V27 yield
$M^\dagger=Q^\dagger\times\NN$, where $Q^\dagger$ is a reasonable finite
unary expansion of $Q$. Every nonempty terminal sort of $Q^\dagger$ is
infinite. In each corresponding clone sort $s$, $K_s$ is proper and exact.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Independent permutations within clone fibres make $K_s$ the least
nonidentity invariant equivalence in every current clone-saturated sort. A
proper finite-index interval $Y<X$ therefore has $K_s\le Y$. The source gate
$L_*$ used by V27 contains $Y$. For a transported bridge $R:s\to t$, the
target gate is $L_t=R^\smile R$ and $L_*R=R$. Given $yK_ty'$ and $xRy$,
replace $x$ by a fresh source clone $x_0$ distinct from $y,y'$. Transposing the
two target clones while fixing $x_0$ gives $x_0Ry'$, hence $yL_ty'$.
Thus every gate contains its clone congruence. Bits pulled back from these
gates are constant on clones, at every step.

For infinitude, any looped same-sort atom $a$ admits phase difference zero.
If its cylinder is rigid with group element $h$, an $(a,a,a)$ triangle gives
$h^2=h$, so $h=1$; a nonrigid cylinder admits both differences. An infinite
parent quotient unary type has an infinite monochromatic clique of a looped
atom outside $K$. Give arbitrarily many such points the same new bit as any
realised child type. Every old triangle and its permitted phase differences
are valid in the specific V27 lifted triangle table. The child type therefore
has arbitrarily many distinct quotient realisations. Iterate from the infinite
transitive $Q$. This uses the phase table, not arbitrary unary labelling.
\end{proof}

In the finite terminal lattice $\Lambda_s$, choose a fixed list of distinct
proper MI equivalences $J_{s,1},\ldots,J_{s,r_s}$ above $K_s$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-mi-meet}
                  \bigcap_{i=1}^{r_s}J_{s,i}=K_s.
\end{equation}
One may choose all proper MI elements above $K_s$. They are represented and
are already sorts $B_{s,J_{s,i}}$ of V27's hidden language. Each old point
$q\in Q_s^\dagger$ gives a tuple
\[
       \eta_s(q)=\bigl([x]_{J_{s,i}}\bigr)_{i=1}^{r_s},
       \qquad x\in\pi_K^{-1}(q).
\]
This is independent of the clone $x$ and is injective by~\eqref{eq:ramsey-mi-meet}.
Only the finite list of \emph{symbols} is ordered once and for all. No
point-dependent choice is required. We do not introduce hidden cell vertices.

\subsection{Recognising tuples and reconstructing fine colours}

For actual coordinate vertices $b\in B_{s,J}$, $d\in B_{t,L}$, let
$\Spec(b,d)$ be the diversity colours realised by distinct representatives.
For distinct hidden endpoints this is the named mixed semantic cylinder of
V27. On the diagonal of one coordinate sort define
$\Spec(b,b)=J^\circ$, the diversity support of $J$. This is an equality case,
not an off-diagonal cylinder predicate.

\begin{lemma}[Quantifier-free tuple domain]\label{lem:ramsey-mi-domain}
For $b_i\in B_{s,J_{s,i}}$, the tuple $(b_i)_i$ represents one old
$Q_s^\dagger$ point if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-domain}
                 c_s\in\Spec(b_i,b_j)\quad(i<j).
\end{equation}
This condition is quantifier-free in the existing binary hidden language.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two actual $J,L$ classes meet if and only if their representative spectrum
contains $c_s$. A common point has two distinct clones in both classes.
Conversely, a $c_s$-pair in the two classes belongs to one $K_s$-class, which
lies entirely in both because $K_s\le J,L$. Thus~\eqref{eq:ramsey-domain} says that
the classes meet pairwise. Terminal class Helly gives a nonempty common
intersection, and~\eqref{eq:ramsey-mi-meet} makes it exactly one $K_s$-class. Membership
of the fixed atom $c_s$ is a finite disjunction of named cylinder predicates.
The sort tags are included in the formula. The case $r_s=1$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

This also applies to orphan coordinates: a completed finite hidden object is
an induced closed substructure of actual coordinate data. Actual realisation
is used to prove the formula, not to define a noncanonical choice of tuples.

For the colour calculation we use $*$ for strong typed \emph{diversity}
composition. In this convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-clone-diversity-meet}
                 \bigcap_iJ_{s,i}^\circ=\{c_s\}.
\end{equation}
The meets are nonempty. No logical equality atom is inserted into these
strong path products.

\begin{lemma}[Typed meet-product distributivity]
\label{lem:ramsey-typed-distributivity}
For composable nonempty strong typed exact domains
$A=\bigcap_iP_i$, $B=\bigcap_jQ_j$ in the terminal
\emph{diversity} pair palettes, with positive reachable-profile
presentations, one has
\[
 A*B=\bigcap_{i,j}(P_i*Q_j).
\]
Consequently strong products distribute over any nonempty
exact meet in either coordinate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The general typed exact-product theorem
\cref{lem:typed-exact-products} from Part~I applies verbatim:
its proof uses only named transitive typed palettes, SAP,
semantic homomorphic CCO and strong off-diagonal path semantics,
not primitivity or (NR2). The actual terminal clone source has
these properties under the genuine V27 safe splits. In
particular, all products here exclude the logical diagonal;
the equality-inclusive represented product used on an AP-only
quotient is \emph{not} substituted for $*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Singleton fine-colour reconstruction]\label{lem:ramsey-singleton-recovery}
Let $q,r$ be distinct old points of terminal sorts $s,t$ and let $a$ be their
original typed fine colour. For their compatible coordinate tuples,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-singleton}
 \bigcap_{i,j}\Spec\bigl(\eta_s(q)_i,\eta_t(r)_j\bigr)=\{a\}.
\end{equation}
In particular every original fine colour is quantifier-free definable on
valid tuples using the hidden binary shadows and equality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
V27's mixed-cylinder theorem gives
\[
 \Spec\bigl(\eta_s(q)_i,\eta_t(r)_j\bigr)
       =J_{s,i}^\circ*\{a\}*J_{t,j}^\circ.
\]
For coincident coordinates in one hidden sort, both sides are the full
support of that equivalence, by equivalence reproduction. By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:ramsey-typed-distributivity} and~\eqref{eq:ramsey-clone-diversity-meet}, the intersection is
\[
     \{c_s\}*\{a\}*\{c_t\}=\{a\}.
\]
The last equality is the pure-clone rule: shifting either endpoint to a
fresh clone preserves its literal fine colour. For two equal visible tuples
the same calculation returns the clone marker $c_s$, which is interpreted as
\emph{visible equality}, not as a diversity edge. Distinct tuples of one
sort differ in at least one coordinate, and different visible sort tags
cannot represent one old point. All diagonal cases are thus separated.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Class CRT alone would not prove Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-singleton-recovery}. An equivalence reduct
can forget all RED/BLUE data. The full mixed cylinders and typed
meet-product distributivity are the load-bearing additions. It is important
to use the transition-enriched typed hidden language, not just the earliest
same-coordinate shadow presentation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The finite-tuple Ramsey readout interface}

Use the full-code construction of
\cref{lem:foundational-full-code-embeddings,thm:ramsey-tuple-readout}.
Here $\HH$ denotes the hereditary ordered hidden Ramsey class,
$N(D)$ consists of all tagged tuples satisfying the chosen quantifier-free
domains $\delta_s$, and $\KK$ is the hereditary age of their induced full
codes. All hidden unary/binary, equality and order codes on coordinate
terms are retained, including same-root instances and equalities between
different roots. Function domains are determined by sorts, so no further
definedness information is needed in this application. There is no
quotient of tuples by a further equivalence relation.

\begin{lemma}[Templates and all embeddings]\label{lem:ramsey-template-bijection}
The codes determine the generated hidden support $h(A^*)$ up to a unique
isomorphism over the root coordinates. For every $D\in\HH$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-embeddings}
       \Emb_{\KK}(A^*,N(D))\ \cong\ \Emb_{\HH}(h(A^*),D).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{lem:foundational-full-code-embeddings} for the chosen tuple
domains and the finite hidden unary-term normal forms. Cross-root equality
codes identify all repeated term presentations. The resulting Ramsey
transfer is exactly \cref{thm:ramsey-tuple-readout}; it does not require
visible SAP, a cofinality hypothesis or an independent order on tuple points.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Application and reasonable finite expansion}

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ramsey-tuple-readout}
to the PURE-IMAGINARY, unary-functionally
closed sort restriction of the terminal
clone source's V27 hidden ordered Ramsey
class. This restricted class is itself
hereditary Ramsey (and JEP/SAP) by
\cref{lem:foundational-ramsey-sort-restriction},
because no hidden upward map or quotient
transition from a proper quotient-coordinate
sort has target in a clone-real sort
(\cref{lem:no-real-algebraic-transition}).
Use the MI tuple families
\eqref{eq:ramsey-mi-meet} and the domains
of \cref{lem:ramsey-mi-domain}.
Every selected coordinate and every unary
transition term remains in this
pure-imaginary age. The finite unary term
bound thus applies to their coordinate
templates, while NO free ordering of deleted
clone-real hidden vertices is introduced
into the visible full-code interpretation.

For any hidden $D$, realise its unordered structure in the actual coordinate
system. Each valid tuple gives one actual $K$-class; all these classes coexist
in that same source. Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-singleton-recovery} shows that the resulting finite
$Q^\dagger$-structure is independent of this realisation and functorial on
embeddings. There is no further simultaneous-cell completion obligation.

Every finite original $Q$-structure has a safe terminal unary expansion. Its
actual coordinate template is finite and can be freely ordered, so the
construction is reduct-surjective. It is hereditary by definition. For
reasonableness, along an original embedding $A\to B$ first extend the safe
unary labels. Homogeneity and canonical quotient-term lifting give the
induced unexpanded template embedding $h(A)\to h(B)$. Extend the chosen
finite hidden order and read back the codes. This preserves the entire given
expansion of $A$, including comparisons on absent packet partners or other
term values.

Finite language gives precompactness, hidden JEP gives JEP, and a visible
automorphism extends uniquely to an automorphism of its finite ordered
template, hence is trivial. Theorem~\ref{thm:ramsey-tuple-readout} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:ramsey-general-ap}. If desired, hereditary Ramsey plus JEP supplies AP of
the visible expanded age. We do not assert SAP there.

\begin{corollary}[A functorial finite coordinate completion]
Put $C(A)=N(h(A))$. Then $A\subseteq C(A)$ and
\[
                      h(C(A))=h(A),\qquad C(C(A))=C(A).
\]
Every expanded embedding extends naturally through this completion. If each
root has at most $m$ coordinate-term values, then
\[
 |h(A)|\le m|A|,\qquad |N(D)|\le\sum_s|D|^{r_s}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All coordinates of a new tuple in $C(A)$ lie in $h(A)$, whose unary closure
is already complete. Conversely the old roots still generate $h(A)$.
Naturality follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-template-bijection}; the cardinality bounds count
term values and coordinate tuples. We do not identify this completion with
the full original algebraic closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Functorial sort-block normalisation]
\label{lem:ramsey-block-retract}
Let $\mathcal H^{<}$ be a Ramsey hereditary class whose finite hidden
sort predicates partition the universe and whose expanded order is an
arbitrary total order of all hidden points. Fix a total priority on the
hidden SORT SYMBOLS. Replace any order by the concatenation of its
within-sort restrictions in this fixed priority. Then the resulting
block-ordered hidden class is Ramsey.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The block normalisation keeps the unordered reducts and acts identically
on vertex maps. Every original embedding preserves the sort labels and the
within-sort orders, and therefore remains an embedding after
normalisation. Thus this is an idempotent functor whose fixed objects
are exactly the block-ordered structures. Their class is hereditary,
since restricting a block order preserves its fixed sort priority.
Apply \cref{lem:foundational-ramsey-normalization}. No surjectivity on
embedding sets is required.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A smaller visible signature]\label{cor:ramsey-small-language}
In the concrete application one can retain only the original fine colours,
the safe terminal unary labels, and finitely many coordinate-term order
comparisons. After fixed hidden-sort block normalisation, comparisons between
different hidden target sorts can also be omitted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every non-order atomic or equality code is invariant under
$\operatorname{Aut}(Q^\dagger)$: the automorphism lifts to the pure-clone
source and its canonical imaginary terms. Homogeneity of the complete finite
binary $Q^\dagger$ language therefore makes each such unary/binary code a
finite union of its atomic types. Deleting these definable predicates
preserves all embedding sets and the Ramsey arrows. We do not delete the
original fine colours.

For the final assertion use Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-block-retract}: replace a hidden order
by concatenation of fixed sort-symbol blocks, keeping each within-sort order.
This is an idempotent functor preserving embeddings, so its full image is
Ramsey. Apply the tuple readout there. Different-target-sort comparisons are
then fixed by the tags. Comparisons of terms from different visible sorts
must still be retained when their hidden target sorts agree.
\end{proof}



\subsection{MI and MR are the same Ramsey construction}
The proof above does not distinguish whether the original greatest
finite-class equivalence $F=\Sigma_Q(\Id)$ is meet-irreducible (MI)
or meet-reducible (MR).  The distinction affects only the number
$r_s$ of hidden coordinates necessary to represent one terminal
original vertex.  In the MI case the prior real-hull theorem
$\operatorname{acl}_Q(A)=F[A]$, together with the source-specific
phase splitting preserving quotient algebraicity, implies that
$K_s$ is itself represented MI after terminalisation; hence
$r_s=1$ and original vertices are literal hidden coordinates.
In MR, $r_s$ can exceed one; the compatibility formula from
Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-mi-domain} and the singleton reconstruction
Lemma~\ref{lem:ramsey-singleton-recovery} make the tuple a single
original vertex, with no separate cell-imaginary sort.

This is the exact mathematical reason that the pure-clone reduction
avoids the failure of hidden SAP for independently materialised
multi-parent cells: such cells are interpreted \emph{as tuples},
not added as a fresh hidden vertex with multiple outgoing parents.

\subsection{Scope of the Ramsey construction and expansion property}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ramsey-general-ap} proves the Ramsey property,
reasonableness and precompactness of the finite unary/binary
readout from compatible MI tuples. Its proof does \emph{not}
invoke the later EPPA construction or assume expansion property.
The additional \emph{expansion property} of the explicit
\emph{unfiltered} V27 safe unary labels and transition-synchronised
subquotient-order code is established separately in
\cref{sec:ep-exact-traces,sec:ep-automatic-core,sec:ep-unfiltered}.
The central new input is complete original-bipartite-atom
separation, \eqref{eq:ep-bp-separation}, which implies that the
terminal unary type table is always a core. Thus no finite
core retraction is needed for a genuine maximal-exact-character
tower.

This is not an application of Braunfeld's ordering property
for the bare generic $\Lambda$-ultrametric \emph{reduct}
\cite{Braunfeld}: the old fine binary colours and actual mixed
semantic cylinders are retained throughout the relative-reset
order argument. Finite-index sheet names must come from the
\emph{safe, globally compatible} parity splittings, not from
arbitrary independent local labellings. The construction does
not identify a canonical smallest language or the universal
minimal flow, and its in-house source dependencies still
require independent audit.
