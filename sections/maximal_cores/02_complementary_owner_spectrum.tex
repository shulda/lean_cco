\begin{lemma}[Complete complementary owner-pair spectrum]
\label{lem:acf-complementary-spectrum}
Keep the complementary exact quotients
\(Y=M/C\), \(X=M/K\), \(T=M/(C\vee K)\)
with \(C\cap K=\Id\). Let
\(B\subseteq T\) be a finite induced
set of ACTUAL owner classes, and let
\(P\subseteq X\) be a finite induced
structure projecting ONTO \(B\).
Assume \(P\) contains, for EVERY
ordered owner pair \((t,t')\in B^2\)
and every ACTUAL \(X\)-atomic orbital
\(b\) realized between some two
\(X\)-points above exactly \(t,t'\),
a pair \(p,p'\in P\) with owners
\(t,t'\) and orbital \(b\).
The diagonal \(b=\Id_X\) is included
when the two owner labels agree.

Let \(y,y'\in Y\) be ACTUAL DISTINCT
quotient points, with owners
\(t,t'\in B\), and let
\(q=\col_Y(y,y')\). Then as \(p,p'\)
range over all \(P\)-points with
owners \(t,t'\), the pairwise old
fine colours of the CRT points
\[
               (y,p),\ (y',p')
                   \quad\in M
\]
include EVERY original fine atomic
colour \(a\) lying over the quotient
orbital \(q\) (that is,
\(a\subseteq C;q;C\), using the
actual saturated old pullback).
In particular no individual
\(C\)-representative or \(K\)-representative
choice is assumed to realize all fine
colours at once.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an old atomic fine colour
\(a\subseteq\widetilde q\), where
\(\widetilde q=C;a;C\) is the old
pullback of \(q\).
Choose a real \(a\)-edge
\(u,v\) whose \(Y\)-quotient
pair has orbital \(q\).
The pair of \(T\)-owners
\((\pi_J(u),\pi_J(v))\), with
\(J=C\vee K\), has the same
actual \(T\)-pair orbital as
\((t,t')\), because both are
projections of a \(q\)-edge.
The \(T\)-orbital is defined by
the old \(\Aut(M)\) action, so
an OLD automorphism moves
those two owner classes to
\(t,t'\) simultaneously.
Applying it to \(u,v\)
produces an actual \(a\)-edge
\(u_1,v_1\) with owners
exactly \(t,t'\).

Let \(b\) be the \(X\)-orbital
of \((\pi_Ku_1,\pi_Kv_1)\).
The hypothesis on \(P\)
supplies \(p,p'\in P\)
with precisely those owners
and orbital \(b\).
By the exact CRT, the tuples
\((y,p)\) and \((y',p')\)
are ACTUAL real points of \(M\).
Their \(Y\)-pair orbital is
\(q\), and their \(X\)-pair
orbital is \(b\), exactly as
for \(u_1,v_1\). Since
the old fine-colour pullbacks
of those two orbital blocks
intersect in one actual atom
by \eqref{eq:colour-meet},
the old fine colour between
the tuples is \(a\).
The points are distinct because
\(y\ne y'\); this argument
also covers the case \(p=p'\)
of an \(X\)-diagonal pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[An opposite-coordinate diagonal is a genuine fine lift]
\label{rem:acf-opposite-diagonal-fine-lift}
In \cref{lem:acf-complementary-spectrum}
the two \(Y\)-coordinates \(y\ne y'\)
need NOT have distinct
\(X\)-coordinates \(p,p'\).
Equality \(p=p'\) is one of the
ACTUAL RAW \(X\)-pair orbitals
that must occur in the single
complementary helper spectrum.

For example take the original
Cartesian-phase source
\(M_*=\mathbb N^2\times\mathbb F_2\)
of \cref{ex:two-coordinate}.
Its EXACT row-and-phase and
column-and-phase kernels
\(C,K\) have
\(C\cap K=\Id\), while its
finite two-point phase packet
bottom \(F\) is NONEXACT.
Fix different rows \(r\ne r'\),
one column \(c\) and one phase
\(g\). The distinct old real
points \((r,c,g)\),
\((r',c,g)\) have DIFFERENT
\(Y=M_*/C\)-coordinates
but the SAME \(X=M_*/K\)
coordinate. Their old fine
diversity atom is thus
witnessed over the ACTUAL
\(X\)-DIAGONAL pair \(p=p'\).
Discarding diagonal \(X\)
orbital witnesses because
the original real endpoints
are distinct would omit
a genuine fine lift and
invalidate full-support
\(S_Y\)-safety. No exactness
of the unrelated finite
bottom \(F\) is used.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[A non-Boolean actual exact owner split with diagonal lifts]
\label{ex:acf-nonboolean-phase-safety}
In the ACTUAL Cartesian-phase
homogeneous AP+semantic-CCO
source $M_*=\mathbb N^2\times\mathbb F_2$
of \cref{ex:two-coordinate},
the named fine atomic colour
of two distinct points
$(r,c,g),(r',c',g')$
records exactly
$\mathbf 1_{r=r'}$,
$\mathbf 1_{c=c'}$
and $g+g'$.
Let $C$ be SAME ROW AND
SAME PHASE, and $K$ SAME
COLUMN AND SAME PHASE.
Then $C,K$ are FULL RAW exact,
$C\cap K=\Id$ and
$J=C\vee K$ is SAME PHASE,
strictly below $\Top$.
Indeed the old atomic row-edge
with zero phase difference
has represented square
exactly $C$, and similarly
the column-edge square is
$K$; their RAW product is J.

The ORIGINAL FULL RAW exact
invariant-equivalence lattice
is the genuinely NONBOOLEAN
five-element distributive
lattice
\[
          \Id<C,K<J<\Top,
 \qquad C\not\le K,\quad
        K\not\le C.
\]
Every nonempty old atomic
word forces ONE endpoint
phase difference, so every
PROPER full RAW exact
equivalence, which must
contain real equality,
preserves phase.
Within each phase layer,
the invariance and
transitivity of an
equivalence on the
Cartesian grid give
ONLY equality, same row,
same column, or the
whole phase layer:
if an equivalence relates
a pair differing in BOTH
coordinates, its
two-edge compositions
generate row and column
pairs; if it contains
both row and column
differences, their
composition generates
every pair.

Here $Y=M_*/C$ has
points $(r,g)$,
$X=M_*/K$ has
points $(c,g)$, and
$T=M_*/J$ has the
TWO phase owners $g$.
Consider the ORIGINAL
diversity safe palette
$S=\{a\in\mathcal D(M_*):
                       a\not\subseteq F\}$,
where $F$ is the
two-point thin
same-$(r,c)$ packet
equivalence. This is
a SET OF OLD DIVERSITY
ATOM NAMES, not a
set-theoretic difference
between a palette and a
binary relation. Thus
S consists precisely
of old fine diversity
atoms for which
$r\ne r'$ OR $c\ne c'$.
It has strong closed
amalgamation. In fact
every finite nonempty
absolutely acl-closed
real set is a COMPLETE
rectangle $R_0\times
C_0\times\mathbb F_2$:
the original
\(\Aut(M_*)=
 \operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb N)
 \times\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb N)
 \times\mathbb F_2\)
stabiliser fixes the
finitely named rows and
columns, and every
corner of the resulting
rectangle has finite
orbit. To strongly
amalgamate closed spans,
rename all row coordinates
new to the two sides
disjointly, and do
likewise with all new
column coordinates;
all new cross pairs
then have at least
one different coordinate,
so their literal old
fine colour lies in S.
For the empty base,
choose disjoint finite
row and column sets
for the two factors.

The FULL-support
projected safe palette
on $Y$ is
\[
 S_Y=\{\,q\in\mathcal D(Y):
       \text{the two }Y\text{-points
       have DIFFERENT rows}\,\}.
\]
For different rows,
EVERY old fine lift
belongs to S; for equal
rows the two distinct
$Y$-points have opposite
phases and the lift with
the SAME column is the
unsafe thin phase flip.
This $S_Y$ strongly
amalgamates finite
absolutely closed
$Y$-rectangles
$R_0\times\mathbb F_2$,
by making all newly
introduced row values
disjoint.

The common owner set
$B=T=\mathbb F_2$
is nonempty and
absolutely acl-closed.
Take finite sets of
rows $R_0$ and columns
$C_0$, and the FULL
acl-closed complementary
helper
$P=C_0\times\mathbb F_2$
in X. The full rectangle
$D\times_B P$ for
$D=R_0\times\mathbb F_2$
is exactly
$R_0\times C_0\times
\mathbb F_2$, independently
acl-closed by
\cref{lem:acf-finite-exact-closed-preimages}.

Finally take $r\ne r'$
with one common
phase $g$ and fix a
column c. The DISTINCT
old points
$(r,c,g),(r',c,g)$
have different $Y$-classes
but IDENTICAL $X$-classes
$p=p'=(c,g)$. Their
old atom $(0,1,0)$
can be witnessed among
the helper lifts ONLY
using this ACTUAL
X-diagonal orbital.
Discarding diagonal
X witnesses would
therefore destroy the
full fine-lift spectrum
\cref{lem:acf-complementary-spectrum},
even though original
real endpoints are
distinct. This is a
NONBOOLEAN exact-lattice
test, not an assumption
that the two quotient
coordinates are independent
or that original
$M_*$ has SAP.
\end{example}
