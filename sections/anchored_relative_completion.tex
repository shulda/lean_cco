\section{Anchored relative completion over actual exact owners}
\label{sec:anchored-relative}

The conditional groupoid by itself does not give completions of
parcels with prescribed owner labels.  We next prove the missing
\emph{anchored} assertion without assuming SAP.  In this section
$E$ is any nontrivial \emph{reachable} invariant equivalence, meaning
$E=a_1\cdots a_m$ is a represented positive word profile.  It need
not be minimal infinite until we invoke the previous chapter.
Write $Q=M/E$ and $\pi:M\to Q$ for the actual quotient map, whose
binary orbital language is the exact quotient language of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-sorts-source}.  For a raw atom $a$, write
$\overline a=EaE$ for the pullback of the corresponding quotient
orbital support.  This may contain several actual fine raw atoms.

A finite raw \emph{partial edge-coloured diagram} $P$ has distinct
formal vertices and at most one specified diversity colour on each
unordered pair.  A \emph{quotient assignment} for $P$ is a map
$\lambda:V(P)\to Q$.  It is \emph{edge-compatible} if
\[
  xy\text{ is specified with colour }a
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  (\lambda(x),\lambda(y))\in\overline a.
\]
The map $\lambda$ need not be injective; equal assigned quotient
points mean equal \emph{owners}, not identification of real points.

\begin{lemma}[Closed owner walks and saturated word products]
\label{lem:anchored-closed-walk}
Let $E$ be an invariant equivalence on the transitive real source
$M$.  Let $W=w_1\cdots w_m$ be a represented product whose factors
are raw atomic relations or the full relation $E$, and suppose at
least one factor is $E$.  Suppose there are actual $E$-classes
$X_0,\ldots,X_m=X_0$ such that for every atomic factor $w_i=a$
there is an $a$-edge from $X_{i-1}$ to $X_i$, while for every
factor $w_i=E$ one has $X_{i-1}=X_i$.  Then
$\Id\subseteq W$.  If every $E$-factor is expanded as a
positive word of raw atoms representing $E$, the expanded closed
raw word admits a colour-preserving homomorphism to $M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a raw atomic factor put $C_i=EaE$ and for an $E$-factor put
$C_i=E$.  Each $C_i$ is saturated in both variables under $E$.
The stated owner compatibility means
$X_{i-1}\times X_i\subseteq C_i$.  Choose arbitrary real points
$u_i\in X_i$ with $u_m=u_0$.  Then
\[
 (u_0,u_0)\in C_1\cdots C_m=EWE.
\]
Here the equality is the represented relational-product identity:
$E^2=E$, and all invariant binary relations commute because all
raw orbitals are symmetric.  As $W$ already has an $E$-factor,
$EWE=W$.  Thus $(u_0,u_0)\in W$.  The source is transitive and
$W$ invariant, hence $\Id\subseteq W$.  The definition of the
represented product now gives a closed walk with these prescribed
factors.  If an $E$-factor arose from a positive raw word, expanding
that segment supplies the corresponding raw path.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anchored relative completion]
\label{thm:anchored-relative}
Suppose $E$ is reachable.  Let $P$ be a finite raw partial diagram
admitting a colour-preserving homomorphism to $M$, and let
$\lambda:V(P)\to Q$ be edge-compatible.  Then there is a
colour-preserving homomorphism $f:P\to M$ with
$\pi f=\lambda$.

More precisely, if $A\subseteq V(P)$ induces a \emph{complete}
raw substructure and $j:A\hookrightarrow M$ is an embedding with
$\pi j=\lambda|_A$, then $f$ can be chosen to extend $j$.
If moreover $\Age(M)$ has SAP and every $E$-class is infinite,
$f$ can be chosen injective.  No SAP is assumed for the first two
assertions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the complete anchored substructure $A$ and its embedding $j$;
for the unanchored case take $A=\varnothing$.  Since $P$ already has a
homomorphism to $M$, its restriction to $A$ is an embedding (every
distinct pair in $A$ has a specified diversity colour).  Homogeneity
of $M$ lets us postcompose this homomorphism by an automorphism
so that it agrees with $j$ on $A$.  Denote the resulting homomorphism
by $f_0$.

Let $B=\lambda(V(P))$, a finite set of actual $E$-owners.  For each
$b\in B$ choose one real representative $u_b\in\pi^{-1}(b)$, using
an already embedded point $j(a)$ if $b=\lambda(a)$ for some $a\in A$.
Add these representatives to $j(A)$ and retain their complete
\emph{actual} raw induced diagram $T$; its intersection with $P$
consists precisely of the already anchored vertices $A$.  In the
augmented partial graph $P\cup_A T$, for each $x\in P\setminus A$
attach a private path from $x$ to $u_{\lambda(x)}$ whose edge-colour
word represents $E$.  Since $E>\Id$, the chosen positive word may be taken
to have only diversity atoms (delete any equality factors).
All intermediate path vertices are fresh and are used by no other
path; a one-edge word needs no intermediate vertex.

We check that each specified simple cycle of this augmentation has
a homomorphism to $M$.  A simple cycle containing no private path
lies in $P\cup_A T$ and is realised by $f_0$ on $P$ and the identity
on $T$, since they agree on the complete common substructure $A$.
A cycle using a private path traverses that entire path, because
its interior vertices have degree two.  Regard each such path as
one factor $E$ of the represented cyclic word $W$, keeping all
other specified edges as their original atomic factors.  Give each
vertex of $P$ its prescribed quotient owner and each vertex of $T$
its actual owner.  Along a private $E$-path the two endpoint owners
are equal; along a specified edge of $P$ the owner pair belongs to
$EaE$ by edge compatibility; and along an edge of $T$ this is
true because the edge is actually present.  Thus the marked owner
labels constitute a closed walk satisfying the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchored-closed-walk}.  That lemma gives
$\Id\subseteq W$, so the specified cycle has a homomorphism to
$M$, with the positive raw word for each private $E$-factor
expanded again into its original path.  Notice that this does not
assert that independently chosen representatives of the successive
owners realize the individual raw colours: they only realize the
$E$-saturated relations, and the displayed lemma performs the
necessary synchronisation.

Semantic homomorphic CCO applied to the whole augmented partial
diagram gives a homomorphism to $M$.  Its restriction to the complete
anchor diagram $T$ is an embedding.  By homogeneity we may
postcompose by an ambient automorphism to make it the identity on
$T$.  Each private path then forces the image of its initial real
vertex $x$ to be $E$-related to $u_{\lambda(x)}$.  Hence the
restriction $f$ to $P$ satisfies $\pi f=\lambda$ and fixes $A$.

Now assume SAP and infinite $E$-classes.  We first justify the
relative orbit property needed for collision removal.  For every
finite real set $C$, SAP implies that any $v\notin C$ has infinite
$\Aut(M/C)$-orbit: take arbitrarily many strong amalgams over $C$
of the one-point extension $C\cup\{v\}$, embed the finite result in
$M$, and use homogeneity to move $v$ between its copies while fixing
$C$.  For finitely many infinite owners $B$, choose a real
representative of each outside $C\cup\{v\}$.  Any automorphism fixing
those representatives also fixes all the corresponding $E$-owners.
Thus the orbit of $v$ over $C$ and the named owners $B$ remains
infinite.

If two formal vertices of $P$ have the same image under $f$, choose
one such vertex $x\notin A$.  No displayed edge from $x$ meets a
vertex with the same image, since every displayed edge has an
irreflexive diversity colour.  Let $C$ be \emph{only} the finite set of images of displayed
neighbours of $x$; it does not contain $f(x)$.  We must \emph{not}
include all anchored vertices in $C$, because $f(x)$ might collide
with an anchored vertex to which $x$ has no specified edge.
The orbit of $f(x)$ over $C$ and all named owners $B$ is infinite.
Choose a new member of this orbit outside the entire finite current
image of $P$, and change \emph{only} the image of $x$ (rather than
applying the corresponding automorphism to the whole diagram).
The displayed colours, the prescribed owner $\lambda(x)$, and
all images of $A$ are preserved.  This strictly decreases collisions.  Repeating finitely
many times produces an injective $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact caps of completion spectra]
\label{cor:relative-cap}
Let $D_{xy}$ be the set of possible raw colours on a currently
unspecified pair $x,y$ in completions of a finite raw partial
diagram $P$ in $M$, where the completions are allowed to be
homomorphic (and equality is treated by identification).  Let
$C_{xy}$ be the raw pullback of the unique actual quotient orbital
from $\lambda(x)$ to $\lambda(y)$.  If $P$ has a raw completion
and $\lambda$ is edge-compatible, then the colours possible on
$x,y$ \emph{with the prescribed owner assignment} are exactly
\[
                         D_{xy}\cap C_{xy}.
\]
Moreover all quotient owner caps have a common completion.  Under
SAP and infinite fibres the same holds for injective completions,
with the diversity-colour interpretation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is immediate.  Given any $a\in D_{xy}\cap C_{xy}$,
add the specified edge $xy=a$ to $P$.  By the definition of
$D_{xy}$, the new partial diagram is raw-completable; by
$a\in C_{xy}$ it remains edge-compatible with $\lambda$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-relative}.  Apply that theorem
to the original $P$ to obtain one common capped completion.
The injective statement uses its SAP clause.  Equality, if allowed
in the homomorphic spectrum, is represented by identifying the
corresponding formal vertices rather than by adding a diversity
edge between them.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Joint exact owner caps are governed by the raw cycles]
\label{prop:joint-owner-caps}
Assume $E$ is reachable and retain an edge-compatible quotient
assignment $\lambda$ for a finite raw partial diagram $P$.
Let $S$ be any collection of its presently unspecified unordered
pairs, and prescribe a diversity colour $a_{xy}$ on each pair
$xy\in S$.  Write $P_S$ for the diagram with all these colours
added simultaneously.  If a complete induced subdiagram
$A\subseteq P$ is already embedded by $j:A\hookrightarrow M$
with $\pi j=\lambda|_A$, retain that anchor (and otherwise take
$A=\varnothing$).
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is a colour-preserving homomorphism
$f:P_S\to M$ extending $j$ and satisfying $\pi f=\lambda$.
\item Every newly prescribed $a_{xy}$ is compatible with the
actual owner pair $(\lambda(x),\lambda(y))$, and every
specified simple cycle of the \emph{whole augmented diagram}
$P_S$ admits a raw colour-preserving homomorphism to $M$.
\end{enumerate}
Under the additional SAP and infinite-$E$-class hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-relative}, ``homomorphism'' in (1)
may be strengthened to ``embedding'' (for diversity constraints).
Thus a product of the \emph{individual} capped edge spectra is
in general only an upper bound on the joint completion spectrum;
the remaining compatibility constraints are exactly those of raw
semantic CCO, not an independent choice on each edge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Necessity follows by restricting the single realised diagram
to its edges and cycles.  Conversely, (2) and semantic raw CCO
give a colour-preserving homomorphism of $P_S$ to $M$.
All displayed edges of $P_S$ are compatible with $\lambda$:
this holds for the old edges by hypothesis and for the new
edges by (2).  Apply the anchored relative completion theorem
to $P_S$, with the same complete anchor $j$.  Its optional
injectivity clause gives the final assertion.  No choice of
independent edge completions or extra SAP is made in the basic
implication.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A four-cycle blocks independently admissible caps]
\label{ex:joint-cap-four-cycle}
In the Cartesian phase model of Example~\ref{ex:two-coordinate},
take $E$ to be equality of the row and phase coordinates;
it is reachable, since the square of the colour
$(\text{same row},\text{different column},\text{same phase})$
is exactly $E$.  Let $x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3$ be formal vertices with
four pairwise distinct prescribed row owners and the same
phase.  Between two different row owners at the same phase,
both the same-column colour $b$ and the different-column
colour $d$ are individually possible.  Nevertheless the four prescriptions
\[
 x_0x_1=b,\qquad x_1x_2=b,\qquad
 x_2x_3=b,\qquad x_3x_0=d
\]
have no common completion: the first three force all four
column coordinates to agree, contradicting the last.  Every
proper subfamily of these four constraints is realizable with
the specified owners.  There is no specified triangle;
the obstructing cycle has length four.  Thus owner-wise
edge compatibility, nonempty individual capped spectra and
checks confined to triangles cannot replace the cyclic
condition in Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-owner-caps}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Actual finite-owner typed source]
\label{prop:anchored-typed-source}
For a finite actual induced quotient substructure $B\subseteq Q$,
let $N_B$ be the raw induced structure on $\pi^{-1}(B)$ with one
named unary owner predicate for each $b\in B$.  Then $N_B$ is
homogeneous, and its finite complete structures are exactly the
raw triangle-legal diagrams with owner assignments in $B$ that
are edge-compatible with all prescribed raw colours.  Its age has
AP and semantic typed homomorphic CCO.  If the original raw age
has SAP and the $E$-classes are infinite, the typed age has SAP.

Further, writing $\Gamma_B=\Aut(B)$, every finite raw partial
isomorphism $p$ of $M$ whose domain and range lie in
$\pi^{-1}(B)$ and for which a prescribed $\gamma\in\Gamma_B$
satisfies $\pi\circ p=\gamma\circ\pi$ on its domain extends
to an ambient automorphism of $M$ inducing $\gamma$ on the
entire finite set of owners $B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the extension assertion, take a finite complete raw structure
$C\subseteq\pi^{-1}(B)$ containing the domain of $p$ and one
representative of every owner in $B$.  The assignment $\lambda=\gamma\circ\pi$
on $C$ is edge-compatible: $\gamma$ preserves the actual quotient
orbital of each pair, while each raw edge belongs to its quotient
pullback.  The restriction of $p$ to its complete domain is a
compatible anchored embedding.  Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-relative}
therefore gives a homomorphic completion of $C$ with precisely this
quotient assignment and extending $p$.  Since $C$ is complete, it is
an embedding.  Homogeneity extends it to an ambient automorphism,
and the chosen representatives force its action on \emph{all} owners
in $B$ to be $\gamma$.  Taking $\gamma=\mathrm{id}_B$ shows that
every partial automorphism of the named unary expansion extends,
hence $N_B$ is homogeneous.

Every finite complete raw triangle-legal diagram embeds into $M$
by the standing triangle-defined source hypothesis.  If its given
owner assignment is edge-compatible, the anchored theorem re-embeds
it into $N_B$.  This proves the description of its age, and AP
follows from homogeneity.  A typed partial diagram with raw
cycle-consistency has a raw homomorphic completion by semantic CCO,
and the anchored theorem forces its owner assignment, proving typed
CCO.  If raw SAP is available, the union of two finite typed
diagrams over a common complete typed subdiagram is raw-completable
by SAP; apply the injective anchored theorem to obtain a typed
strong amalgam.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Prescribed finite-language-action EPPA]
\label{def:gamma-eppa}
For $\Gamma_B=\Aut(B)$, each $\gamma\in\Gamma_B$ acts on the
language of $N_B$ by sending the owner predicate $P_b$ to
$P_{\gamma b}$ and fixing every original named binary raw colour.
A \emph{$\Gamma_B$-partial automorphism} of a finite typed $A$
is a pair $(p,\gamma)$ where $p$ is a partial bijection that
preserves all raw colours and sends each typed vertex in $P_b$
to a vertex in $P_{\gamma b}$.  A \emph{$\Gamma_B$-EPPA witness}
for $A$ is a single finite typed extension $W$ in the same age
such that every such pair extends to an automorphism of the
\emph{language-action structure}: there is a bijection $\widehat p$
of $W$ extending $p$, preserving raw colours and satisfying
$\widehat p(P_b^W)=P_{\gamma b}^W$ for all $b$.  The choices
for different pairs need not be coherent.  The typed age is closed
under the indicated finite language action, since the
actual quotient orbital caps are preserved by $\gamma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional transfer of ordinary EPPA across $E$]
\label{thm:relative-transfer}
Suppose the exact quotient age $\Age(Q)$ has ordinary EPPA.
Suppose, for every finite actual $B\subseteq Q$, the typed age
$\Age(N_B)$ has $\Gamma_B$-EPPA for the finite language action
which permutes its unary owner names by $\Gamma_B=\Aut(B)$
and fixes \emph{each} original raw colour name.  Then
$\Age(M)$ has ordinary EPPA.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $A\subseteq M$ be finite.  Take one finite quotient EPPA
witness $B\subseteq Q$ containing $\pi(A)$.  Every real partial
automorphism $p$ of $A$ induces a partial isomorphism of the
quotient image $\pi(A)$, because the exact relation $E$ and all
its quotient orbitals are invariant raw unions.  Choose
$\gamma_p\in\Aut(B)$ extending this induced partial map.
Regard $A$ as a typed member of $\Age(N_B)$ with unary labels
$\pi|_A$.  Each $(p,\gamma_p)$ is a legitimate language-group
partial isomorphism of this typed input.  Take a \emph{single}
finite $\Gamma_B$-EPPA witness which extends all such pairs.
Forget the owner predicates.  The language action does not change
any raw fine colour, so the reduct is a finite raw EPPA witness
for $A$.  No coherent choice of the $\gamma_p$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
\label{rem:relative-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-relative} uses only raw semantic
homomorphic CCO, exact quotient geometry and triangle-definedness
up to its final injectivity clause.  The stronger raw SAP assumption
there is \emph{not} part of the standing main EPPA theorem; it is
used only later for an actual \emph{closed} transversal which has
independently been proved to admit strong amalgamation.  Likewise
Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-transfer} is a conditional reduction, not
a theorem asserting $\Gamma_B$-EPPA for arbitrary typed sources.
The latter is constructed subsequently by the HKN/selector
argument, with all mixed fine colours present.
\end{remark}
