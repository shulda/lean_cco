
\begin{lemma}[AP and semantic homomorphic CCO imply triangle fullness]
\label{lem:cco-triangle-fullness}
Let $\mathcal K$ be a hereditary class of finite complete atomic
binary-coloured structures with AP and semantic homomorphic CCO.  If
all induced substructures of a finite complete $A$ on at most three
vertices belong to $\mathcal K$, then $A\in\mathcal K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider a specified simple cycle $v_0v_1\cdots v_{m-1}v_0$
of the complete diagram $A$, $m\ge3$.  The triangles
$A|\{v_0,v_i,v_{i+1}\}$ are in $\mathcal K$ for $1\le i\le m-2$.
Starting with the first triangle, amalgamate the next triangle over
the already realised \emph{complete} edge $v_0v_i$, inductively.
Each triangle specifies its next cycle edge and the next chord to
$v_0$.  AP is sufficient: a new formal vertex may be identified with
an earlier non-neighbour along the cycle.  Thus the final amalgam
receives a colour-preserving \emph{homomorphism} of this cycle,
including the closing edge.  No injectivity of this cycle map is
needed or asserted.

Every simple cycle of $A$ is therefore homomorphically realisable.
Semantic CCO yields a colour-preserving homomorphism of the whole
complete $A$ into a member of $\mathcal K$.  This map is injective,
since each distinct pair of $A$ bears an irreflexive diversity colour,
and it is an induced embedding because all pairs have exactly one
named atom.  Heredity gives $A\in\mathcal K$.
The at-most-three-point premise also handles the boundary cases.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The lemma does not claim that a \emph{partial} triangle-consistent
diagram completes.  It uses the actual chord colours of the
\emph{complete} input in the cycle-fan amalgams.  In our homogeneous
source AP is automatic, so triangle-definedness is a consequence of
semantic homomorphic CCO, not an extra substantive assumption.
\end{remark}
