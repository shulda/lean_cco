\section{Stationary independence with no finite relative equivalence covers}
\label{sec:sir-selector}

\begin{theorem}[SIR in the no-finite-cover-index SAP case]
\label{thm:sir-no-finite-cover}
Let \(M\) be a countable transitive homogeneous structure in a
finite complete atomic symmetric binary language. Suppose
\(\Age(M)\) has strong amalgamation (SAP) and semantic
homomorphic simple-cycle CCO. Assume that for every pair
\(E<F\) of \(\Aut(M)\)-invariant equivalence relations,
the relative index
\[
       [F:E]=\#\{E\text{-classes contained in one }F\text{-class}\}
\]
is infinite. Then \(M\) admits an \(\Aut(M)\)-invariant
\emph{stationary independence relation on all finite real sets},
including independence over the empty base.

In fact, the same conclusion holds for a finite-sorted,
named-sort homogeneous symmetric SAP+CCO structure as soon as
every named sort satisfies \emph{(NR2)}: no proper invariant
equivalence interval of relative index two. The independence
is given explicitly by the selected strong exact base-path
domains in \eqref{eq:sir-base-domain}--\eqref{eq:sir-independence}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The no-finite-relative-index hypothesis, taken in the
\emph{full lattice} of invariant equivalences (not merely
its exact sublattice), implies (NR2) on the one sort.
Apply the joint hereditary selector theorem
\cref{thm:typed-HS}; the source has the required strong
typed exact-domain calculus because it has SAP and CCO.
The selected-amalgam construction and the complete
verification of the SIR axioms are given below in
\cref{lem:sir-free-span-PC,prop:sir-HS-amalgam,thm:sir-HS-positive}.
The named-sorted assertion is precisely the (NR2)
hypothesis of those results; automorphisms preserve
all named sort tags.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Precise boundary of the result]
\label{rem:sir-positive-boundary}
The hypothesis is a prohibition of \emph{finite relative
indices between any two distinct invariant equivalences};
it is not just the absence of a finite \emph{global}
quotient. We assume SAP of the actual age on the
\emph{real} vertices; AP and CCO alone do not imply SAP,
even without finite relative indices. In the stronger
named-sorted version, only (NR2) is needed and it
is checked inside \emph{each named sort}, while the
language keeps those unary sort names. The theorem
does not assert a SIR on an unlabelled reduct obtained
by forgetting the sort names or on a general AP-only
exact quotient. Nor does it use the Ramsey, expansion
property or EPPA conclusions of the manuscript.
\end{remark}

Here we work in the \emph{strong} typed setting of
\cref{sec:typed-strong,sec:typed-selectors}, not in the
raw equality-inclusive AP-only quotient calculus.
Let \(U\) be the homogeneous limit of a finite-sorted
complete atomic symmetric binary triangle Fra\"iss\'e
class with SAP and semantic homomorphic CCO.
Assume (NR2): no named sort has a proper
invariant-equivalence interval of relative index two.
A stronger no-finite-relative-index hypothesis
certainly implies (NR2). By
\cref{thm:typed-HS} there is one
\emph{joint hereditary selector}
\[
 h_{ij}:\mathcal H_{ij}\longrightarrow L_{ij}
\]
on the original \emph{strong diversity} exact families,
such that \(h(D)\in D\), \(h(\bar D)=\overline{h(D)}\),
and \(h\) preserves \emph{all three} typed PC inclusions.
In addition,
\[
 E\subseteq D,\qquad h(D)\in E
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(E)=h(D).
 \tag{HS}\label{eq:sir-HS}
\]
No equivariance under an arbitrary permutation
of \emph{sort names} is required: the actual
language automorphisms preserve the named sorts
and old atomic relation symbols.

For finite complete induced \(A,B\) with a
common induced substructure \(C\), first take
disjoint formal copies over \(C\), so
\(A\cap B=C\). For \(a\in A\setminus C\)
and \(b\in B\setminus C\) define the
\emph{exact base-path menu}
\[
 D_C(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_{\operatorname{sort}(a),\operatorname{sort}(b)},
       &C=\varnothing,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle\bigcap_{c\in C}
 \left(
 \{\col(a,c)\}*\{\col(c,b)\}
 \right), &C\ne\varnothing .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{SIR-D}\label{eq:sir-base-domain}
\]
All products in this section are \emph{strong}
typed products of diversity atoms. Since
\(a,b\notin C\), every displayed edge to \(C\)
is a genuine diversity edge, even if its
endpoint sort labels happen to coincide.

\begin{lemma}[The strong free-span domain network is PC]
\label{lem:sir-free-span-PC}
Every \(D_C(a,b)\) is a nonempty member of the
original strong exact family
\(\mathcal H_{\operatorname{sort}(a),\operatorname{sort}(b)}\).
Put singleton literal domains on all pairs
inside \(A\) or \(B\), and \(D_C(a,b)\)
on the cross pairs. Every triple of
\emph{distinct} vertices of \(A\cup B\)
satisfies all three typed PC inclusions
\eqref{eq:typed-PC}. In particular, this
claim also holds for the empty base.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each factor in \eqref{eq:sir-base-domain}
is a product of atomic singleton exact
menus and therefore is exact. Strongly
amalgamate \(A,B\) over \(C\) using SAP.
Every actual cross edge in that amalgam
belongs to \emph{all} displayed factors,
by the triangles through \(C\), so the
intersection is nonempty and exact.
For an empty base the top is exact.

Triangles entirely within \(A\) or
within \(B\) are literal legal triangles.
For \(a\in A\setminus C\),
\(b\in B\setminus C\), and
\(c\in C\), write
\(u=\col(a,c)\), \(v=\col(c,b)\),
\(D=D_C(a,b)\). By its definition,
\(D\subseteq\{u\}*\{v\}\).
The other two PC inclusions hold because
\emph{some} chosen strong amalgam
realises a cross atom \(d\in D\)
and the triangle \(u,v,d\).
All singleton coordinates are
therefore supported.

For the only other mixed triangle
type take \(a,a'\in A\setminus C\),
\(b\in B\setminus C\), and let
\(p=\col(a,a')\).
In the old triangle \(a,a',c\),
\(\col(a,c)\in\{p\}*\{\col(a',c)\}\)
for every \(c\in C\).
Associativity and finite-meet
distributivity for the
\emph{nonempty exact} menus give
\[
\begin{split}
 D_C(a,b)
 &\subseteq
 \bigcap_{c\in C}
 \bigl(\{p\}*
       (\{\col(a',c)\}*\{\col(c,b)\})\bigr)\\
 &=\{p\}*D_C(a',b).
\end{split}
 \tag{SIR-PC1}\label{eq:sir-PC-first}
\]
The reverse-support inclusion is
\[
   D_C(a',b)\subseteq\{\bar p\}*D_C(a,b),
\]
proved by interchanging \(a,a'\) and
taking the \emph{typed converse}.
The converse cannot be omitted
when \(a,a'\) have different
named sorts.
Finally a strong amalgam witnesses
two actual cross colours
\(d\in D_C(a,b)\) and
\(d'\in D_C(a',b)\) with
\(p\in\{d\}*\{\bar d'\}\);
hence \(p\) belongs to the
appropriately typed product of
the two cross domains. These
are exactly all three PC
coordinates. The analogous case
with two vertices of \(B\setminus C\)
follows by converse and symmetry.

If \(C=\varnothing\), the same
argument uses the \emph{strong}
top absorption identity
\(\{p\}*L=L\), proved using SAP
in \cref{lem:typed-exact-products};
the third coordinate is again
witnessed by a strong amalgam.
Repeated \emph{sort labels} cause
no change: all actual vertices of
a mixed triple remain distinct.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical strong independent amalgam]
\label{prop:sir-HS-amalgam}
There is one finite complete amalgam
\(A\otimes_C^h B\) on the literal
disjoint union over \(C\), agreeing
with the two input structures and
assigning
\[
          \col(a,b)=h(D_C(a,b))
       \quad(a\in A\setminus C,\ b\in B\setminus C).
 \tag{SIR-A}\label{eq:sir-HS-amalgam}
\]
It lies in \(\Age(U)\).
It depends only on the two
complete finite structures and
their \emph{named} common base
\(C\), is natural under embeddings
of the ENTIRE SPAN that preserve
that specified common base as
the exact intersection of the
two factors, commutes with
converse symmetry, and uses
no linear ordering of vertices.
We do NOT claim that enlarging
the base from \(C\) to
\(C'\supsetneq C\) leaves the
selected cross colour unchanged:
the exact base-path menu can
shrink. Its behaviour under
base enlargement is precisely
the survivor-heredity calculation
in the transitivity proof below.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The selector fixes each atomic
singleton and preserves all
typed PC triples by
\cref{thm:typed-HS}.
Apply it to the network of
\cref{lem:sir-free-span-PC}.
All three singleton output domains
on a triple form a legal atomic
triangle; all old triangles and
literal input pair atoms are fixed.
The resulting complete labelled
structure is triangle-legal,
hence belongs to the
triangle-defined age of \(U\).
Converses of new cross colours
agree by converse compatibility.
The exact menu \eqref{eq:sir-base-domain}
is defined solely from the two
finite input types and \(C\);
no arbitrary choice of a strong
amalgam used in the preceding
\emph{existence proof} enters the
resulting output. Its formula
commutes with embeddings of the
input span.
\end{proof}

For arbitrary finite real subsets
\(A,B,C\subseteq U\), interpret
\(A\downarrow_C^h B\) by first
adjoining \(C\) to both sides.
For convenience suppose
\(C\subseteq A\cap B\). Define
\[
 A\downarrow_C^h B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A\cap B=C\quad\text{and}\quad
 \col(a,b)=h(D_C(a,b))
 \quad(a\in A\setminus C,\ b\in B\setminus C).
 \tag{SIR-I}\label{eq:sir-independence}
\]
When applying this notation to
\(A,B,C\) without either inclusion,
use \((A\cup C)\downarrow_C^h(B\cup C)\).

\begin{theorem}[The hereditary selected amalgam is stationary]
\label{thm:sir-HS-positive}
Under the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:typed-HS}, the relation
\(\downarrow^h\) from
\eqref{eq:sir-independence} is an
\emph{Aut-invariant stationary independence
relation on finite real vertex sets}.
It satisfies symmetry, existence,
monotonicity, both directions of
transitivity, and stationarity.
Consequently every finite-sorted
symmetric complete atomic SAP+
homomorphic-CCO class with no proper
finite-relative-index invariant
equivalence cover admits a SIR.
In particular, any named terminal SAP source
constructed in Part~III with homomorphic CCO
and (NR2) in each of its actual named sorts
satisfies this theorem. This is conditional
on the terminal \emph{age itself} having SAP;
neither terminality nor reasonable unary
expansion alone is being used to infer SAP.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Invariance and symmetry.}
Atomic pair colours and the named
sort tags are preserved by old
automorphisms. Hence the exact
menus \(D_C(a,b)\), and their
fixed named selector values, are
invariant. The identity
\(D_C(b,a)=\overline{D_C(a,b)}\),
together with converse compatibility
of \(h\), gives symmetry.

\emph{Existence.}
For finite \(A,B,C\) with the
same prescribed induced base
\(C\), first take a formal
copy of \(A\) over \(C\) disjoint
from \(B\) outside \(C\).
Use \cref{prop:sir-HS-amalgam}
to construct the finite
complete canonical strong amalgam.
It belongs to the age, and the
Fra\"iss\'e extension property
realises it over the
\emph{fixed} actual copy of \(B\)
inside \(U\). Its image of the
formal \(A\)-copy is independent
from \(B\) over \(C\) and has
the required type over \(C\).
No condition of algebraically
closed base is being hidden;
SAP supplies the literal strong
amalgam on these real vertices.

\emph{Monotonicity.}
Dropping vertices from one
independent side keeps each
remaining cross equality
\eqref{eq:sir-independence}
unchanged. More generally,
the forward direction of
transitivity below also proves
the standard base-monotonicity
axiom.

\emph{Transitivity.}
We verify, with the standard
identification of subsets with
their unions with the base,
\[
 A\downarrow_C^h(B\cup D)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(A\downarrow_C^h B
       \ \text{and}\
       A\downarrow_{B\cup C}^h D\bigr).
 \tag{SIR-T}\label{eq:sir-transitivity}
\]
Suppose the left side holds.
The first right conjunct is
monotonicity. Take new vertices
\(a\in A\setminus(B\cup C)\),
\(d\in D\setminus(B\cup C)\).
The exact menu over the enlarged
base is
\[
 D_{B\cup C}(a,d)=
 D_C(a,d)\cap
 \bigcap_{b\in B\setminus C}
 (\{\col(a,b)\}*\{\col(b,d)\}).
 \tag{SIR-B}\label{eq:sir-base-extension}
\]
The actual triangle through
each \(b\) contains
\(\col(a,d)=h(D_C(a,d))\);
hence this selected atom lies
in the nonempty exact
\(D_{B\cup C}(a,d)\).
By survivor heredity
\eqref{eq:sir-HS},
\[
 h(D_{B\cup C}(a,d))
       =h(D_C(a,d))=\col(a,d).
\]
Thus \(A\downarrow_{B\cup C}^h D\).

Conversely suppose both right
conjuncts hold. The disjointness
conditions imply that no pair
from \(A\setminus C\) and
\((B\cup D)\setminus C\)
is literally the same vertex.
The required cross colours
to \(B\) are already selected.
For \(a\in A\setminus C\)
and \(d\in D\setminus(B\cup C)\),
let \(q=h(D_C(a,d))\).
For every \(b\in B\setminus C\)
the triple of exact/literal
domains
\[
  D_C(a,b),\
  \{\col(b,d)\},\
  D_C(a,d)
\]
satisfies all three PC
conditions by
\cref{lem:sir-free-span-PC},
now applied to the left side
\(A\) and the right finite
structure \(B\cup D\) over
\(C\). Applying the joint
selector gives
\[
 q\in
   \{h(D_C(a,b))\}*
   \{\col(b,d)\}
 = \{\col(a,b)\}*
   \{\col(b,d)\},
\]
where the last equality is
\(A\downarrow_C^h B\).
For \(b\in C\) the same
membership follows directly
from the definition of
\(D_C(a,d)\). Consequently
\(q\) belongs to EACH of the
original strong base-path
constraints defining
\(D_{B\cup C}(a,d)\).
This simultaneously proves
that the enlarged-base exact
menu is NONEMPTY and that
\[
  q\in D_{B\cup C}(a,d)
                  \subseteq D_C(a,d).
\]
The hypotheses of survivor
heredity
\eqref{eq:sir-HS} are therefore
met, and they give
\(h(D_{B\cup C}(a,d))=q\).
No arbitrary selector choice
was recomputed after changing
the base.
By the other right conjunct,
\(\col(a,d)=h(D_{B\cup C}(a,d))=q\).
This proves all cross colours
needed for the left side.
The argument uses \emph{both}
full PC preservation and
heredity; either property alone
would not justify transitivity.

\emph{Stationarity.}
Suppose \(A\equiv_C A'\),
and both are independent
from \(B\) over \(C\).
The isomorphism \(A\cong_C A'\)
preserves each colour
\(\col(a,c)\), while the
named set \(B\) fixes
each \(\col(c,b)\). Therefore
\[
 D_C(a,b)=D_C(a',b)
\]
for corresponding cross
pairs, with identical tagged
endpoint sorts. Their selected
cross atoms are the same
\(h(D_C)\). Thus the given
isomorphism together with
the identity on \(B\) is
a finite induced isomorphism
\(AB\cong_B A'B\).
Homogeneity extends it,
showing \(A\equiv_{BC}A'\).
The statement includes the
empty base and the full
unary sort language.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why SAP cannot be deleted from this argument]
\label{rem:sir-HS-AP-firewall}
The positive theorem applies to
the \emph{SAP} no-finite-cover
source, not automatically to
the original AP-only
class from Part~IV. A homogeneous
symmetric binary homomorphic-CCO
source can have \emph{no}
finite-relative-index invariant
equivalence cover and nevertheless
fail SAP.

Indeed let \(M=I\times J\) for
infinite pure sets \(I,J\).
Name exactly the three symmetric
diversity colours: same \(I\)
coordinate, same \(J\) coordinate,
and different in both.
This source is homogeneous,
with invariant equivalence lattice
\(\{\Id,E_I,E_J,\Top\}\);
all proper cover indices are
infinite. Homomorphic CCO holds:
an inconsistency in the row
coordinate is precisely a
specified row-disequality edge
closing a path of forced row
equalities; likewise for columns.
Such a contradictory path
plus edge is a noncompletable
specified simple cycle.
If no such contradiction occurs,
choose independent new row
values for row-equality components
and column values for
column-equality components,
giving a homomorphic completion.
But SAP fails: over two
opposite corners
\((i_0,j_0),(i_1,j_1)\)
with both coordinates different,
the cell \((i_0,j_1)\)
is the \emph{unique} point
simultaneously in the first
row and second column.
Two copies of that one-point
extension cannot remain disjoint
in any amalgam over the
two original corners.

The selector theorem
\cref{thm:typed-HS} uses
\emph{strong} products and
strong exact domains in an
SAP language. One cannot
replace them by equality-inclusive
AP products or infer SIR
on the original grid merely
from \eqref{eq:sir-HS-amalgam}.
A separate closure-aware
or compatible-imaginary
interpretation is required
for the general AP-only
case. No negative SIR claim
about this grid is being made.
\end{remark}
