\subsection{Internal index-two covers become global packets}

Fix a realised quantifier-free one-point type $q(x/A)$ over a nonempty
finite \emph{complete real} base $A=\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$, with finite
fibre $Z=X_q$. Let $p_i=\col(x,a_i)$ and set
\[
 T=\bigcap_i A_{p_i}.
\]
The triangle-defined age implies that $T$ is precisely the diversity
palette of the induced homogeneous structure on $Z$: every colour in
$T$ can be realised by two new points over the entire complete base.
We call $R\subseteq T$ \emph{exact for $Z$} if $R$ is a global
equivalence support and every global $R$-class meeting $Z$ lies in $Z$.

\begin{lemma}[Spoke fixedness]\label{lem:rh-spokefixed}
If $R$ is exact for $Z$, every $p_i$ is $R$-fixed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in Z$ and let $r\in R$ and $s\in rp_i$.
Peircean rotation in the ACTUAL represented RAW product gives
$p_i\in rs$.  Anchor this legal configuration at the actual
$p_i$-pair $(x,a_i)$.  If $s=1$, then $r=p_i$ and we may
put $y=a_i$, obtaining $\col(x,y)=r$ and $ya_i=1$.
If $s$ is diversity, triangle-definedness and old
pair-homogeneity give a distinct real point $y$ such that
$\col(x,y)=r$ and $\col(y,a_i)=s$.  Thus in both cases
$y\in[x]_{E_R}\subseteq Z$ by exactness of $R$ for $Z$.
The case $s=1$ would give $y=a_i\in Z$, impossible since
$q(x/A)$ requires its new point to be distinct from every
actual real parameter.  Hence $s$ is diversity.
Since every $y\in Z$ has the same $p_i$-spoke
to $a_i$, necessarily $s=p_i$. The additional
identity factor in $R^+$ fixes $p_i$ literally,
so $R^+p_i=\{p_i\}$ as claimed.
In particular $p_i\notin R$, since otherwise
$1\in p_ip_i\subseteq R^+p_i$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spoke-square localisation from the original relative separator]
\label{lem:rh-spoke-square-localisation}
Let $R\subseteq\mathcal D$ be an ORIGINAL global
invariant equivalence diversity support and
let $P\subseteq\mathcal D$ be a nonempty
finite set of $R$-fixed diversity colours.
Put $T=\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p$.
If $c\in T$ and $A_c\cap T\subseteq R$, then
\[
              A_c\subseteq R,
              \qquad pc=\{p\}\quad(p\in P).
\]
No globalisation of a finite one-point fibre is
assumed here. The product is the original
equality-inclusive represented RAW product.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $c\in R$, equivalence closure gives
$A_c\subseteq R$, while $R$-fixedness
gives $pc=\{p\}$ for all $p\in P$.
Assume henceforth $c\notin R$.
Choose an inclusion-minimal family of the
square rows $A_c$ and $A_p$, $p\in P$,
whose intersection is contained in $R$;
one exists because $A_c\cap T\subseteq R$.
It MUST contain $A_c$: the intersection
of any subfamily consisting only of
spoke rows contains $c\in T\setminus R$.

If at least TWO spoke rows remain in this
minimal family, omit one of them, say $A_p$.
Minimality supplies an atom
$x\notin R$ in the intersection of all
remaining rows, so in particular
$x\in A_c$ and
$x\in(\bigcap_{A_b\text{ remaining}}A_b)\setminus R$.
But $c\in A_p$ because $c\in T$.
This contradicts the ORIGINAL
square-avoidance
\cref{lem:rh-collapse}(3), applied to
the chosen minimal blocker with
$b_i=p$ and $a=c\in A_p$.
Consequently either $A_c\subseteq R$
already, or the minimal family is
$\{A_c,A_p\}$ for ONE $p\in P$.
In the latter case $c\in A_p$ and
$A_c\cap A_p\subseteq R$, so
self-side collapse
\cref{lem:rh-collapse}(2) yields
$A_c\subseteq R$.
Now for EVERY $p\in P$ we have
$c\in A_p$ and
$A_c\cap A_p\subseteq R$.
A final application of
\cref{lem:rh-collapse}(2) gives
$pc=\{p\}$ simultaneously for all
spokes, including repeated
original atom names (duplicates
may be deleted from $P$).
All logical-identity branches are
those already proved in
\cref{lem:rh-collapse}; no
synthetic STRONG identity was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical spoke-fixer and direct finite-fibre globalisation]
\label{lem:rh-direct-spoke-fixer}
Let $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\subseteq M$ be
nonempty and COMPLETE, and let
$q(x/A)$ be an ACTUAL realised
one-point type with $x\notin A$.
Write $p_i=\col(x,a_i)$,
$P=\{p_i:1\le i\le n\}$, and
$Z=X_q$, with its actual
diversity palette
$T=\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p$.
Define the ORIGINAL spoke-fixer
diversity support
\[
 R_P=\{d\in\mathcal D:
                      pd=\{p\}\text{ for EVERY }p\in P\}.
\]
Then $R_P$ is a global invariant
equivalence support, and EVERY
$R_P$-class meeting $Z$ lies
wholly in $Z$; in particular
$R_P\subseteq T$.
Moreover $R_P$ is the LARGEST
global invariant equivalence
support fixing all spoke colours
in $P$.

If the realised one-point fibre
$Z$ is FINITE, then
\[
                        R_P=T,
             \qquad Z=[x]_{E_{R_P}}
                       \quad(x\in Z).
\]
The existence proof uses only ONE
internal index-two Hasse cover,
not a sequence of intermediate
globalised equivalences.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All products here are original
RAW products with TRUE logical
identity $1$. Let $d,e\in R_P$
and take any actual RAW atom
$f\in de$, possibly $f=1$.
For every $p\in P$,
\[
 pf\subseteq p(de)=(pd)e=\{p\}e=\{p\}.
\]
The product $pf$ is nonempty by
transitivity of the original
one-sort structure (and $p1=p$
when $f=1$), hence
$pf=\{p\}$. Thus any diversity
$f\in de$ belongs to $R_P$.
Symmetry of ORIGINAL atom names
and this composition closure
show that $E_{R_P}=1\cup R_P$
is an actual global invariant
equivalence. If some other global
invariant support $R$ has all
$p\in P$ $R$-fixed, then
$pd=\{p\}$ for every
$d\in R$, hence $R\subseteq R_P$.

Fix $z\in Z$, and let $y$ be
$R_P$-related to $z$. For every
anchor $a_i$, the actual
triangle $a_i,z,y$ and
$p_iR_P^+=\{p_i\}$ show
$\col(y,a_i)=p_i$; if
$y=a_i$, its logical-identity
colour would contradict this
diversity singleton.
So $y\in Z$, proving
EXACTNESS of $R_P$ for this
realised one-point fibre.
Every diversity atom $d\in R_P$
has an actual neighbour from
any fixed $z$ by original
transitivity; this neighbour
lies in $Z$. Since the
original triangle-fullness
argument identifies the
actual diversity palette of
$Z$ with $T$, we obtain
$R_P\subseteq T$.

Suppose now $Z$ is FINITE
and $R_P\subsetneq T$.
The induced $Z$ is finite,
transitive, homogeneous
and homomorphic-CCO by
\cref{lem:fibreCCO}.
The restriction of $E_{R_P}$
to $Z$ is a PROPER invariant
equivalence. Select ONE
Hasse cover above it in the
finite invariant-equivalence
lattice of $Z$.
By \cref{cor:hasse}, its
relative index is exactly
two. Let $C\subseteq
T\setminus R_P$ be its
nonempty diversity colour
layer. For every $c\in C$
the internal two-step
$c;c$ has only $R_P$
endpoints (or equality):
two $c$-edges traverse
the two classes of the
index-two extension twice.

We must transfer that
INTERNAL square statement
to the ORIGINAL $A_c$.
For any $d\in A_c\cap T$,
the original $(c,c,d)$
triangle is legal.
Adjoin THREE distinct new
$q$-points over the SAME
complete real anchor $A$,
with that triangle among
the new points.
Every old--old--new triangle
is copied from the realised
$q$-type; every
old--new--new triangle is
legal because $c,d\in
T=\bigcap_i A_{p_i}$;
the three-new triangle is
legal by $d\in A_c$.
ORIGINAL triangle fullness
and homogeneity therefore
realise all three new points
in the SAME actual fibre $Z$.
The internal index-two
statement forces $d\in R_P$.
Thus
\[
                         A_c\cap T\subseteq R_P
                       \qquad(c\in C).
\]

Every $p\in P$ is
$R_P$-fixed: since the
ORIGINAL RAW orbital
relations are symmetric,
their products commute, so
\[
       R_P^+p=pR_P^+=\{p\}
\]
by the very definition of
$R_P$ (with the TRUE identity
factor acting neutrally).
Every $c\in C$ lies in $T$.
Apply
\cref{lem:rh-spoke-square-localisation}
with $R=R_P$. It gives
$pc=\{p\}$ for ALL $p\in P$,
so $c\in R_P$ by definition,
contradicting
$C\subseteq T\setminus R_P$.
Hence $R_P=T$.

Finally every pair of
distinct points of $Z$
has original colour in
$T=R_P$, so
$Z\subseteq[x]_{E_{R_P}}$.
The reverse inclusion is
the proved exactness of
$R_P$ for $Z$. Therefore
$Z=[x]_{E_{R_P}}$, as
claimed. If $Z$ has one
point, then $T=R_P$ is
empty and this is just
the TRUE logical equality
class, without an invented
diversity identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Globalisation of finite real fibres]\label{thm:rh-globalise}
Every finite realised one-point fibre $Z=X_q$ is exactly one class of
a parameter-free invariant finite-class equivalence. More precisely,
$T=\bigcap_i A_{p_i}$ is a global equivalence support. Every internal
saturated chain from equality to the universal equivalence of $Z$
globalises, with each successive global cover of index two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a NONEMPTY finite real anchor $A$,
\cref{lem:rh-direct-spoke-fixer}
constructs the original global
equivalence $E_T$ DIRECTLY:
its diversity support is the
canonical spoke-fixer $R_P=T$
and the whole realised finite
one-point fibre $Z$ is exactly
ONE global $E_T$-class.
In particular this existence
proof uses only the ORIGINAL
relative separator/square
calculus and ONE index-two
Hasse cover in $Z$; it never
assumes that a proper
intermediate internal fibre
equivalence has already been
globalised.

For the further CHAIN claim,
fix ANY internal invariant
equivalence $V$ on $Z$.
By its homogeneity, $V$ is a
union of complete original
fine-colour atom names
$S\subseteq T$, together
with true logical identity.
The same original old atom
union $H=\Id\cup S$ is a
GLOBAL invariant equivalence:
two successive $H$-edges
lie inside ONE $E_T$-class,
and original transitivity
moves that class into $Z$,
where internal $V$ is
transitive. Thus the third
original edge is also in
$S$ or is true identity.
Every atom of $S$ occurs
within $Z$, so this is the
UNIQUE global original
equivalence lifting $V$;
it is exact for $Z$
because $H\le E_T$.
Restriction and extension
therefore identify the
whole original interval
$[\Id,E_T]$ with the full
internal invariant-equivalence
lattice of $Z$.
This direct extension argument
does NOT invoke the later
block-interval theorem, whose
more general proof is separate.
In particular EVERY internal
saturated Hasse chain
globalises, independently
of its choice, and each
successive global cover has
relative index two by
\cref{cor:hasse} on finite $Z$.

For $A=\varnothing$, if
$M$ is infinite transitive
there is no finite
real one-point fibre.
If $M$ is finite, $Z=M$,
$T=\mathcal D$ is already
the global universal
equivalence support, and
all internal Hasse covers
have index two by
\cref{cor:hasse}.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[Spoke-set criterion and uniform sizes of realised one-point fibres]
\label{cor:rh-fibre-spoke-set-uniformity}
Let $P\subseteq\mathcal D$ be
a nonempty finite set of
old diversity colours realised
as the SET of spokes of
some one-point type over
a finite nonempty COMPLETE
original real base.
Put
\[
       T_P=\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p,
       \qquad
       R_P=\{d\in\mathcal D:
                            pd=\{p\}\ (p\in P)\}.
\]
For EVERY realised old
one-point type $q(x/A)$
with this same SET $P$ of
distinct spoke colours,
its real fibre $Z=X_q$
satisfies
\[
 Z=[x]_{E_{R_P}}
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                     T_P=R_P.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |Z|<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_P=R_P\ \text{and}\ 
              [E_{R_P}:\Id]<\infty.
\]
If ONE such realised type has
finite fibre, EVERY realised
one-point type with the same
set $P$ has the SAME finite
fibre cardinality
$[E_{R_P}:\Id]$, which is a
power of two. In fact these
finite fibres are pairwise
CONJUGATE under
$\Aut(M)$, since they are
classes of the SAME invariant
equivalence in a transitive
original structure. The
statement does NOT say that
every arbitrarily prescribed
list of spokes is realisable
or that the chosen
automorphism must transport
the anchor parameters.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Original triangle fullness
and homogeneity show that
for ANY realised one-point
type with this set $P$,
the diversity palette of
its fibre is EXACTLY $T_P$:
realise each
$d\in T_P$ by two new
points with the same
spokes over the SAME
complete base, and
use the anchor alignment.
By
\cref{lem:rh-direct-spoke-fixer},
$R_P\subseteq T_P$ and
each $E_{R_P}$-class
meeting $Z$ lies wholly
inside $Z$.

If $T_P=R_P$, every pair
of distinct points of $Z$
has $E_{R_P}$-colour.
Thus $Z$ lies in one
$E_{R_P}$-class, and
the preceding exactness
gives equality.
Conversely, if $Z$ is
one such class, all
its diversity colours
belong to $R_P$.
Since they are exactly
$T_P$, equality follows.
The finiteness equivalence
is immediate. By original
transitivity all classes
of a global invariant
equivalence have the SAME
cardinality, and the
sets $T_P,R_P$ depend
only on the SET of spoke
colour names, not the
parameter configuration.
If two fibres are whole
$E_{R_P}$-classes, an
original automorphism taking
any point of the first to
any point of the second
takes the ENTIRE first class
to the second one.
If one such fibre is finite,
\cref{lem:rh-direct-spoke-fixer}
gives $T_P=R_P$; all
others are therefore full
$E_{R_P}$-classes of the
same finite size.
The two-power conclusion
follows from
\cref{cor:rh-class-2power};
its proof is independent
of this corollary and
uses the finite CCO
index-two theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A nontrivial two-point spoke-fixer fibre]
\label{ex:rh-direct-spoke-fixer-two-point}
Let $I$ be countably infinite and let
\[
                    M=I\times\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2 .
\]
Name the ORIGINAL symmetric diversity
atom $q$ for points with the same
$I$-block and first bit but opposite
second bit; name $r$ when the
$I$-block agrees but the FIRST bit
differs, regardless of the second bit;
name $b$ between different $I$-blocks.
This original one-sort complete binary
structure is homogeneous and has AP
and semantic HOMOMORPHIC CCO.

For CCO, in any finite partial diagram
the specified non-$b$ edges force
common $I$-blocks. An incompatible
specified $b$ edge inside such a
connected component gives a
noncompletable SIMPLE cycle consisting
of that $b$ edge and a non-$b$ path.
Within one non-$b$ component, the
first-bit constraints are
$q$-edges of parity zero and
$r$-edges of parity one; an
inconsistent parity system gives a
specified simple cycle with odd
total parity. The second-bit
constraints occur only on $q$-edges
and require opposite bits; an
inconsistent system gives an odd
$q$-only simple cycle. Conversely,
if every specified simple cycle
homomorphically completes, none
of these inconsistencies occurs.
Solve the two parity systems
and assign distinct fresh
$I$-blocks to distinct non-$b$
connected components. This produces
one simultaneous colour-preserving
homomorphism into $M$, proving
semantic CCO. Homogeneity follows
by permutations of the $I$-blocks,
swapping the two first-bit parts
and independently swapping the
two second-bit values in each part.

Here the ORIGINAL RAW products are
\[
                  rr=\{1,q\},
                       \qquad rq=\{r\}.
\]
Fix any anchor $a=(i,0,0)$ and
the realised one-point type
$q_0(x/\{a\})$ requiring
$\col(x,a)=r$.
Its fibre is exactly
\[
        Z=\{(i,1,0),(i,1,1)\},
\]
whose original diversity palette
is $T=A_r=\{q\}$. The canonical
spoke-fixer for $P=\{r\}$ is
$R_P=\{q\}$, since $rq=\{r\}$
but $rr\ne\{r\}$ and $rb=\{b\}$.
Thus the direct theorem gives the
ACTUAL global $q$-matching class
$Z=[(i,1,0)]_{E_{R_P}}$, with
$|Z|=2$. This checks a NONSINGLETON
finite real fibre, with no choice
of an internal Hasse chain or
an artificial RAW/STRONG identity.
\end{example}
