\subsection{Relative pseudocomplements and looped descent}

A diversity colour $r$ is \emph{looped} if the triangle $(r,r,r)$ is
allowed, or equivalently $r\in A_r$. Write
$L=\{r\in\mathcal D:r\in A_r\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-family relative pseudocomplement]
\label{lem:rh-pseudo}
Let $R$ be a global equivalence support and let $P\subseteq\mathcal D$
be a finite nonempty set of $R$-fixed colours. Put
\[
 S_P=\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p,
 \qquad W_R(P)=\{a\in\mathcal D:A_a\cap S_P\subseteq R\}.
\]
Then $W_R(P)$ is a global equivalence support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, $R\subseteq W_R(P)$, because the square of a colour belonging
to a global equivalence support belongs to that support together with
identity. Also $S_P\setminus R$ is saturated under the one-sided
$R$-orbit relation. Indeed for each $R$-fixed $p\in P$,
\[
 R^+(pp)=(R^+p)p=pp.
\]
Because $1\in pp$, this also implies $R^+\subseteq pp$,
so every atom of $R$ lies in $A_p$. If $t\in A_p\setminus R$
and $t'\in R^+t$, then $t'\in pp$, while neither $t'=1$
nor $t'\in R$ is possible: either would imply $t\in R^+$
by symmetry and transitivity of the equivalence $R^+$.
Thus $t'\in A_p\setminus R$. Intersecting over $p\in P$
proves the required saturation of $S_P\setminus R$,
including every equality-output edge case.

We prove closure under products. Fix $a\in W_R(P)$ with $a\notin R$.
Let $x\in A_a$, and suppose, seeking a contradiction,
$t\in A_x\cap S_P\setminus R$. Associativity of $x\in aa$ and
$t\in xx$ supplies an atom $d\in ax$ with $t\in ad$.
If $d=1$, then the equality output in $ax$ forces $x=a$,
and $t\in a1$ forces $t=a$.  Since $x=a\in A_a$ and
$t=a\in S_P\setminus R$, this contradicts $a\in W_R(P)$.
If $d=a$, then $t\in A_a\cap S_P\setminus R$ is already a
contradiction. Thus $d$ is a diversity colour distinct from $a$.
The row $a$ fails to separate $a,d$, witnessed by
$x\in A_a\cap H(a,d)$. Each row $p\in P$ fails to separate
$a,d$, witnessed by $t\in A_p\cap H(a,d)$. The relative
separator applied to $\{a\}\cup P$ (whose square intersection
lies in $R$) forces $a\sim_R d$. Orbit folding now applies to
the distinct colours $a,d$ and $t\in H(a,d)\setminus R$;
it yields a colour $t'\in A_a\cap S_P\setminus R$, again a
contradiction.
This proves $A_x\cap S_P\subseteq R$ for every $x\in A_a$.
If instead $a\in R$, then $A_a\subseteq R\subseteq W_R(P)$,
so diagonal closure also holds in that case.

For mixed closure take distinct $a,b\in W_R(P)$ and
$c\in H(a,b)=ab$. Assume $t\in A_c\cap S_P\setminus R$.
Since $c\in ct$, associativity with $c\in ab$ produces an atom
$d\in bt$ such that $c\in ad$. If $d=1$, then the equality
output in $bt$ forces $b=t$, and $c\in a1$ forces $c=a$.
Since $c=a\in ab$, Peircean rotation yields
$b\in aa$, so the diversity atom $t=b$
belongs to $A_a\cap S_P\setminus R$, contradicting
$a\in W_R(P)$. If $d=b$, then $t\in A_b\cap S_P\setminus R$
is already forbidden. Thus $d$ is a diversity colour distinct
from $b$, as required by the rectangle criterion. The row $a$
does not separate $b,d$: $c\in H(a,b)\cap H(a,d)$ witnesses
this. The other rows $p\in P$ do not separate them either:
$t\in A_p\cap H(b,d)$, so the rectangle criterion supplies an
element of $H(p,b)\cap H(p,d)$. Relative separation for the
blocker family $\{a\}\cup P$ forces $b\sim_R d$. Orbit folding,
now applicable to the distinct colours $b,d$, gives a colour
$t'\in A_b\setminus R$ in the orbit of $t$, and saturation of
$S_P\setminus R$ puts $t'$ in $S_P\setminus R$, a contradiction.
Hence $c\in W_R(P)$. Together with diagonal closure and the
identity atom, this proves product closure of $W_R(P)^+$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Carrier insertion]\label{lem:rh-carrier}
Suppose $P\subseteq\mathcal D$ is a finite nonempty family of spoke
colours and $b$ is an additional spoke colour; they can be jointly
realised around one new point by AP. Put
\[
 S=\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p,\qquad C=S\cap A_b.
\]
If $r\in L\cap S\cap\langle A_b\rangle$, then
$L\cap C\ne\varnothing$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $b\in P$, then $C=S$ and the conclusion follows directly
from the assumed $r\in L\cap S$; no extra anchor is needed.
Hence assume $b\notin P$.
Assume $L\cap C=\varnothing$. Choose a complete finite set of
anchors realising the star with spoke colours $P\cup\{b\}$, and let
$Z$ be the realised one-point fibre. Its diversity palette is EXACTLY
$C$: if two points realise the same spoke type, every
anchor--new--new triangle forces their mutual diversity
colour into $A_p$ for each $p\in P$ and into $A_b$,
so it lies in $C$. Conversely, for any diversity
$c\in C$, adjoin two DISTINCT new points with
identical prescribed $P\cup\{b\}$ spokes and
mutual colour $c$ to the COMPLETE finite anchor
diagram. Every triangle with two old anchors
already occurs in the realised one-point star;
each triangle with one old anchor is legal
by $c\in A_p$ or $c\in A_b$; the two-new-point
diagram has no three-new-point triangle.
Triangle-definedness realises the complete
diagram, and homogeneity aligns its anchor
copy with the original one. Both new points
then lie in $Z$ and realise $c$.
In particular if $C=\varnothing$, the
nonempty fibre $Z$ has precisely ONE point;
the equality equivalence support
$C=\varnothing$ is already global and exact
for this singleton, so the globalisation
theorem applies without an artificial
nonempty-diversity hypothesis.
The fibre is finite: if it were
infinite, infinite Ramsey would give an infinite monochromatic
triangle of some colour $c\in C\cap L$, contradicting the assumption.
The globalisation theorem makes $C$ a global equivalence support
exact for $Z$, and spoke fixedness makes all colours in $P$ $C$-fixed.
Consequently $W_C(P)$ is a global equivalence support.
By definition $A_b\cap S=C$, so $b\in W_C(P)$. The support
$W_C(P)$ is closed and hence contains $\langle A_b\rangle$
(because it contains $b$, it also contains $A_b$ and its generated
support). Thus $r\in W_C(P)$. On the other hand,
$r\in L\cap S\setminus C$ means
$r\in A_r\cap S\setminus C$, exactly contradicting the defining
condition for $r\in W_C(P)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Looped diagonal Helly]\label{lem:rh-loophelly}
For every finite family of diversity colours $p_1,\dots,p_n$,
\[
 L\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^n\langle A_{p_i}\rangle\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 L\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^n A_{p_i}\ne\varnothing.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Remove duplicates and start with a looped colour $r_0$ in all
$\langle A_{p_i}\rangle$. We process the rows in order, maintaining
a looped colour $r_j$ that belongs directly to every already processed
$A_{p_i}$ and belongs to every not-yet-processed generated support
$\langle A_{p_k}\rangle$.

If the current looped colour $r$ equals the next row $p_j$, that row
is already satisfied because $r\in A_r$; keep $r$.
Otherwise take as $P$ all previously processed rows together with
the row $r$, removing duplicates. A finite star with these prescribed
spoke colours and one further spoke $p_j$ is realisable: amalgamate
the finitely many prescribed edges over their common central point,
then use triangle-definedness. The current $r$ belongs to
$L\cap\bigcap_{p\in P}A_p\cap\langle A_{p_j}\rangle$;
the membership in $A_r$ is exactly why we included the extra row $r$.
Carrier insertion yields a looped $r'$ satisfying every row in $P$
and also $p_j$. Moreover $r'\in A_r$, so every generated equivalence
support containing $r$ also contains $r'$. This preserves membership
in all remaining $\langle A_{p_k}\rangle$. Repeat until all rows
have been imposed. The resulting looped colour lies in every $A_{p_i}$.
\end{proof}
