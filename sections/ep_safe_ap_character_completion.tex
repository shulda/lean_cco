\section{AP-only safe exact character splitting}
\label{sec:ep-ap-only-safe-character}

This section gives the \emph{actual AP-only} safe-character
completion theorem as part of the common Foundations.
It is used later for exact-quotient character towers in
\cref{thm:ep-quotient-tower,thm:ep-automatic-core}.
In contrast to the historical SAP-only V27 terminal
construction reproduced in the technical appendix,
the present proof constructs genuine finite marked
AP and homomorphic-CCO completions, never inferring
SAP from abstract associative products.

Let \(M\) be countable homogeneous complete finite binary,
with finitely many named transitive original unary sorts,
same-sort symmetric orbital relations and homomorphic
semantic simple-cycle CCO. No old SAP or
triangle-definedness is assumed in this section.
All old products are actual represented relations,
\emph{including logical identity}. We use the positive
full typed exact-domain convolution and 2-Helly
from Part~I (or its direct A130 proof).

The SAP-free finite-relative orbital parity theorem is now stated and
proved in Foundations, \cref{lem:ep-ap-orbital-parity}. We use its actual
represented even/odd relations, without requiring injective intermediate
vertices or replacing logical identity by a diversity atom.

Choose one such reachable even/odd interval
\(Y<X\), an inclusion-maximal full exact
complement \(K\) with \(Y\le K\) and
\(K\cap X=Y\), and the full groupoid of
actual quotient-functional exact bridges.
The \emph{source maximal exact complement} and
its distinguished odd quotient involution follow
directly from the earlier SAP-free foundational
\cref{thm:foundational-maximal-parity-gate}; the
\emph{quotient-functional bridge groupoid}
and its finite character now follow from
the earlier general full exact source results
\cref{thm:foundational-rigid-groupoid,thm:foundational-rigid-character}.
Those common results require old homomorphic
CCO and exact convolution but no SAP:
\[
  K\prec K\vee X,\quad [K\vee X:K]=2,
\]
and \(K;O;K\) induces a nontrivial
fixed-point-free quotient involution
\(\tau\) with the following
\emph{maximal source gate}:
\[
 Z>\Id_{M_a/K}\text{ full exact}
          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau\subseteq Z.
 \tag{S0}\label{eq:ep-ap-source-gate}
\]
Indeed any larger quotient exact equivalence
omitting \(\tau\) pulls back to an original
exact \(K'>K\) satisfying
\(K'\cap X=Y\), contradicting maximality.
The full exact functional bridges transport
the gate and its odd involution; path
independence and elementary abelian
isotropy are
\cref{thm:foundational-rigid-groupoid}.
The resulting character \(\chi\) takes
each transported odd involution to \(1\).
All compatible finite phase prescriptions
extend to global orbit-seed markings
exactly by
\cref{lem:foundational-finite-phase-extension};
this proves reasonableness and
reduct-surjectivity of the character
marking, but does \emph{not} itself
establish AP or CCO of the marked age.
Those properties are proved below.
On active
original sorts, the actual phase masks are
\[
 A_{st}^{\varepsilon,\eta}
    =B_{st}^{\varepsilon+\eta}\cup N_{st},
 \tag{S1}\label{eq:ep-ap-mask}
\]
where \(B^\delta\) consists of rigid quotient arrows
of character \(\delta\), while \(N\) consists of
nonrigid atoms. The forbidden mask is precisely
the opposite rigid sheet. Inactive sorts have
only their prescribed bit. The literal diagonal
\(\Id\) is an allowed output only for
equal old sorts and identical phase labels;
it is never a diversity edge joining two
distinct formal vertices.

The original relative-$K$ exact closure-separation lemma is now proved
in Foundations, \cref{lem:ep-relative-K-closure}. In the following
phase-primality argument its base relation is the \emph{whole old
$K$-equivalence}, not original logical equality.

\begin{lemma}[SAP-free fork totality and phase-primality]
\label{lem:ep-ap-TP}
For the \emph{maximal} exact-character
groupoid phase masks \eqref{eq:ep-ap-mask},
the following hold without old SAP:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textup{\alph*}),leftmargin=*]
\item[(T)] Given two allowed old atomic
pair types with compatible middle phase,
some old represented output is allowed
at the prescribed endpoint phases.
\item[(P)] If old reachable typed word
endpoint profiles \(W_1,\ldots,W_r\)
have nonempty intersection and each
meets the requested endpoint phase mask,
their intersection also meets the mask.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For (T), represented products of nonempty
typed invariant relations are nonempty
because their intermediate original sort
is transitive. If either output endpoint
sort is inactive, every old output with
the prescribed inactive bit is allowed,
so (T) holds immediately. If both are
active, any nonrigid old output is
phase-flexible. Otherwise, suppose all
old outputs have the wrong rigid character.
Saturating the output product by the
endpoint exact gates yields a nonempty
full exact domain \(D\), confined to a
single rigid hom-torsor. The general exact rigid-slice theorem
\cref{lem:foundational-rigid-affine} gives
\(D=dS\) for a full exact isotropy subgroup \(S\),
since the constructed domain is explicitly
endpoint-gate bisaturated.
The wrong character is constant on \(D\),
so \(S\subseteq\ker\chi\).
If \(S\ne1\), the union of its old
$K$-bisaturated represented arrows is
a nontrivial \emph{original full exact
equivalence strictly above $K$}. It
omits the selected old phase-one relation
$t=KOK$, because $S\subseteq\ker\chi$
and $\chi(t)=1$. This contradicts the
strong old-level maximal gate
\cref{thm:foundational-maximal-parity-gate},
without a converse exact-descent assumption. Hence \(S=1\),
and all old output atoms lie in one
functional rigid bridge \(R\).
The shared rigid-product factorisation
\cref{lem:foundational-rigid-factor}
factors the two old inputs through
rigid bridges \(R_1,R_2\) with
\(R_1;R_2=R\).
Their admissible characters add,
contradicting the wrong character of
\(R\). This proves (T), including
identity outputs.

For (P), if either endpoint is inactive,
the requested mask contains the entire
old typed atom palette, so the assertion
is immediate. When both are active,
suppose contrapositively the
nonempty old intersection \(D=\bigcap_iW_i\)
is confined to the wrong rigid sheet
but each \(W_i\) meets the allowed mask.
Saturate each profile by the endpoint full exact gates,
writing \(E_i=K_s;W_i;K_t\). The RAW full typed
saturation--intersection theorem
\cref{lem:full-typed-exact-saturation} gives
\[
 \bigcap_i E_i=K_s;\Bigl(\bigcap_iW_i\Bigr);K_t.
\]
This is nonempty and entirely supported on wrong-character
rigid arrows. We do \emph{not} use the SAP/strong-diversity
variant \cref{lem:exact-saturation-intersection}.
The affine rigid-slice lemma gives a coset \(dS\);
the original maximal gate forces \(S=1\), as in (T).
Thus \(\bigcap_iE_i=d\) is ONE complete rigid
\(K_s,K_t\)-bridge, not an old fine atomic relation.

Each \(W_i\) meets the allowed mask, and \(d\) lies wholly
in the wrong rigid sheet. Hence \(E_i\supsetneq d\).
Set \(A_i=E_i;d^\smile\), of type \(s\to s\).
The \(A_i\) are symmetric full exact \(K_s\)-bisaturated
relations, and each contains \(K_s=d;d^\smile\).
Since \(d^\smile;d=K_t\) and each \(E_i\) is
right-\(K_t\)-saturated,
\(A_i=K_s\) would imply \(E_i=d\), impossible.
Consequently EVERY \(A_i\) strictly contains \(K_s\).
The nonempty exact meet-distributivity and the bridge
identities give
\[
 \bigcap_iA_i=
 \Bigl(\bigcap_iE_i\Bigr);d^\smile
       =d;d^\smile=K_s .
\]
This is the \emph{whole original represented
equivalence \(K_s\)}, not old logical \(\Id_s\).

Choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily whose intersection is
\(K_s\). It has at least two members. Choose one member
\(A\) and let \(B\) be the intersection of all the others.
Both \(A,B\) strictly contain \(K_s\):
for \(A\) this was proved above, and for \(B\) it follows
from minimality of the subfamily. They are symmetric full
exact, \(K_s\)-bisaturated, with \(A\cap B=K_s\).
Their represented stable equivalence closures are therefore
both strictly above \(K_s\). The transported
\emph{old-level} maximal gate (via exact bridge conjugation)
puts the phase-one \(K_s\)-bisaturated relation into BOTH
closures, contradicting their intersection being exactly
\(K_s\) by \cref{lem:ep-relative-K-closure}.
This proves (P), without identifying a quotient
identity with old equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact algebraic endpoint support of a marked path]
\label{lem:ep-ap-path}
Let $w=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ be a nonempty
old \emph{typed diversity} path on formal
vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_n$, with each
specified edge $a_j$ locally allowed for
the prescribed endpoint phase bits
$\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_n$.
Write
\[
 P_r=\{a_1\};\cdots;\{a_r\},\qquad
 \delta_r=\epsilon_0+\epsilon_r,
\]
and calculate the \emph{formal lifted
endpoint supports} recursively by
\[
 L_1=\{a_1\},\qquad
 L_{r+1}=(L_r;\{a_{r+1}\})\cap
       A_{s_0s_{r+1}}^{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_{r+1}},
\]
where the last mask is the \emph{actual two-endpoint-bit}
mask from \eqref{eq:ep-ap-mask}; no extra independent
bit is introduced when an endpoint sort is inactive.
Then for every $r=1,\ldots,n$,
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 L_r=P_r\cap A_{s_0s_r}^{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_r}
                      \ne\varnothing .\qquad}
 \tag{S4}\label{eq:ep-ap-path-normal-form}
\]
The equality uses \emph{actual represented}
equality-inclusive old products: a literal
old identity may occur in $L_r$ as a
possible \emph{endpoint output}, never
as a specified diversity edge.
The supports $L_r$ are a formal
algebraic reduction of phase-constrained
paths; their equality alone does not
assume or assert that the entire marked
age has already been proved to have AP.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The one-edge case is the definition
of local phase legality. Assume the
formula for $r$ and put
$b=a_{r+1}$, $\alpha=\delta_r$,
$\beta=\epsilon_r+\epsilon_{r+1}$,
so $\delta_{r+1}=\alpha+\beta$.
In this proof $A^\alpha$ is shorthand for the
literal typed mask $A_{s_0s_r}^{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_r}$
and $A^{\alpha+\beta}$ for
$A_{s_0s_{r+1}}^{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_{r+1}}$;
this convention only abbreviates the already
prescribed endpoint bits. The forward inclusion
\[
 (L_r;\{b\})\cap A^{\alpha+\beta}
 \subseteq (P_r;\{b\})\cap A^{\alpha+\beta}
\]
is monotonicity. For the converse take
any old output atom
\[
 c\in (P_r;\{b\})\cap A^{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
Typed Peircean rotation puts
$P_r\cap T\ne\varnothing$, where
\[
       T=\{c\};\{\bar b\}
\]
is a nonempty represented \emph{word
profile of type $s_0\to s_r$}.
This is the required converse $\bar b$,
not the generally ill-typed product
$c;b$.

The induction hypothesis says
$P_r\cap A^{\alpha}\ne\varnothing$.
Both the old output $c$ with phase
$\alpha+\beta$ and the reversed edge
$\bar b$ with phase $\beta$ are allowed
(the latter uses exponent-two phase
bits). Totality (T) of
\cref{lem:ep-ap-TP}, applied to these
two inputs with the common middle
vertex of sort $s_{r+1}$, gives
$T\cap A^{\alpha}\ne\varnothing$.
By primality (P), since the two
represented word profiles $P_r,T$
have nonempty intersection and both
meet the same $A^{\alpha}$ mask,
\[
             P_r\cap T\cap A^\alpha
                        \ne\varnothing .
\]
Choose an atom $a$ in this intersection.
It lies in $L_r$ by induction and
$c\in a;b$, so $c\in L_r;\{b\}$.
This proves the reverse inclusion.

Finally $L_r$ is nonempty by induction,
and (T) applied to one old atom from
$L_r$ and the locally allowed final
edge $b$ supplies an allowed output
in $(L_r;b)\cap A^{\alpha+\beta}$.
Thus $L_{r+1}\ne\varnothing$, and the
induction is complete. No SAP or
injective intermediate witnesses
are used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Identity folding preserves individual old cycles]
\label{lem:ep-ap-identity-fold}
Let \(G\) be a finite simple, typed old diversity partial network
such that every specified simple cycle has a homomorphism into \(M\).
Let \(x\ne y\) be nonadjacent vertices of the same original sort.
Suppose \(\Id\) belongs to the represented endpoint profile of
\emph{every} simple \(x\)--\(y\) path in \(G\)
(the condition is vacuous when there is no such path).
Then one may identify \(x=y\), deduplicate incident edges,
and obtain another simple typed old diversity partial network
whose every specified simple cycle admits a homomorphism into \(M\).
If \(x,y\) have the same phase bit, their prescribed bits
also descend to this quotient. No SAP or already constructed
marked Fra\"iss\'e limit is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(x\to z\) and \(y\to z\) have old atomic colours
\(a,b\), respectively. The simple path \(x-z-y\)
has endpoint profile \(\{a\};\{\bar b\}\).
Since its profile contains the actual old identity, some
real pair \((u,v)\) belongs to both \(a\) and \(b\);
the old orbital partition gives \(a=b\).
Thus the identification never creates conflicting parallel
edge labels. Edges with only one former representative
retain their label; the quotient is a well-defined simple
typed diversity partial network.

Consider a simple cycle \(C\) in the quotient.
If it avoids the identified vertex, it was already a cycle
of \(G\). Otherwise the two distinct neighbours of the
identified vertex on \(C\) have incident edge representatives
coming from \(x\) or \(y\). If both can be chosen from the
same former vertex, replacing the identified vertex by that
former vertex lifts \(C\) to an old simple cycle of \(G\).
If the two chosen representatives come from different former
vertices, cutting \(C\) at the identified vertex produces a
simple \(x\)--\(y\) path in \(G\).
By hypothesis its \emph{represented} profile contains \(\Id\):
there are actual old witnesses for its edges with its two
endpoints mapped to the same real point. They realize a
homomorphism of \(C\) into \(M\).
These cases exhaust the quotient simple cycles.
Equal phase bits plainly descend when the vertices are
identified; no claim about marked-cycle realization is
needed for this old-cycle argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Real marked completion and AP-only safe splitting]
\label{thm:ep-ap-marked-completion}
A finite locally \(\chi\)-legal phase-marked
old diversity partial network \(P\)
has a homomorphic marked completion
provided each specified old simple
cycle is homomorphically completable in
the original \(M\).
Consequently the marked finite age
is reasonable, reduct-surjective,
precompact, has AP/JEP and homomorphic
semantic CCO, and its Fra\"iss\'e
limit is homogeneous.
Its complete diagrams are precisely
the locally pair-admissible old
complete diagrams.
These conclusions do \emph{not}
require original SAP or triangle-definedness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We construct an actual finite completion;
formal associativity alone would not
prove the assertion.

For any currently missing unordered
pair \(x,y\), original CCO supplies a
common old endpoint atom in the
intersection \(D\) of all its old
simple-path profiles, with the
typed top when there is no path.
By \cref{lem:ep-ap-path}, each
individual old path profile meets the
requested endpoint phase mask.
By (P), their nonempty intersection
meets that mask. With no path,
nonemptiness follows from the actual
free-isotropy groupoid orbit-seed
construction. Choose one allowed
old output \(c\in D\), possibly
\(\Id\).

If \(c\) is diversity, add its
specified edge to the partial graph.
Every new simple specified cycle is
exactly the new edge together with
one old simple \(x\)-\(y\) path.
Its old profile contains \(c\), hence
it is homomorphically completable.
The old cycles are unchanged.

If \(c=\Id\), immediately
\emph{identify} the formal vertices
\(x,y\), whose original sorts and
phase bits agree. The choice \(c\in D\)
means that \(\Id\) lies in \emph{every}
old simple \(x\)--\(y\) path profile.
Therefore \cref{lem:ep-ap-identity-fold}
proves that incident duplicate edges agree
and EVERY new specified simple cycle remains
old-completable, including cycles obtained by
joining old paths through the identified vertex.
The identity selection lies in the prescribed
literal endpoint mask. Moreover
\eqref{eq:ep-ap-path-normal-form}
places \(\Id\) in the \emph{formal
phase-compatible support} of each such old
path, so no path carries an unrecorded
phase obstruction. This last assertion is
algebraic only: it does \emph{not} assume
a marked-age homomorphism before the final
global phase extension. No artificial
identity-labelled graph edge is added.
All local phase requirements remain satisfied.

Every diversity insertion decreases
the number of unspecified pairs.
Every identity contraction decreases
the number of formal vertices.
Thus the lexicographic measure
(number of vertices, number of
unspecified pairs) strictly decreases.
We terminate in a \emph{strict complete
old diversity network} \(P_*\)
whose specified simple cycles
are still individually old-completable.
Apply the ORIGINAL homomorphic
semantic CCO directly to this
complete graph. Its homomorphism
into \(M\) is now \emph{injective},
since each distinct formal pair
is connected by an irreflexive
diversity atom. Thus \(P_*\)
embeds as an induced old complete
substructure, independently of old
triangle-definedness.
We verify the \emph{actual} hypotheses of
the global phase-extension lemma rather
than treating arbitrary marks as independent
bits. For any two of the finitely many
embedded real points whose current
$K$-quotient points are in the SAME
rigid-groupoid orbit, their old pair
orbital lies in the unique rigid arrow
$R$ relating those quotient points:
freeness of the rigid action makes
this the entire corresponding quotient
orbital. Since the final old pair colour
was chosen from its prescribed phase mask,
the two endpoint phase bits differ by
exactly $\chi(R)$. For a pair in different
rigid-groupoid orbits there is no
constraint on their relative seeds.
If two marked vertices map into the
same $K$-class, their relation lies
in the identity bridge and local
legality similarly forces equal bits.
Thus EVERY pairwise character condition
of \cref{lem:foundational-finite-phase-extension}
holds on the finite set of actual
$K$-quotient points represented by
$P_*$. Apply that lemma once to obtain
ONE global phase marking of the original
homogeneous source extending all the
prescribed finite bits. Inactive sorts
retain their unique fixed label.
Composing contractions with this
marked embedding gives a marked
homomorphic completion of $P$.

A marked partial diagram whose marked
simple cycles are completable has all
its old simple cycles completable, so
the same algorithm gives marked CCO.
For marked AP, take a span of complete
marked finite structures. By OLD AP
and homogeneity its formal partial
free union over the common complete
base has an old homomorphism into \(M\),
so all specified old cycles are
individually completable. Complete it
by the marked procedure. The resulting
map is injective on EACH original
complete leg because all its
distinct-vertex pair atoms are
irreflexive; hence the resulting
amalgamation is genuine AP, allowing
cross-leg collisions outside the base.
With empty base the same gives JEP.

Reasonableness and reduct-surjectivity
hold directly for full rigid-groupoid
character markings: every finite
complete old pair already records
its within-orbit \(\chi\)-difference
when rigid, and new quotient orbits
admit independent initial seed bits.
Hence any legal old finite embedding
extends any prescribed legal finite
marking. The hereditary finite
marked class now has AP and JEP
and therefore a homogeneous Fra\"iss\'e
limit; its old reduct is the original
homogeneous source by reasonableness
and age equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Strict rank drop under a genuine binary character split]
\label{lem:exact-character-rank-drop}
Let $\mathcal C$ be any homogeneous finite complete
binary age with finitely many named transitive current
sorts. Suppose a \emph{genuine} finite unary character
split replaces a nonempty family $S$ of active sorts
$a$ by two \emph{nonempty} child sorts
$a^0,a^1$, with all original binary atom names
unchanged. Assume on each active parent $a$
there is a realised old same-sort diversity atom
$c_a$ which always has phase difference one,
so that no $c_a$-pair can lie inside
$a^0$ or inside $a^1$.
For a current sort $a$, let $k_a$ count its realised
\emph{nonidentity same-sort pair orbitals}.
Then
\[
 k_{a^\epsilon}\le k_a-1\quad(\epsilon=0,1),
 \qquad
 \Phi(\mathcal C)=\sum_{\text{current sorts }a}3^{k_a}
\]
strictly decreases at this split:
\[
 \Phi(\mathcal C^{\rm new})
 \le \Phi(\mathcal C)
       -\sum_{a\in S}3^{k_a}
       +\sum_{a\in S}2\cdot3^{k_a-1}
 <\Phi(\mathcal C).
 \tag{ACR}\label{eq:exact-character-rank-drop}
\]
No SAP is needed for this counting lemma.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Within a fixed child sort the expanded binary
language adds only unary labels, so an old same-sort
atomic colour cannot split into two distinct
new pair types \emph{within that fixed child}.
The designated old colour $c_a$ is absent from
both children because its phase difference is one,
whereas points of a given child have equal phase.
Thus each child has at most $k_a-1$ distinct
nonidentity pair types. All inactive sorts are
unchanged. Each active parent contributes at most
$2\cdot3^{k_a-1}$ after splitting, strictly
less than its old contribution $3^{k_a}$,
since $k_a\ge1$. Summing gives the displayed
strict inequality. Both child sorts are nonempty
by the hypothesis; for the maximal exact character
splits below this also follows directly from
the phase-one involution on every active quotient
sort. No amalgamation argument enters this proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite AP-only exact character terminalisation]
\label{thm:ap-finite-character-elimination}
Starting from any homogeneous finite complete
named-sorted symmetric binary AP+semantic
homomorphic-CCO source in the class considered
above, successive \emph{maximal full exact}
character splits of
\cref{lem:ep-ap-TP,thm:ep-ap-marked-completion}
terminate after finitely many stages.
The resulting finite unary expansion remains
reasonable, reduct-surjective, precompact,
homogeneous, has AP/JEP and homomorphic
CCO, and on every terminal named sort
there are no distinct invariant equivalences
of \emph{finite relative index}.
Neither the initial nor the terminal age
is asserted to have SAP.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a current named sort has an invariant
equivalence interval of finite relative
index greater than one, choose a saturated
cover of finite index. The SAP-free orbital
parity theorem
\cref{lem:ep-ap-orbital-parity} provides a
reachable two-sheet even/odd layer on that
sort; its exact odd relation and the maximal
complement give a nontrivial character gate
by \cref{thm:foundational-maximal-parity-gate}.
Apply the real marked completion theorem
\cref{thm:ep-ap-marked-completion}; its
single-step age is again homogeneous,
reasonable, AP/JEP and homomorphic CCO,
and has finitely many named transitive
child sorts. Every active parent has a
nonidentity transported odd involution;
its represented relation is nonempty and
contains at least one actual old
same-sort diversity atom $c_a$. The character
takes that involution to one; hence its
old atom $c_a$ has phase difference one,
and both new children are nonempty.
Thus \cref{lem:exact-character-rank-drop}
strictly decreases the positive integer
$\Phi$ at every nontrivial step.

An infinite sequence is impossible.
If the terminal structure still had a
finite relative equivalence interval,
the same SAP-free orbital parity source
would permit another nontrivial split,
contradicting termination. A finite
composition of reasonable reduct-surjective
precompact unary expansions preserves
those three properties and the original
reduct. No use was made of SAP of an
intermediate quotient or original source.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[AP-only source for the exact-quotient core induction]
\label{cor:ep-ap-safe-splitting}
Every available finite-relative-index invariant
equivalence interval in a homogeneous symmetric
AP+homomorphic-CCO source admits a reachable
maximal exact character split whose actual
expanded age has AP/JEP and homomorphic CCO
with unchanged old reduct. Moreover finitely
many such steps eliminate \emph{all} finite
relative-index intervals on the resulting
named sorts, without introducing any SAP
hypothesis on the original or a quotient.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The parity theorem and maximal full exact
source gate supply a two-sheet layer and the
character with phase-one involution.
One-step marked AP/CCO completion is
\cref{lem:ep-ap-TP,thm:ep-ap-marked-completion}.
Finite termination, preservation of AP/CCO,
and absence of residual finite relative indices
are proved independently in Part~I by
\cref{thm:ap-finite-character-elimination}.
In particular this assertion does \emph{not}
rely on the later SAP-specific V27 terminal
Ramsey construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and retained source dependencies]
\label{rem:ep-ap-source-boundary}
The old parity and maximal gate, full exact
word 2-Helly/convolution, rigid groupoid,
rigid-product factorisation, affine coset
and relative-\(K\) closure-separation
results all occur earlier in Part~I.
The substantial new content here is the
SAP-free actual finite marked AP/CCO
completion, including real equality
contractions. Finite termination of the
\emph{entire} AP-only character tower is
proved IN THIS Part-I module by the
standalone potential-decrease lemma
\cref{lem:exact-character-rank-drop};
it does NOT invoke the later SAP-only V27
terminal Ramsey construction.
The underlying old parity, exact and
rigid-groupoid inputs remain substantial
source-relative premises, not human/Lean
certified. In particular the proof
does not infer SAP of a marked age
when the old age lacks it.
\end{remark}
